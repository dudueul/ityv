# Public access to electoral registers: United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Italy, Spain, Portugal (state as of November 2026)

Scope note: each country section below answers the same set of questions: legal basis; public inspection; copies (who, purpose limits, penalties); fields included or withheld; whether participation (who voted) is recorded and disclosed; opt-outs and protections; data-protection-authority (DPA) guidance and case law; changes from 2018 to 2026. Primary statute text was read directly where marked "(statute text read)". Items taken only from search-engine summaries or press reports are flagged. Anything dated before 2018 is marked as possibly outdated.

---

## United Kingdom (England and Wales rules; Scotland and Northern Ireland have parallel regulations not reviewed here)

### Takeaway
The UK keeps two versions of the register. The **full register** can be inspected by anyone, but only under supervision and without electronic copying. Bulk copies go only to listed users, each with a statutory purpose limit backed by criminal offences: elected representatives, parties, candidates, government bodies and credit reference agencies. The **open (edited) register** can be bought by anyone, including for marketing, unless the elector opts out. The **marked register** shows who voted and is supplied after elections to parties, candidates and others who can justify needing it. Wales abolished the open register for its local-government register from 1 October 2026. A UK Bill still before the House of Lords (November 2026) would make the open register opt-in.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis**
- The register is kept under the Representation of the People Act 1983 (RPA 1983), s.9. Each register must contain the names of registered persons, their qualifying addresses ("subject to any prescribed exceptions") and each person's **electoral number**. The parliamentary and local-government registers are combined, with marks for people registered under only one franchise (statute text read) — [RPA 1983 s.9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1983/2/section/9)
- Access, supply and sale are governed by the Representation of the People (England and Wales) Regulations 2001 (SI 2001/341), Parts 3, 6 and 8 (statute text read) — [SI 2001/341 reg 43](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/43)

**Public inspection**
- The full register is "published" by making a copy available **for inspection under supervision** at the Electoral Registration Officer's (ERO's) office and other places. Where inspection is on screen, the equipment must not let anyone (a) search it electronically by a person's name, or (b) copy or transmit any part of it — [reg 43(1), (1A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/43)
- At the British Library's copy of the full register, inspectors may not make copies or record particulars "otherwise than by means of hand-written notes" — [reg 97(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/97)
- Anyone who gets access to the full register by any route may use it only for the "permitted purpose" for which it was supplied or inspected — [reg 96](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/96)

**Copies of the full register (free supply, or sale, with purpose limits)**
- Free copies, in data form by default, go to MPs, Senedd members, councillors, mayors and London Assembly members for their area (reg 103), on written request. Supply can include the monthly update notices — [reg 102](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/102), [reg 103](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/103)
- Local constituency parties (one nominee per party per constituency) get copies but may use them only "for electoral purposes or the purposes of electoral registration" — [reg 105](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/105)
- Registered political parties (other than minor parties), recognised third parties and permitted participants receive the **whole** register. Parties may use it for electoral purposes and for checking donations under the Political Parties, Elections and Referendums Act 2000 (PPERA) — [reg 106](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/106)
- The full register is sold to government departments, the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA), the Environment Agency and national-security vetting bodies. Their use is limited to crime prevention and detection, statutory vetting and national-security vetting — [reg 113](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/113)
- **Credit reference agencies** can buy the full register. Use is limited to (a) vetting credit applications, (b) anti-money-laundering obligations, and (c) statistical credit-risk analysis that names no one — [reg 114](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/114)
- The British Library receives a free printed copy and a data copy. After 10 years it may disclose the register for "Article 84A GDPR purposes" (research, archiving, statistics) — [reg 97](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/97)
- **Penalties:** breaching the supply and use restrictions (regs 94–113) is a criminal offence. Supervisors and directors are also liable if they failed to take appropriate steps — [reg 115](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/115)
- ICO guidance: the full register "has restricted access and there is no right to opt out". The open register "is available for anybody to purchase" (search-engine summary) — [ICO, Electoral register (public guidance)](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/your-data-and-elections/electoral-register/)

**The open (edited) register and opt-out**
- When the full register is published, the ERO must also publish the edited register. It omits the name and address of any elector who has asked to be excluded, and omits all anonymous entries. "In other respects" it is identical to the full register, including any mark or date recorded against an elector — [reg 93(1)–(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/93)
- Reg 93 "does not apply to a register of local government electors for an area in Wales". This is the legislative form of the Welsh abolition — [reg 93(8)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/93)
- **Wales:** The Senedd approved the Representation of the People (Removal of the Edited Register) (Wales) Regulations 2026 on 10 March 2026. They take effect on 1 October 2026 and end the sale of Welsh voters' details to commercial organisations. Rationale: protecting young and vulnerable people from "harmful organisations", and unlocking automatic registration, which under the Elections and Elected Bodies (Wales) Act 2024 "cannot be rolled out until the open register is abolished" — [Welsh Government](https://www.gov.wales/node/75285)
- **UK Bill (pending):** The Representation of the People Bill "will end the presumption that electors will be added to the open register ... Instead, we will ask applicants whether they wish to opt in". The government says that with more automated registration "nobody's details will appear on the open register without their affirmative consent". It cites ICO guidance (policy paper updated 28 Oct 2026) — [GOV.UK policy summary: improving voter registration](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/representation-of-the-people-bill-policy-summaries/improving-voter-registration)
- Bill status (search-engine summary, not verified on the Parliament site): Commons third reading on 2 Sept 2026, Lords first reading on 3 Sept 2026 (HL Bill 47), Lords committee stage from about 2 Nov 2026. The opt-in change is reportedly clause 38. Under-16s will not appear on any publicly available version of the register and cannot opt in — [UK Parliament bill page](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4080); [GOV.UK votes at 16 summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/representation-of-the-people-bill-policy-summaries/votes-at-16)
- The Electoral Commission has long argued that the edited register should no longer be compiled or sold. A 2011 Commons committee said it could not justify selling electoral data to commercial organisations (secondary source, 2011–2014, possibly outdated) — [Stop Junk Mail summary](https://www.stopjunkmail.org.uk/news/2011/house-commons-committee-recommends-abolition-edited-register.html)

**Anonymous registration**
- Electors whose safety, or that of a household member, would be at risk can register anonymously. Anonymous entries are omitted from the edited register — [reg 93(2A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/93)
- At present anonymous registration lasts one year and must be renewed annually. The Bill would extend it to five years, aligned with the absent-vote cycle — [GOV.UK policy summary: anonymous electors](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/representation-of-the-people-bill-policy-summaries/anonymous-electors)

**Participation: the marked register**
- Anyone entitled to the full register under regs 100, 103, 105, 106, 108, 109 or 113 may request copies of the **marked register or lists** after an election. This covers parties, candidates and elected representatives, among others. The request must state the purposes and "why the supply or purchase of a copy of the full register or unmarked lists would not be sufficient". The ERO supplies a copy only if satisfied the requester "needs to see the marks" — [reg 117](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/117)
- Fee: £10, plus £1 per 1,000 entries in data form or £2 per 1,000 printed — [reg 120](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/120)
- **Any person** may ask to inspect the marked register under supervision, stating purposes and why the full register would not suffice. On-screen inspection must not allow name search or copying. Inspectors "may make hand written notes" of the marked register but may not otherwise copy — [reg 118](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/118)
- Permitted purposes for marked-register copies or inspection are "Article 84A GDPR purposes" (research, statistics, archiving) or "electoral purposes" — [reg 119(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/119)
- ICO: the marked register "enables identification of individuals who have voted in previous elections and referendums", but does not show how anyone voted. Parties must still give UK GDPR privacy information, must not share register data with other controllers, and should regularly request updated registers. The ICO notes this guidance is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — [ICO, Use of the electoral register](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-for-the-use-of-personal-data-in-political-campaigning-1/use-of-the-electoral-register/)

**Fields**
- Name, qualifying address and electoral number (a polling-district prefix plus a number). The register is marked where a person is registered for only one franchise. In Scotland and Wales, 16–17-year-olds registered only for local elections have the date they turn 18 shown, and Wales also marks qualifying foreign nationals. Date of birth and nationality are not otherwise listed fields — [RPA 1983 s.9(2)–(5A)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1983/2/section/9)

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- "UK GDPR"/"Article 84A GDPR purposes" wording now runs through Part 6 (regs 92, 97, 119), reflecting post-Brexit and Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amendments — [reg 92](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/92), [reg 119](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/341/regulation/119)
- Wales abolished its open register (1 Oct 2026). The UK Bill would make the open register opt-in and extend anonymous registration to 5 years (sources above).

### Inferences
- The UK model separates three access tiers. Tier 1 is supervised viewing by anyone, which makes the roll transparent for accuracy. Tier 2 is bulk data to political and state users, with statute-defined purposes and criminal offences. Tier 3 is a consent-based commercial version, which is now being removed or made opt-in. The ICO, the Electoral Commission and the Welsh Government all treat the commercial tier as the problem, not public inspection.
- The marked register is the closest European analogue to a "who voted" disclosure. Even there, supply is need-tested ("needs to see the marks"), and public inspection is limited to handwritten notes.

### Gaps
- Maximum penalty for a reg 115 offence: not verified.
- Exact text of Bill clause 38, and whether the Bill changes supply of the full or marked register: not found.
- Scotland and Northern Ireland regulations: not reviewed.
- Reg 108 (candidates) and reg 110 (sale of the edited register) were not read directly. The edited-register sale is confirmed only via ICO.

## Ireland

### Takeaway
Anyone may inspect the register for registration-related purposes, and copies can be bought. However, the Electoral Reform Act 2022 abolished the **edited register** (the marketing version) from October 2022. Any use of the register other than for "electoral or other statutory purposes" is an offence. Elected representatives and candidates get free copies. Marked copies of the register, showing who voted, have historically been open to inspection and sold to parties and academics (Electoral Act 1992 s.131); whether that regime changed after 2022 is unverified.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis**
- Part II of, and the Second Schedule to, the Electoral Act 1992, as amended (notably by the Electoral Reform Act 2022) — [Dept. of Housing / Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown CoCo leaflet, Oct 2023](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)
- ERA 2022 s.97 substitutes s.20(1) of the 1992 Act. Registration authorities must maintain the register and, "subject to section 15E(3)(b)", **make available for inspection the register** (statute text read) — [ERA 2022 s.97](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2022/act/30/section/97/revised/en/html)

**Public inspection**
- "Any person may inspect the register, for purposes related to the registration of electors, during office hours at the local authority offices" or at other places such as libraries or post offices. Anyone who forms an opinion on reasonable grounds that someone else's entry is inaccurate may file a "third party request" for amendment — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)
- The pending-elector list of 16–17-year-olds "is not part of the register, and it is not available for inspection" — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)

**Copies and purpose limits**
- "Copies of the register are provided free to elected public representatives and election candidates who may use it for electoral purposes." In addition, "a copy of the register, or extracts thereof, may be purchased from the registration authority" for a fee not exceeding reasonable cost — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)
- "The information contained in the register of electors can only be used for electoral or other statutory purposes, and any person who misuses, or causes or permits the misuse of the register, shall be guilty of an offence" — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)
- **Edited register abolished:** the Electoral Reform Act 2022 provides for abolition of the edited register, with provisions commenced from 13 October 2022 (S.I. No. 512 of 2022) (search-engine summary of gov.ie) — [gov.ie, Electoral Reform Act 2022](https://www.gov.ie/en/publication/72f88-electoral-reform-act-2022). ERA 2022 s.79 deletes the statutory definitions of "edited register" and "national edited register" from s.6 of the 1992 Act (search-engine summary) — [ERA 2022 s.79](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2022/act/30/section/79/revised/en/html)
- The pre-2022 edited register dated from Electoral Act amendments around 2001 that let voters opt out of lists sold to direct marketers (2002 press, possibly outdated) — [Irish Times, 15 Oct 2002](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/parties-buy-registers-showing-who-voted-1.1099451)

**Fields**
- "The electoral register includes the name, address, and voting entitlement of electors. It also notes where a person has a postal or special voting arrangement." The registration authority may require a person's Personal Public Service Number (PPSN), date of birth and Eircode for registration. These are collected for verification and are not listed as published fields — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)

**Anonymous electors**
- Since the 2022 Act, a person whose safety or a household member's safety would be at risk can register as an anonymous elector and vote by post. Supporting evidence can be a Domestic Violence Act 2018 order, a restraining or harassment order, or a declaration by a Garda superintendent or a medical practitioner. Their details "will not appear on the register that is available for public inspection" — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf)

**Participation: marked register**
- Electoral Act 1992 s.131 (as enacted): documents a returning officer sends to the Clerk of the Dáil, other than s.130 documents, "shall be open to public inspection", and the Clerk "shall supply copies of or extracts from the said documents to any person demanding the same" for a fee (statute text read, enacted version) — [Electoral Act 1992 s.131](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/1992/act/23/section/131)
- A 2014 parliamentary answer (search-engine summary) says the rules on public inspection and availability of marked copies of the register are in s.131 of the 1992 Act for Dáil elections, with a separate provision in the European Parliament Elections Act 1997, and that the marked copy is available "subject to conditions" — [Oireachtas PQ, 30 Apr 2014](https://www.oireachtas.ie/ga/debates/question/2014-04-30/509/)
- 2002 practice (possibly outdated): parties, academics and others bought more than 100 marked registers after the 2002 general election, at up to €6,000 for all 42 constituencies. The registers show non-voters, since voters' names are crossed out. Fianna Fáil used the 1997 marked register to map low-turnout areas. No equivalent provision existed for referendums — [Irish Times, 15 Oct 2002](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/parties-buy-registers-showing-who-voted-1.1099451)

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- Electoral Reform Act 2022: rolling register, PPSN/date of birth/Eircode verification, abolition of the edited register, anonymous electors, pre-registration of 16–17-year-olds, and annual reporting by registration authorities to An Coimisiún Toghcháin (Electoral Commission) — [2023 leaflet](https://www.dlrcoco.ie/sites/dlrcoco/files/2023-10/Voting%20-%20The%20Register%20of%20Electors%20-%20English.pdf); [gov.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/publication/72f88-electoral-reform-act-2022)

### Inferences
- Ireland moved away from the UK model in 2022. It kept public inspection and purchasable copies but removed the commercial opt-out version altogether, relying on a criminal ban on non-electoral, non-statutory use. Unlike Spain, it did not restrict who can obtain copies.
- If s.131 still applies as enacted, Ireland is among the most permissive of the six on participation data: anyone can buy marked copies.

### Gaps
- Current (post-2022) text of s.131 and of the "use of register" offence provision, including its exact section number and penalty: not verified. The Irish Statute Book and revised Acts pages returned 403/404 for some sections.
- Data Protection Commission (DPC) guidance specific to the register: not found.
- Whether the Electoral Commission publishes the register online: not found.

## France

### Takeaway
France is the most open of the six for the voter list. Under Code électoral art. **L37**, any elector, candidate, party or political group may consult **and obtain a copy** of the electoral list, if they undertake not to use it commercially. Commercial use is punished by a €15,000 fine. Participation is also open: under art. **L68**, the signed **listes d'émargement** (sign-in lists showing who voted) can be consulted by any elector for 10 days after the election. Electors cannot opt out of disclosure. The CNIL instead regulates downstream use through GDPR transparency, purpose, sorting and retention rules.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis and copies**
- Art. L37: any elector, candidate, party or political group "peut prendre communication et obtenir une copie de la liste électorale" ("may consult and obtain a copy of the electoral list"), "à condition de s'engager à ne pas en faire un usage commercial" ("on condition of undertaking not to use it commercially"). The commune may ask for details where there are serious grounds to fear commercial use (CNIL page dated 21 Oct 2025) — [CNIL, L'utilisation de listes d'électeurs pour la communication politique](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)
- Penalty: the Code électoral "punit l'usage commercial d'une liste électorale ou d'une liste électorale consulaire d'une amende de 15 000 €" ("punishes commercial use of an electoral list or a consular electoral list with a €15,000 fine"). CNIL cites art. L.113-2 — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)
- L37 was introduced by loi n° 2016-1048 of 1 August 2016 ("Pochon-Warsmann"), replacing former L28. The former R16 banned only "purely commercial" use; the current text bans any commercial use (2017 law-firm note; ministerial answers 2013–2019, possibly outdated in detail) — [Seban Avocats 2017](https://www.seban-associes.avocat.fr/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Listes_electorales_une_utilisation_et_reutilisation_sous_controle.pdf)
- The CADA (Commission d'accès aux documents administratifs) has treated some mixed associative or institutional uses as commercial (search-engine summary) — [CADA avis 20190154](https://www.data.gouv.fr/explore/cada/20190154)
- Access follows the CRPA (code des relations entre le public et l'administration): free on-site consultation, free email of electronic files, or a copy at no more than reproduction cost. The CADA handles disputes, except over consular lists. The Conseil d'État held that access is **not limited to election periods** (CE, 9 Nov 2022, n° 449863, as cited by CNIL) — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)

**Fields**
- Electoral lists contain name, usual name, first names, **date and place of birth**, registration address, **polling-station number** and a **sequence number** on the sign-in list. Consular lists may also include email addresses — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)

**Participation: listes d'émargement**
- Sign-in lists contain the same data, except emails, "plus the voter's signature, which shows whether they voted" — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)
- L68: "les listes d'émargement déposées à la préfecture ou à la sous-préfecture sont communiquées à tout électeur requérant pendant un délai de dix jours à compter de l'élection" ("the sign-in lists filed at the prefecture or sub-prefecture are communicated to any requesting elector for ten days from the election"). They are also available between the two rounds — [Assemblée nationale written question QE 853 (17th legislature)](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE853.pdf); [CNIL, contacting abstentionists between rounds (11 Jun 2024)](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/elections/sadresser-aux-abstentionnistes-entre-les-deux-tours-est-ce-possible)
- After the litigation period, sign-in lists are no longer communicable and become public archives, closed for 50 years to protect privacy (local-government guide) — [Maison des Communes de la Vendée](https://www.maisondescommunes85.fr/node/6421)
- In 2020 (COVID), an ordinance shortened the first-round consultation period to five days, and the Conseil d'État upheld it (CE, 23 Oct 2020, n° 440827). This was a temporary derogation — [Conseil d'État 440827](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/fr/arianeweb/CE/decision/2020-10-23/440827)
- CNIL describes contacting abstentionists between rounds as an accepted "republican practice", subject to GDPR information duties. Senders must give, by the first message at the latest, their identity, the source (the electoral list), the legal basis, the retention period and the right to object and complain — [CNIL (11 Jun 2024)](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/elections/sadresser-aux-abstentionnistes-entre-les-deux-tours-est-ce-possible)

**Opt-outs and protections**
- "Voters cannot block their information from being given to list requesters." Recipients must offer "un moyen simple de s'opposer au traitement" ("a simple way to object to the processing") and honour objections "facilement et à tout moment" ("easily and at any time") — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)
- CNIL limits on use:
  - Sorting by age or address is allowed, for example for door-to-door canvassing.
  - Sorting by name origin is prohibited because of discrimination risk.
  - Sorting by place of birth is considered unjustified.
  - Data should be deleted after the vote, including by contractors.
  - Canvassers should see only the data of the people they contact.
  - Under EU Regulation 2024/900 on political advertising, data not collected directly from individuals cannot be used for political targeting or ad delivery.
  - Communes should systematically inform requesters of their data-protection obligations.
  - [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- From 1 Jan 2019 the **Répertoire électoral unique (REU)**, held by INSEE, centralises voter data. "Ce registre ne peut pas être utilisé pour d'autres finalités" ("this register may not be used for other purposes"). Since that date nobody may be on both a commune list and a consular list — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)
- CNIL guidance updated 21 Oct 2025 to integrate Regulation (EU) 2024/900 — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)

### Inferences
- France pairs very open access with use-side controls: no opt-out at source, but a commercial-use ban, a CNIL purpose and minimisation doctrine, deletion after the vote, and EU targeting limits. Its participation transparency (L68) is time-boxed (10 days) and tied to contesting the election, after which the lists are sealed for 50 years.
- The French list includes date and place of birth, so a full copy carries more re-identification risk than UK or Irish copies.

### Gaps
- Légifrance article pages could not be fetched (404/blocked). The texts of L37 and L68 are quoted via CNIL and an Assemblée nationale written question, not Légifrance directly.
- Whether L68 lets the requesting elector photocopy or photograph sign-in lists, rather than only consult them: not confirmed by the CNIL pages read.
- Whether complementary lists for EU citizens show nationality: not found.

## Italy

### Takeaway
Italian electoral lists are public in principle. Revision acts are "sempre ostensibili a chiunque" ("always open to anyone") and every citizen may view the lists during publication periods. However, **copies** may be issued only for listed purposes: electoral law, study and research, socio-assistance, or a collective or diffuse interest (DPR 223/1967 art. 51). Parties and candidates may use the municipal lists for propaganda without consent (Garante 2014 and 2019). The **section lists used at polling stations**, which record who voted, are excluded: they may not be used for propaganda and are checkable only to verify the regularity of the vote. No individual opt-out was found.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis and inspection**
- DPR 20 March 1967 n. 223 (testo unico on the active electorate and electoral lists): art. 51(1) says the revision acts "sono sempre ostensibili a chiunque". Art. 18(2) says "Ogni cittadino ha diritto di prenderne visione" ("every citizen has the right to view them") during publication. Art. 30(4) says corrected lists stay deposited at the municipal secretariat on 21–30 June and 21–31 December, open to every citizen — [DPR 223/1967 text, Edizioni Europee](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/16/zn39_01_014.html)

**Copies and purpose limits**
- Art. 51 (the copy paragraph, as substituted by art. 177 d.lgs. 196/2003 from 1 Jan 2004): "le liste elettorali possono essere rilasciate in copia per finalità di applicazione della disciplina in materia di elettorato attivo e passivo, di studio, di ricerca statistica, scientifica o storica, o carattere socio-assistenziale o per il perseguimento di un interesse collettivo o diffuso" ("electoral lists may be issued in copy for the purposes of applying the rules on the right to vote and stand, of study, of statistical, scientific or historical research, of a socio-assistance nature, or for pursuing a collective or diffuse interest") — [Regione FVG opinion, 26 Jan 2018](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/30-01-2018-richiesta-rilascio-copia-liste-elettorali/30/01/2018); [DPR text](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/16/zn39_01_014.html)
  - Numbering conflict: one consolidated text numbers this as comma 4, while other sources call it comma 5 — [Edizioni Europee](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/16/zn39_01_014.html) vs [FVG opinion](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/30-01-2018-richiesta-rilascio-copia-liste-elettorali/30/01/2018)
- **Revival dispute:** art. 177 of d.lgs. 196/2003 was itself repealed by art. 27 of d.lgs. 101/2018, the GDPR adaptation decree — [DPR text notes](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/16/zn39_01_014.html). This raised the question of whether the pre-2004 text ("copy to anyone") revived. A 2018 commentary called it an interpretive "rebus". A Ministry of the Interior opinion (search-engine summary) holds that the restricted text "continua ad operare nel testo vigente" ("continues to operate in the text in force") and that the old text does not revive — [segretaricomunalivighenzi.it 2018 items](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/?p=36273)
  - Caveat: which of the two search hits carries the Ministry opinion was not individually verified.
- The requester must specify the concrete use; repeating the statutory formula is not enough (TAR Cagliari, sez. II, 17 Feb 2011, n. 148). A request for "promotional material" is unclear, but if it is electoral propaganda there is no ground to refuse — [FVG opinion 2018](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/30-01-2018-richiesta-rilascio-copia-liste-elettorali/30/01/2018)
- Ministry of the Interior opinion n. 21945 of 16 July 2026, on a minority councillor's request for lists by section with addresses: access to the general and section lists "sembrerebbe consentito" ("would appear to be allowed") under art. 51, and the decision belongs to the municipality, not the Prefettura. It refers to a Garante opinion of 8 May 2026 calling for an "equilibrato bilanciamento" ("balanced weighing") between the councillor's mandate and third parties' privacy. It also cites Consiglio di Stato 2189/2023, which suggests masking names where possible, and TAR Lazio 49/2023, which holds councillors personally responsible for unlawful dissemination — [segretaricomunalivighenzi.it, 30 Sept / 5 Oct 2026](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/?p=71869)

**Fields**
- Art. 5: lists are compiled alphabetically in duplicate and show surname and name, place and date of birth, number/part/series of the birth record, and **abitazione** (address). The former entries for **titolo di studio** (education) and **professione** (occupation) were repealed by the 2003 privacy code — [DPR text](https://www.edizionieuropee.it/law/html/16/zn39_01_014.html)

**Participation: section lists**
- Copies of section lists "già utilizzate" ("already used") at polling stations, which carry the annotation of who voted, do not seem to fall under art. 51 (Ministry opinion 2026). A Garante opinion of 4 April 2001 says such lists are checkable "al solo fine del controllo sulla regolarità delle operazioni elettorali" ("solely to check the regularity of the electoral operations") — [segretaricomunalivighenzi.it 2026](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/?p=71869)
- The Garante excludes "liste elettorali di sezione già utilizzate nei seggi" ("section lists already used at polling stations") from propaganda use — [Garante communiqué 19 Apr 2019](https://garanteprivacy.it/garante/doc.jsp?ID=9105208); these lists record non-voters and serve only to check the operations (art. 62 DPR 570/1960) — [FVG opinion 2018 summarising Garante 6 Mar 2014](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/30-01-2018-richiesta-rilascio-copia-liste-elettorali/30/01/2018)

**DPA guidance on political communication**
- Garante provvedimento of 18 April 2019 (European elections): parties, movements, committees and candidates may use, without consent, data in the "liste elettorali detenute dai Comuni" ("electoral lists held by municipalities") and other public electorate registers, such as the list of voters resident abroad.
  - Not usable: registry, civil-status and professional-register data, the national digital-domicile index (INI-PEC), transparency publications, and data collected by office-holders in their mandate.
  - Consent needed: phone directories, SMS and email, social networks, scraped data, and petition signers.
  - Recipients must be informed, within one month where data was not collected directly.
  - The European Authority for Political Parties can fine up to 5% of a party's annual budget.
  - [Garante communiqué 19 Apr 2019](https://garanteprivacy.it/garante/doc.jsp?ID=9105208)
- Garante provvedimento 6 March 2014: parties may omit the information notice from the 60th day before to the 60th day after the vote, if materials give a contact point for exercising rights — [Garante 2014 (PDF via Consiglio regionale Piemonte)](https://www.cr.piemonte.it/dwd/web/media/files/Garante_privacy_107_2014.pdf); [FVG opinion 2018](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/30-01-2018-richiesta-rilascio-copia-liste-elettorali/30/01/2018)
- Older Garante positions (pre-2003, possibly outdated) described "piena conoscibilità e pubblicità" ("full knowability and publicity") of the lists regardless of the requester's purpose (search-engine summary) — [Garante doc 40493](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/garante/doc.jsp?ID=40493)

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- d.lgs. 101/2018 repealed art. 177 Codice privacy, prompting the revival dispute (above).
- 2024: Ministry of the Interior (DAIT, Department for Internal and Territorial Affairs) circular on integrating electoral lists and section-list registration data into ANPR (Anagrafe Nazionale della Popolazione Residente, the national resident register) and issuing electoral certificates via ANPR (title only; contents not read) — [segretaricomunalivighenzi.it, 1 Mar 2024](https://www.segretaricomunalivighenzi.it/modalita-di-integrazione-nellanpr-delle-liste-elettorali-e-dei-dati-relativi-alliscrizione-nelle-liste-di-sezione-attivazione-servizio-di-rilascio-dei-certificati-elettorali-tramite-anpr-circola/01/03/2024)
- 2026: Ministry opinion n. 21945 and Garante opinion of 8 May 2026 on councillors' access (above).

### Inferences
- Italy is in between France and Spain. Viewing is open to anyone and copying is purpose-limited, but the purposes are broad: "interesse collettivo o diffuso" and electoral propaganda are accepted. Commercial marketing is generally treated as outside them (search-engine summary of a Garante opinion; not verified).
- Italy draws a clear line between the roll, which is public, and the participation record (marked section lists), which is control-only. This is the opposite of France's L68.

### Gaps
- Normattiva consolidated text could not be extracted (JavaScript page). The art. 51 paragraph numbering and current text should be confirmed there.
- The Garante document excluding marketing use of lists was not identified individually.
- The Garante opinion of 8 May 2026: primary text not read.
- Any 2022–2026 Garante election provvedimento (e.g. 2022 political or 2024 European elections): not found.

## Spain

### Takeaway
Spain is the most restrictive of the six. LOREG art. 41.2 **prohibits "any particularised information"** from the electoral census, except by court order. Public "consultation" is a time-limited (8-day) service for electors to check their own entries after an election is called. Full copies (name, address, date of birth, polling table) go only to the representatives of proclaimed candidacies, for their district, exclusively for LOREG purposes, now downloaded encrypted from the national statistics institute (INE). Electors may oppose inclusion of their **address** in those copies, and threatened persons can be excluded. The Constitutional Court (STC 76/2019) struck down the 2018 provision letting parties collect political-opinion data.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis**
- Ley Orgánica 5/1985 del Régimen Electoral General (LOREG), arts. 39–41 (consolidated text updated 2 July 2026; statute text read) — [BOE LOREG](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Real Decreto 1799/2003 on the content of electoral lists and census copies (last amended by RD 189/2023; statute text read) — [BOE RD 1799/2003](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)

**Public inspection (consultation)**
- Art. 39.2: town halls and consulates must keep a consultation service for the electoral lists for **8 days from the 6th day after the election is called**. Consultation is by computer, "previa identificación del interesado" ("after identifying the person concerned"), or by public display if computer means are insufficient — [LOREG art. 39](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Art. 39.3: "cualquier persona" ("any person") may file a claim "sobre sus datos censales" ("about their own census data"): errors, address changes, or omission — [LOREG art. 39](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- At polling places, the only list publicly displayed shows the street index of the section and the alphabetical range of surname initials. The full voting list is "para uso de la mesa" ("for the polling table's use") — [RD 1799/2003 art. 3](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)
- The municipal consultation file contains surname and name, date of birth, sex, DNI/passport/residence-card number, education level, address, place of birth, district/section/table, polling place, country of residence (for Spaniards abroad) and country of nationality (for foreign electors) — [RD 1799/2003 art. 2](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)

**Prohibition and copies**
- Art. 41.2: "Queda prohibida cualquier información particularizada sobre los datos personales contenidos en el censo electoral, a excepción de los que se soliciten por conducto judicial" ("any particularised information on personal data in the electoral census is prohibited, except where requested through the courts"). Art. 41.3: the Oficina del Censo Electoral (OCE, Electoral Census Office) may provide non-identifying statistics — [LOREG art. 41](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Art. 41.4: Autonomous Communities may obtain an electronic copy after each election call — [LOREG art. 41](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Art. 41.5: "Los representantes de cada candidatura podrán obtener dentro de los dos días siguientes a la proclamación de su candidatura una copia del censo del distrito correspondiente, ordenado por mesas, en soporte apto para su tratamiento informático, que podrá ser utilizado **exclusivamente** para los fines previstos en la presente Ley" ("representatives of each candidacy may obtain, within two days of its proclamation, a copy of the census for their district, ordered by polling table, in machine-readable form, to be used **exclusively** for the purposes of this Law").
  - General representatives may instead get the census of all districts where their party runs.
  - Zone electoral boards also hold a copy.
  - Boards may suspend delivery where a candidacy's proclamation is under appeal or the candidacy may fall under art. 44.4 (successor lists of banned parties).
  - [LOREG art. 41](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Junta Electoral Central (JEC, Central Electoral Board) Instrucción 1/2026 of 26 March 2026 (BOE 31 Mar 2026; replaces Instrucción 3/2023): representatives apply via the INE e-office, authenticated with the Cl@ve national e-ID login system, until proclamation day. Files are sent **encrypted** to one email address, with decryption keys sent to a second address, and are delivered within 2 days of proclamation (statute text read) — [BOE-A-2026-7297](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-7297)
- JEC Instrucción 4/2009: representatives no longer have to return copies but must undertake not to keep the information and to **delete it at the end of the process** (search-engine summary) — [BOE Instrucción 4/2009](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/ins/2009/12/17/4)

**Fields in candidates' copies**
- For residents in Spain: order number, surnames and name, province and municipality, district/section/table, **address**, **date of birth**, and country of nationality for non-Spanish EU or other electors. For Spaniards abroad: order number, name, province/municipality of registration, address, country of residence and date of birth — [RD 1799/2003 art. 5.3](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)
- DNI number, sex, education level and place of birth are **not** in candidates' copies. They appear only in the municipal consultation file and, for DNI, in the polling-table voting lists (RD arts. 2 and 4) — [RD 1799/2003](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)

**Opt-outs and protections**
- Art. 39.3 (as amended by LO 3/2018): requests from electors "que se opongan a su inclusión en las copias del censo electoral que se faciliten a los representantes de las candidaturas para realizar envíos postales de propaganda electoral" ("who object to being included in the census copies given to candidacy representatives for postal campaign mailings") will be granted — [LOREG art. 39](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Effect: the **address field is left blank** with an express note citing art. 39.3. The objection is permanent until the elector revokes it — [RD 1799/2003 art. 5.3](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)
- Art. 41.6 and RD art. 6: persons who could face threats or coercion endangering life, physical integrity or liberty may be excluded from the copies, on a decision by the Secretaría de Estado de Seguridad (Order INT/646/2003) — [LOREG art. 41](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672); [RD 1799/2003 art. 6](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/12/26/1799/con)

**Participation**
- Art. 86.4: polling-table members and, if they wish, party **interventores** (party scrutineers) each note in a numbered list the name and surnames of voters in the order they vote, with their census number. "Todo elector tiene derecho a examinar si ha sido bien anotado su nombre" ("every elector has the right to check that their name was correctly recorded") — [LOREG art. 86](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- Art. 100.2: the numbered list of voters and the census list used go into the sealed first envelope of the electoral record, sent to the electoral board — [LOREG art. 100](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)

**Case law and DPA**
- **STC 76/2019 (22 May 2019)**, on an action by the Defensor del Pueblo (Ombudsman), declared **art. 58 bis.1 LOREG** unconstitutional and void. That provision, added by LO 3/2018, let parties collect personal data revealing **political opinions** "en el marco de sus actividades electorales" ("in the framework of their electoral activities"), justified by public interest "únicamente cuando se ofrezcan garantías adecuadas" ("only when adequate guarantees are offered").
  - The Court found three violations of art. 18.4 read with art. 53.1 of the Constitution: no identified legitimate purpose, no defined scope or conditions, and no guarantees.
  - "Las garantías adecuadas deben estar incorporadas a la propia regulación legal del tratamiento" ("the adequate guarantees must be built into the statutory regulation of the processing itself").
  - Neither GDPR art. 9.2(g), the data-protection act (LOPDGDD) nor the data-protection authority's (AEPD's) Circular 1/2019 could cure the gap.
  - [BOE-A-2019-9548 (STC 76/2019)](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2019-9548)
- Art. 58 bis.2–5 survive. Parties may use personal data "obtenidos en páginas web y otras fuentes de acceso público" ("obtained from websites and other publicly accessible sources") for political activities during the electoral period. Electronic campaign messages are not commercial communications. Recipients must be given "un modo sencillo y gratuito de ejercicio del derecho de oposición" ("a simple and free way to exercise the right to object") — [LOREG art. 58 bis](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- LO 3/2018: art. 39.3 opt-out from candidates' copies, and art. 58 bis (para. 1 annulled in 2019) — [LOREG](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1985-11672)
- RD 189/2023 amended RD 1799/2003 (telematic consultation by INE). JEC Instrucción 3/2023, replaced by 1/2026, moved copies to encrypted telematic download — [BOE-A-2026-7297](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-7297)

### Inferences
- Spain treats the census as confidential personal data with narrow, statute-listed exceptions: candidates, regions, electoral boards and the courts. Public scrutiny happens through self-checking and claims, not third-party browsing. The constitutional reasoning in STC 76/2019, that safeguards must be in the statute itself, is a strong precedent for any Korean law expanding access: purposes, recipients, fields and safeguards would have to be written into the law, not left to decrees or DPA guidance.
- The "who voted" record exists, and party scrutineers can compile their own, but no route for public disclosure was found.

### Gaps
- Whether the sealed numbered voter lists (art. 100) can later be inspected by parties or the public in electoral litigation: not found.
- AEPD guidance on use of census copies after 2019: not reviewed.
- Specific penalty for misuse of census copies, whether a LOREG electoral offence or a GDPR fine: not verified.

## Portugal

### Takeaway
Portugal's registration law (Lei 13/99, republished by Lei 47/2008) provides public **exposure** of the rolls for consultation and claims: an annual period in March, and a pre-election period from the 39th to the 34th day before the vote. Holders can also check their own entries online. Political parties and citizen groups have a statutory right to obtain a **computerised copy or photocopy** of the rolls at their own cost. For overseas voters, parties may get digital copies from the interior ministry (MAI) for postal campaigning, used only for that purpose and **destroyed after the campaign**. Other disclosures of the central database (BDRE) require a legal basis or authorisation from the data-protection authority (CNPD). No public disclosure of who voted was found.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis**
- Lei n.º 13/99 of 22 March (Regime jurídico do recenseamento eleitoral), as amended and republished by Lei n.º 47/2008 of 27 August (statute text read; later amendments not checked) — [Lei 47/2008 (Madeira gov PDF)](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf); [Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/47-2008-453269)

**Public inspection**
- Art. 56: each February the system generates the rolls ("cadernos") for printing by registration commissions, "para efeitos de consulta e reclamação dos interessados durante o mês de Março" ("for consultation and claims by those concerned during March") — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- Art. 57: between the 39th and 34th days before an election, **lists of changes** to the rolls are displayed at registration commissions for consultation and claims. Art. 57.5: the electoral administration may allow the holder to consult their own data securely online — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- Art. 60: during exposure periods, "qualquer eleitor ou partido político" ("any elector or political party") may file a written claim about omissions or improper entries — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- Art. 59: rolls cannot be changed in the 15 days before an election — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)

**Copies**
- Art. 29.1(c): political parties and citizen voter groups have the "Direito de obter cópia informatizada ou fotocópia dos cadernos de recenseamento, desde que ponham à disposição os meios humanos e técnicos adequados e suportem os respectivos encargos" ("right to obtain a computerised copy or photocopy of the rolls, provided they supply adequate staff and technical means and bear the costs") — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- Overseas voters: under the parliamentary election law (Lei 14/79, LEAR), "os partidos políticos e coligações podem obter, junto do Ministério da Administração Interna, cópia dos cadernos eleitorais dos eleitores residentes no estrangeiro em suporte digital" ("parties and coalitions may obtain from the interior ministry a digital copy of the rolls of voters resident abroad") for postal campaigning. The copies "apenas podem ser utilizadas para a finalidade aí prevista e devem ser destruídas após o termo da campanha eleitoral" ("may be used only for that purpose and must be destroyed after the campaign"). Reportedly this is art. 54.º LEAR; exact text not verified — [Renascença, 23 Feb 2024](https://rr.pt/noticia/politica/2024/02/23/envio-de-cartas-com-propaganda-politica-a-emigrantes-esta-previsto-na-lei/368082/)
- In 2024 the PS mailed emigrants using these copies, and some recipients objected. The CNPD said the procedure is lawful, that the law "contém uma norma de salvaguarda" ("contains a safeguard clause"), and that it had never received a complaint about misuse of overseas voters' addresses — [Notícias ao Minuto/Lusa, 24 Feb 2024](https://www.noticiasaominuto.com/pais/2508617/lei-que-permite-propaganda-a-emigrantes-salvaguarda-protecao-de-dados)
- A 2016 CNPD opinion (press report) held that only direct family members and political parties may access emigrants' personal data from the register (possibly outdated) — [Observador, 9 Oct 2016](https://observador.pt/2016/10/09/parecer-veta-acesso-a-dados-de-emigrantes/)

**Database fields and data protection**
- The central database (BDRE) holds: registration number; registration commission; full name; filiation (parents' names); date of birth; place of birth; sex; parish and municipality or country of residence; address; consular district; ID and residence-document numbers and dates; nationality; date, origin and type of communication; and phone or email only with the holder's consent — [Lei 47/2008, art. on BDRE content](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- The rolls (cadernos) are organised by registration number, with about 1,000 electors each (arts. 52–53). The exact fields printed on a caderno were not found.
- Art. 14: anyone identified may know and correct their own record. Art. 15.3: access conditions are set "mediante prévio parecer vinculativo da CNPD" ("subject to a prior binding opinion of the CNPD"). Art. 16: BDRE data may be communicated to security forces or public bodies only where there is a legal obligation or authorisation, or CNPD authorisation, and the data is indispensable. Art. 17: statistical and research disclosure only if persons are not identifiable — [Lei 47/2008](https://www.madeira.gov.pt//Portals/25/Documentos/Legislacao/Lei_47_2008.pdf)
- CNPD's general stance on addresses (2021, candidate lists rather than voter rolls): publishing candidates' full addresses breaches the GDPR, and the postal code alone would suffice. This put the CNPD at odds with the national election commission (CNE) — [ECO, 1 Aug 2021](https://eco.sapo.pt/2021/08/01/cne-e-protecao-de-dados-colidem-nas-moradas-dos-candidatos-as-autarquicas/)

**Participation**
- Under Lei 80/2023 on dematerialised (electronic) rolls (search-engine summary of the Diário da República page): a polling official records each voter in the digital roll, the data is sent to the Imprensa Nacional-Casa da Moeda (INCM, the state printing and mint office) over secure lines, and it is **deleted after official publication of the results** — [Lei 80/2023, Diário da República](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/80-2023-835809047)
- No provision for public or party consultation of marked rolls after an election was found.

**Opt-outs**
- No opt-out from parties' copies was found. Phone and email are stored only with consent (BDRE fields above).

**Recent changes 2018–2026**
- Lei 47/2018 amended the registration regime (title only; content not verified) — [DR Lei 47/2018](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/lei/47-2018-116029385). Lei 80/2023 introduced electronic rolls (above). The 2024 emigrant-mailing controversy was described above.

### Inferences
- Portugal's model resembles Spain's in purpose (exposure for accuracy, plus copies to parties) but is less restrictive: parties have a standing right to copies, not only at proclamation. As in Spain, a data-protection authority (the CNPD) is built into the access rules (binding opinion on access conditions).
- The destroy-after-campaign rule for overseas copies matches Spain's JEC delete-after-process rule and CNIL's delete-after-vote doctrine. This is a common European safeguard pattern.

### Gaps
- Later amendments to Lei 13/99 after 2008 (e.g. 2018, 2020) were not checked. Art. 29.1(c) is quoted from the 2008 republication and may have been amended.
- Exact fields on printed rolls and on party copies, in particular whether address and date of birth are included: not found.
- Full text of LEAR art. 54 and of Lei 80/2023: not read directly.
- Any CNE or CNPD deliberation on domestic party copies: not found.

## Cross-cutting European standards and case law

### Takeaway
European standards require electoral registers to be **permanent, published and correctable**. No ECtHR or CJEU judgment directly on public release of voter rolls was found. Article 8 case law on publishing personal data (L.B. v. Hungary, 2023) and EU political-advertising rules (Regulation 2024/900) push towards purpose-limited, minimised access rather than open bulk data.

### Cited Findings
- Venice Commission Code of Good Practice in Electoral Matters (2002): among the essential criteria for reliable registers are permanence, publication and regular (at least yearly) updating, with appeal rights for omitted voters (search-engine summary; exact wording of paragraph I.1.2 not verified) — [ACE Project copy of the Code](https://aceproject.org/ero-en/misc/code-of-good-practice-in-electoral-matters)
- ECtHR, closest cases (search-engine summaries, not read in full):
  - Tasev v. North Macedonia (no. 9825/13, 16 May 2019): Article 8 violation where a judge-candidate could not correct his personal data in the electoral roll.
  - L.B. v. Hungary (Grand Chamber, 2023): online publication of a tax debtor's name and home address; the leading Article 8 case on state publication of personal data.
  - [Council of Europe data-protection case-law compilation](https://rm.coe.int/case-law-on-data-protection/1680766992); [ROL Platform on Tasev](https://www.rolplatform.org/refusal-to-make-the-change-of-nationality-in-the-electoral-roll-for-judges-has-led-to-violations-of-the-right-to-privacy/)
- EU Regulation 2024/900 on political advertising: according to CNIL, data not collected directly from the individual cannot be used for political targeting or ad delivery. This limits how register-derived data can be used in online campaigning across the EU — [CNIL](https://cnil.fr/fr/la-liste-electorale)

### Inferences
- The European consensus is: (1) publish for verification; (2) supply bulk data only to electoral actors, under statutory purpose limits, deletion duties and penalties; (3) treat participation records as more sensitive than the roll itself. On (3), France and possibly Ireland are the outliers in disclosing who voted. Commercial access is disappearing: Ireland (2022), Wales (2026), and UK opt-in pending.

### Gaps
- A CJEU judgment on electoral rolls: none found.
- Venice Commission or Council of Europe material specifically on voter-roll privacy versus publication (e.g. guidelines on processing personal data in electoral processes): titles surfaced but were not read — [CoE T-PD compilation of comments, 2023](https://rm.coe.int/compilation-of-comments-tpd-2023-2rev3-processing-pd-in-vote-and-elect/1680ae9ec7)

## Comparison table (one row per country)

### Takeaway
Of the six countries, only France lets any elector take a full copy of the voter list. France and (historically) Ireland disclose participation broadly. The UK supplies marked registers to political users on a need test. Italy, Spain and Portugal keep participation records internal. All six allow public inspection for accuracy, and all impose purpose limits on bulk copies.

### Inferences
- Openness of the roll itself, from most to least open: France (any elector may copy), then Ireland (anyone may buy, use-restricted), then Italy (purpose-restricted copies, broad purposes), then the UK (supervised viewing; bulk only to listed users, apart from the open-register opt-out tier), then Portugal (exposure plus party copies), then Spain (ban on particularised information; candidates only).
- Disclosure of who voted, from most to least: France (any elector, 10 days), Ireland (historically any purchaser), the UK (need-tested supply to political users and supervised inspection), then Italy, Spain and Portugal (internal or control-only).
- Common safeguards worth borrowing for Korea:
  - statutory purpose limits with criminal penalties (UK, Ireland, France, Spain);
  - deletion after the campaign (Spain, Portugal, CNIL);
  - an address opt-out for campaign mailings (Spain);
  - anonymous registration for at-risk electors (UK, Ireland, Spain art. 41.6);
  - no electronic searchability or copying of inspection copies (UK);
  - withholding national-ID numbers from copies (Spain; UK and Ireland do not publish them);
  - building safeguards into the statute itself (STC 76/2019).
- The trend from 2018 to 2026 is to **narrow commercial access** (Ireland 2022, Wales 2026, UK opt-in pending) while keeping political and transparency access. No country moved toward full open-data publication of the roll.
