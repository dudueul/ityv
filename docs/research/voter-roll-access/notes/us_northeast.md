# Public access to statewide voter lists: US Northeast and Mid-Atlantic (CT, DE, ME, MD, MA, NH, NJ, NY, PA, RI, VT, DC)

Research date: 2026-11-12. Items dated before 2024 are marked **[pre-2024, possibly outdated]**.
Method note: NCSL's "Access to and Use of Voter Registration Lists" page, the New York State Board of Elections site (elections.ny.gov) and the Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth site returned HTTP 403 or bot challenges. Facts from those sources come from search-result snippets, and they are marked that way. Official PDFs (DE, VT, DC, RI-Providence) were downloaded and read in full with pdftotext.

## Q1. Who may obtain the statewide list, and how (free download, sale, or on request)?

### Takeaway
None of the 12 jurisdictions offers a free, open online bulk download like North Carolina's. DC comes closest: the full list is free on request via a secure link, with no eligibility test on the form. Six give it to any requester for a fee, usually with a sworn non-commercial or election-use affirmation: CT, DE, PA, RI, VT and, since 2025, ME (for election-law compliance purposes only). The rest restrict it:
- NY: any requester, but for "election purposes" only.
- MD: Maryland registered voters only.
- NH: parties, political committees and candidates only.
- MA: state parties and statewide campaigns only.
- NJ: no statewide state-level channel was confirmed. County-level lists go to "any voter."

### Cited Findings
**Connecticut**
- The statewide voter registry file is bought by a written request (mail or email) to the Secretary of the State's LEAD unit. The fee is $300, payable before release. The page sets no eligibility limit. — [CT SOTS, Centralized Voter Registration Information – Voter Privacy Program](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/election-services/election-services/centralized-voter-registration-information---voter-privacy-program)
- Political parties, candidates, state agencies and county or local governments (for government business) may get lists free. Others pay a fee. — [CT SOTS page (search snippet)](https://portal.ct.gov/SOTS/Election-Services/Election-Services/Centralized-Voter-Registration-Information---Voter-Privacy-Program)
- CGS § 9-50a requires registrars to make the active and inactive registry lists available to the public under the FOIA provision, § 1-210. — [CGS 9-50a (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ct/title-9-elections/ct-gen-st-sect-9-50a/)

**Delaware**
- Requester types on the official form: "Major/Minor Political Party", "Candidate", "Public", and "General Assembly; State Agencies; County or Local Government". Scope can be statewide or other. Formats are TXT, CSV or XLSX, delivered by secure email. The fee is $25 per request; candidates and others exempt under 15 Del. C. § 304(e) pay nothing. Allow up to 10 business days, with one active request at a time. — [DE Voter Registration File Request form VRFM006 v7.0, 6/1/2022](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf) [pre-2024 form, but the fee is confirmed in 2025 below]
- In July 2025 Delaware told DOJ that 15 Del. C. § 304(g) sets the fee (currently $25) and § 304(h) defines the list contents. — [DE DOE response letter to US DOJ, 2025-07-25](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/DE_DOE-US_DOJ_Response_Letter_20250725.pdf) (via search snippet)
- Candidates who have filed or been nominated pay nothing. — [DE DOE, Voter Registration Information Requests](https://elections.delaware.gov/candidates/purchasereports.shtml)

**Maine**
- Central voter registration (CVR) data is **confidential by default** (21-A M.R.S. § 196-A). It is released only under enumerated exceptions:
  - (B) political parties, campaign-linked GOTV groups and current officeholders, for party or campaign purposes
  - (C) caucus lists
  - (D) absentee lists
  - (E) government bodies
  - (F) limited public reports
  - (G) law enforcement and court orders
  - (I) courts, for jury selection
  - (K), **added by PL 2025, c. 397**: "anyone may buy a voter file to evaluate, monitor, or seek compliance with voting rights, election administration, or registration laws."
  - Paragraph J was repealed in 2025. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A (Maine Legislature)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)
- Before October 18, 2021, a person could get bulk CVR data via Exception F to assess NVRA compliance. A later filing argued that the 2021 change made the data confidential again. — [search summary of Maine litigation filings incl. Democracy Docket MTD](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/58-mtd.pdf) [pre-2024]
- Members of the public may request the Party/Campaign Use Voter File under NVRA § 8(i). Requests use form MAR24 (Rev. 3/21/24). — [Maine SOS CVR Data Request Form, Rev. 3/21/24](https://www.maine.gov/sos/cec/elec/data/data-pdf/2024/MAR24%20(Rev.%203-21)%20CVR%20Data%20Request%20Form.pdf) (via search snippet)

**Maryland**
- The State Board of Elections "is authorized to sell, to qualified voters, their voter registration lists." County boards and the State Board describe the applicant as a **Maryland registered voter** who signs an oath. — [MD SBE, Voter Registration Data](https://elections.maryland.gov/voter_registration/data.html); [MD SBE application SBEAPPL, rev. March 2025 (search snippet)](https://elections.maryland.gov/pdf/SBEAPPL.pdf)
- Applications must arrive by the advance registration deadline (21 days before an election) or after election day; applications that arrive in between are returned. Data is delivered within 10 working days. — [MD SBE data page](https://elections.maryland.gov/voter_registration/data.html)

**Massachusetts**
- The statewide list "is not a public record" and goes only to statewide campaigns and political parties. M.G.L. c. 51 § 47C makes central-registry names and addresses non-public, but available to state party committees, statewide candidate committees, state ballot question committees and certain officials. Other candidates must request **municipal** lists from each city or town, and those local lists are public. — [MA Secretary of the Commonwealth, Access to Voter Information (search snippet)](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/voting-information/access-to-voter-information.htm); [M.G.L. c. 51 § 47C](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVIII/Chapter51/Section47C)
- A 2001 Attorney General opinion held that other registry data, such as party enrollment, must be released under the public records law. — [AG opinion 2001 (mirror)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/massachusetts/opinions/250158518-ago-01-02-01-october-11-2001) [pre-2024, possibly outdated]

**New Hampshire**
- **Local** public checklists go to "any person" in paper or electronic form. One electronic request per election is free; after that the fee is up to $25, or $25 plus $0.50 per thousand names over 2,500. — [RSA 654:31 II](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)
- **Statewide**: anyone may only *view* public checklist data at the State Records and Archives Center, with no printing, copying or transmitting. Only political parties, political committees (RSA 664:2) and candidates for county, state or federal office may obtain statewide (or district) lists. — [RSA 654:31 III–IV](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)

**New Jersey**
- N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1: the county clerk must furnish copies of the registry lists "to any voter applying." The 2023 text sets $0.25 per copy for each election district's list. The county clerk meets the request by delivering an electronic copy of the county list from the Statewide Voter Registration System. — [N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1 (Justia 2023, via search snippet)](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/nj/title-19-elections/nj-st-sect-19-31-18-1/)
- County practice varies. Ocean County takes requests via an OPRA request with an "Election Order form" and charges a flat $0.50 for a CD. Salem County charges $1.50 per district list. In Bergen County the Superintendent of Elections must approve purchases. — [Ocean County](https://co.ocean.nj.us/OC/Elections/frmInformationRequests.aspx); [Salem County](https://salemcountyclerk.org/election-services/); [Bergen County](https://www.co.bergen.nj.us/lists-labels)
- In 2017 the statewide file was reported as sold for 65 cents. — [Philadelphia Inquirer, 2017-07-17](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/news/politics/presidential/what-will-it-take-for-trumps-voter-fraud-panel-to-get-nj-data-65-cents-20170717.html) [pre-2024, possibly outdated]

**New York**
- A request must include a statement that the data will be used for an "elections purpose" under Election Law § 3-103(5). The Board says "elections purpose" "has traditionally been interpreted broadly", including campaigning, voter outreach, fundraising and academic research. Requests go to the Records Access Officer. — [NYSBOE FOIL page (search snippet; site returned 403)](https://elections.ny.gov/freedom-information-law-foil-requests); [NYSBOE page (snippet)](https://elections.ny.gov/node/86)

**Pennsylvania**
- The Department of State sells the **Full Voter Export (FVE)** by county for $20 through the online PA Voter Services portal. The data shown was current as of 11/09/2026. Authority: 25 Pa.C.S. § 1404(b)(1) and 4 Pa. Code § 183.14(b). — [PA Voter Services, Purchase PA FULL Voter Export](https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/PurchasePAFULLVoterExport.aspx)
- The basic voter data is "available to anyone" for $20. — [WITF, 2026-04-24](https://www.witf.org/2026/04/24/federal-judge-sides-with-pa-over-election-integrity-group-on-use-of-public-voter-registration-data/)
- The $20 charge has applied since July 12, 2006. — [PA DOS Request for Voter Lists](https://Www.Dos.Pa.Gov/VotingElections/Documents/Elections%20Division/requestvoterlists.pdf) (via snippet)

**Rhode Island**
- Rhode Island's public voter records are "available upon request to anybody who fills out an online form and pays a $25 fee." The order form, VoterListOrderForm.pdf, is on vote.sos.ri.gov. — [Rhode Island Current via Yahoo News, Sept 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/r-secretary-state-gregg-amore-153939618.html)
- R.I. Gen. Laws § 17-9.1-15: registration records and the lists of eligible voters are public records. — [§ 17-9.1-15 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/rhode-island/title-17/chapter-17-9-1/section-17-9-1-15/)
- Local example: Providence's Board of Canvassers sells city, ward and district lists for $25 on USB or by email. The city-wide list is not available by email. — [Providence Application for Voter List (2024)](https://providenceri.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/CVRS-Application.pdf)

**Vermont**
- "Any person" may request the full statewide checklist after filing a sworn affirmation with the Secretary of State (17 V.S.A. § 2154(c)). — [17 V.S.A. § 2154](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154)
- The form offers a one-time file at the next monthly issuing, or a standing monthly email subscription, with an optional .TXT format. — [VT SOS Request for Electronic Copy of the Statewide Checklist](https://outside.vermont.gov/dept/sos/Elections_Division/voters/faq/request_electronic_copy_vermont_statewide_checklist.pdf) [undated form, references a Jan. 2019 data-broker deadline; possibly outdated]

**District of Columbia**
- DCBOE's data request form lists "E-mail or Secure Link of Voter Registration Data – Microsoft Excel (**No Charge**)". The entire list is available only through a secure link, with a choice of "without voter history" or "with voter history". The form asks only for requester details and a signature, with no eligibility category or use affirmation. Fulfilment may take up to 15 business days under DC FOIA, § 2-532(c). — [DCBOE Request for Voter Registration Data, Rev. 3/7/23](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf) [pre-2024 form, possibly outdated]

### Inferences
- On access, the region splits into three tiers:
  - Open to any requester with an affirmation and a fee: CT, DE, PA, RI, VT, and DC free.
  - Open with a purpose or residency gate: NY (election purpose), MD (Maryland voters only), ME (only compliance-monitoring purposes, under the 2025 paragraph K).
  - Political actors only, statewide: NH, MA. Both keep local lists public.
- Even the "open" states mostly deliver the list on request, by email or secure link, rather than as an open download. This creates a record of who holds the list, and some states use that record for enforcement (PA refused VRF after a prior violation; see Q4).
- Maine's new paragraph K (2025) looks like a statutory response to *PILF v. Bellows* (1st Cir. 2024; see Q7). It channels NVRA-style access into a defined "compliance" purpose. This reading is my inference; the statute text does not say so.

### Gaps
- NY fee: I could not confirm whether the NYSBOE statewide file is free or charged, because the site was blocked (403).
- NJ: no current Division of Elections procedure or fee for a statewide file was found. Only county-level procedures and the 2017 report were found.
- RI: the statewide order form (VoterListOrderForm.pdf) was not retrieved, so formal eligibility wording is unconfirmed.
- VT: no fee is stated in § 2154 or on the form. Whether the list is free was not confirmed.
- DC: the statute that authorizes or limits voter-list release was not found.
- MD: the statute number (Election Law § 3-506 is commonly cited) was not verified in this session.

## Q2. Which fields are included, and which are withheld?

### Takeaway
Name, residential address, party and registration status are released everywhere they sell the list. There is a clear regional trend toward **year of birth only**: DE (long-standing), VT, ME (paragraphs F and K) and CT (from 2026) withhold month and day. PA still releases **full date of birth**, and New York's export layout contains full DOB, though whether it is released was not confirmed. Driver's licence numbers and SSN digits are withheld everywhere that has been documented. Phone and email are withheld from the public in VT and DE, though DE gives phone numbers to parties and candidates.

### Cited Findings
**Connecticut**
- Under **Public Act 26-1, § 96** (2026), the month and day of birth are exempt from disclosure. Only year of birth is released to the public. — [CT SOTS privacy program page](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/election-services/election-services/centralized-voter-registration-information---voter-privacy-program)

**Delaware**
- 15 Del. C. § 304(h) sets three tiers of contents:
  - **Public**: names, addresses, party, voting history, legislative district, **years of birth**.
  - **Parties and candidates**: the public fields plus **telephone numbers**.
  - **General Assembly and state or local government**: the above plus full **dates of birth**. — [DE request form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf); [DE Statewide Registered Voters File Documentation v1.23, 06/25/2026](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/VoterFileDocumentation.pdf)
- The documentation attributes this tiering to "House Bill 245 of the 145th General Assembly". — [same](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/VoterFileDocumentation.pdf)
- File layout (de_voter_reg.txt): county, status, title, first/middle/last/suffix name, **Voter_ID**, municipality, full residential address with carrier route and military flag, full mailing address with military and foreign flags, party, precinct (ED/RD), date of birth (shown as year of birth), registration date, party affiliation date, phone (blank for the public), 10 elections of voting history with method, last update date, congressional, legislative, county council, school and city districts, permanent absentee status, and per-election absentee ballot fields (see Q3). — [DE documentation v1.23](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/VoterFileDocumentation.pdf)

**Maine**
- The paragraph K voter file (2025) may contain the same fields as paragraph B. It **excludes** "highly sensitive" data: full DOB; SSN, tax ID or driver's licence numbers, or any portion of them; Address Confidentiality Program (ACP) participant records; addresses of voters who filed a signed statement under § 22; and data on registration declinations and the agency used. Paragraph F public reports give year of birth only and either first or last name, not both. Phone and email are not mentioned. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)

**Massachusetts**
- The public **local** voter list includes name, address, party, **date of birth**, active or inactive status and voter ID number. Phone, email, veteran status and permanent absentee status can be withheld. — [MA Secretary of the Commonwealth, Access to Voter Information (search snippet)](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/voting-information/access-to-voter-information.htm)

**New Hampshire**
- The statewide political-use list contains name, domicile address, mailing address, town or city, voter history and party affiliation. — [RSA 654:31 IV](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)

**New Jersey**
- County lists must not include voter **signatures** or the home address of a Daniel's Law "covered person" whose redaction the Office of Information Privacy has approved. — [N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1 (search snippets)](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1)
- In 2017 the statewide file was reported to contain voter ID (not SSN), address, **date of birth**, party, status and elections voted. — [Inquirer 2017](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/news/politics/presidential/what-will-it-take-for-trumps-voter-fraud-panel-to-get-nj-data-65-cents-20170717.html) [pre-2024, possibly outdated]

**New York**
- The NYSVOTER export layout (v2.4, 2022) includes a **DOB field (YYYYMMDD)**. — [NYSBOE Data File Layout for voter list exports (search snippet)](https://elections.ny.gov/foil-layout-voter-list) [2022 layout, possibly outdated]
- An NCSL summary lists New York's public access list as name, residence address and party enrollment. — [NCSL (snippet; page 403)](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/access-to-and-use-of-voter-registration-lists)
- Election Law § 3-112 gives the Attorney General copies of the "non-confidential fields" only. — [N.Y. Elec. Law § 3-112](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/3-112)
- These two sources point in different directions, so whether DOB actually reaches public requesters is unresolved.

**Pennsylvania**
- FVE fields: voter ID, name, sex, **date of birth**, date registered, status (active or inactive), date status last changed, party, residential address, mailing address, polling place, date last voted, all voting districts, voter history, and record last-changed date. — [PA Voter Services FVE page](https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/PurchasePAFULLVoterExport.aspx)
- WITF adds "often a phone number". — [WITF 2026-04-24](https://www.witf.org/2026/04/24/federal-judge-sides-with-pa-over-election-integrity-group-on-use-of-public-voter-registration-data/)
- In 2025 Pennsylvania offered DOJ the FVE "subject to the conditions" but withheld sensitive fields. — [PA DOS Election Data Handbook / PA DOJ response (snippet)](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/PA-DOJ-response.pdf)

**Rhode Island**
- The Secretary of State withholds driver's licence numbers and the last four SSN digits from the public list. — [Rhode Island Current via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/r-secretary-state-gregg-amore-153939618.html)

**Vermont**
- Confidential and exempt from public records: **month and day of birth**, driver's licence or non-driver ID number, **telephone number**, **email address**, and last four SSN digits (17 V.S.A. § 2154(b)(1)). — [17 V.S.A. § 2154](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154)

**District of Columbia**
- The form offers a list "without voter history" or "with voter history". — [DCBOE form Rev. 3/7/23](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf)

**All 12 (DOJ requests)**
- DOJ asked every one of the 12 for driver's licence numbers and partial SSNs. Each refused those fields or was sued for not providing them (see Q7). This confirms that these identifiers are not in any public file. — [Brennan Center DOJ tracker (updated 2026-11-10)](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information)

### Inferences
- The "minimum common public core" across the region is: name, residential (and usually mailing) address, party, status, district or precinct, and in most states voter history. Full DOB is becoming the main dividing line. Most of the region has moved, or is moving, to year of birth. The exceptions are PA and probably NY and MA (local lists), which still release full DOB.
- Delaware's three-tier field schedule (public / parties and candidates / government) is a concrete template for differentiated access by requester class.

### Gaps
- Field lists were not found for:
  - CT, beyond year of birth
  - MD (whether DOB, gender or phone are included)
  - RI
  - DC
  - MA's statewide party file
  - the current NJ statewide file
- Gender is confirmed only for PA ("sex"). Race and ethnicity were not found as a field in any of the 12.
- Signature withholding is explicit only for NJ.

## Q3. Is voter participation history included (and voting method)? Is there a separate absentee/mail-ballot list?

### Takeaway
Participation history is part of the list in nearly every jurisdiction that releases one:
- **DE**: last 10 elections, with method codes.
- **NH**: 2 years, including which party's primary.
- **PA**: voter history and date last voted.
- **ME**: paragraphs B and K.
- **RI**: optional, last 8 elections (local example).
- **DC**: optional.
- **NJ**: elections voted (2017 report).

Delaware's public file even carries per-voter absentee ballot tracking. Maine releases a free absentee list keyed by record number instead of name.

### Cited Findings
**Delaware**
- The file holds Voting_History_1–10 and Voting_Method_1–10 for the last 10 elections. Method codes: A = absentee (including vote-by-mail in 2020), E = early in person, P = polling place, V = provisional. — [DE documentation v1.23](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/VoterFileDocumentation.pdf)
- It also includes Perm_Abs_Status and, for absentee voters: election name, absentee reason code and description, voter type (UOCAVA or domestic), date the application was returned, ballot ID, date sent, method sent, ballot-sent address, date returned, ballot status and ballot-not-accepted reason. — [DE documentation v1.23](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/VoterFileDocumentation.pdf)
- Separate "Election Voted List" and "Absentee Data" request forms exist. — [DE election voted list request form](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/pdfs/election_voted_list_request_form.pdf); [DE absentee data request form](https://elections.delaware.gov/voter/absentee/pdfs/absentee_data_request_form.pdf) (titles from search results; not opened)

**Maine**
- Participation history is available under paragraph B (parties and campaigns) and in the paragraph K file. Paragraph F public reports give only the date of the last statewide election voted. Under paragraph D, **anyone** gets a free electronic list of absentee voters for an election, with the record number used instead of name and address. Printed lists cost a fee. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)

**New Hampshire**
- "Voter history" means whether a person voted in each state and municipal election over the preceding 2 years, and, for primaries, which party's primary. The statute does not separately address absentee voting. — [RSA 654:31 IV](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)

**Pennsylvania**
- The FVE includes "voter history" and "date last voted". The page warns that history for the 11/03/2026 election "is still being processed". It does not specify method codes or the number of elections. — [PA FVE page](https://www.pavoterservices.pa.gov/Pages/PurchasePAFULLVoterExport.aspx)

**Rhode Island (local)**
- Providence's list application offers a checkbox for "voter history from the last eight elections". — [Providence application](https://providenceri.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/CVRS-Application.pdf)

**District of Columbia**
- The list is available "with voter history". — [DCBOE form](https://www.dcboe.org/getmedia/6e2e6a71-294c-43a5-8bb1-02c151372f7b/Data-Request-Form_2023.pdf) [pre-2024]

**Massachusetts**
- Permanent absentee status may be withheld from local lists. — [MA SoC (snippet)](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/voting-information/access-to-voter-information.htm)

**New Jersey**
- Voter history files were changed from pipe-delimited txt to zipped csv and posted on the Division's reports page (2019 OPRA log). — [MuckRock NJ Division of Elections request](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/new-jersey-229/untitled-new-jersey-department-of-state-division-of-elections-84845/) [pre-2024]

**Delaware (statute)**
- Public files include "voting history" by statute (§ 304(h)). — [DE form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf)

### Inferences
- In this region, "who voted" (not how they voted) is treated as part of the public record in nearly all jurisdictions. Delaware also publishes how they voted (method) and mail-ballot processing status. That is the richest participation disclosure in the group and is relevant to any Korean debate over releasing turnout-by-person.
- Maine's design (absentee list with record numbers instead of names) is a privacy-preserving middle path: it allows aggregate or monitoring analysis without naming voters.

### Gaps
- Method codes and history depth were not found for PA, NY, MD, CT, RI-statewide or DC.
- Separate absentee or mail-ballot request lists were not confirmed for PA, MD, NY, NJ, CT, MA, VT, RI or DC.
- The DE absentee and voted-list forms were not opened.

## Q4. Use restrictions, certification/affidavit, penalties, citations

### Takeaway
Every jurisdiction that sells the list bans commercial use, either by statute or by affirmation. Several go further:
- Election-purpose only: NY, MD, DE.
- Ban on publishing online: PA by regulation; DE and ME-B by affirmation or statute.
- Ban on giving the list to federal agencies or foreign governments: VT.
- Ban on using it to investigate voters' behaviour in a specific election: MD.

Penalties range from perjury exposure (DE, MD, VT) through civil fines (ME: $1,000 / $5,000) to a misdemeanor (PA, and NH for individuals) or a felony (NH, for organizations).

### Cited Findings
**Connecticut**
- The SOTS page states no use restriction. — [CT SOTS](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/election-services/election-services/centralized-voter-registration-information---voter-privacy-program)

**Delaware**
- The requester swears under penalty of perjury that the data "will not be used for commercial solicitation or for any other purpose not related to the electoral process", and that they will not "make the information available to the public or third parties or publish or republish the information in a way that allows it to be used in that manner." — [DE form](https://elections.delaware.gov/public/forms/pdfs/statewide_vreg_order_form.pdf)

**Maine**
- Civil violations: commercial use; solicitation (except party or campaign); sale or resale (except as paragraphs B and K allow); discriminatory use (subsection 4, added in 2021); and use of B or K data outside the authorized purposes. Fines: up to **$1,000** for a first violation and up to **$5,000** for later ones. The criminal penalty, subsection 5, was **repealed in 2025**. Paragraph B recipients may not make identifying information publicly available online. — [21-A M.R.S. § 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)

**Maryland**
- The applicant signs, "under penalty of perjury", that the list will not be used "for commercial solicitation or any other purpose not related to the electoral process". **New Hampshire**

**New York**

## Q5. Protections for at-risk voters (ACP, judges, police, opt-outs)

### Takeaway
All jurisdictions with documented rules suppress at-risk voters' data through some mechanism:
- Address confidentiality programmes (ACPs): CT, ME, MA, NH.
- Self-declared safety opt-outs: CT, ME § 22.
- Statutory address shields for judges and police: NJ Daniel's Law; MA street-list opt-out for public-safety personnel.

New Jersey's Daniel's Law is the strongest: courts have upheld it even against republishing an address obtained lawfully from election records.

### Cited Findings
**Connecticut**
- **Voter Registry Privacy Program** (CGS 9-50(d)): anyone who swears under penalty of perjury that disclosure endangers their safety or their family's can have their name withheld from the public registry list. No move is required, and false statements are punishable under CGS 53a-157b. Separately, the ACP covers victims of family violence, sexual assault, stalking and child-injury risk who have moved, and provides a substitute address with mail forwarding. — [CT SOTS](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/election-services/election-services/centralized-voter-registration-information---voter-privacy-program)

**Maine**
- ACP participants are excluded from the K file and from court data. Their data is available only for inspection by authorized agencies. Addresses of voters who filed a § 22 signed statement are excluded from the K file. — [§ 196-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)

**Massachusetts**
- Confidential (ACP) voters get a special number in place of name and address. Under G.L. c. 51 § 4, law-enforcement and public-safety personnel may keep their names and addresses off the street list, and veteran status is shielded. — [MA SoC (snippet)](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/voting-information/access-to-voter-information.htm); [ebench summary of c. 51 § 4](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/massachusetts/statute/51:4) (secondary mirror)

**New Hampshire**
- A "nonpublic checklist" exists for voters legally entitled to keep their voter status private. — [RSA 654:31 I](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)

**New Jersey**
- **Daniel's Law** (P.L.2021, c.371; C.47:1B-3) covers active, former and retired judicial officers, law enforcement officers and related categories. Their home addresses are omitted from voter lists once the Office of Information Privacy approves redaction. Government, individuals and businesses are barred from publishing protected home addresses and phone numbers. — [N.J.S.A. 19:31-18.1 (snippet)](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-19/section-19-31-18-1); [Daniel's Law FAQs](https://danielslawredact.nj.gov/FAQs.aspx); [P.L.2021 c.371](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2020/PL21/371_.PDF)
- An NJ appellate court upheld enforcement against republishing an address that the requester (Kratovil) had obtained lawfully via OPRA from the Cape May Board of Elections, finding the law narrowly tailored. — [NJ App. Div. opinion A-6-24 (2025)](https://www.njcourts.gov/system/files/court-opinions/2025/a_6_24.pdf)
- 2024–2026 bills would extend redaction to elected officials and candidates (e.g., S562, introduced). — [S562 (2026)](https://pub.njleg.gov/Bills/2026/S1000/562_I1.HTM)

**Vermont**
- Phone, email and month and day of birth are confidential for everyone. — [§ 2154(b)(1)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154)

### Inferences
- The region uses two models. One is a narrow, opt-in shield for specific people (ACP, Daniel's Law, CT's safety affidavit). The other is a blanket redaction of specific fields for everyone (VT; CT and DE year of birth). Korea could combine both: a field-level baseline plus an opt-in suppression for at-risk groups.

### Gaps
- No ACP or opt-out rules were found in the voter-list context for DE, MD, NY, PA, RI, VT (beyond field confidentiality) or DC.
- No documented doxxing incidents traced to these states' voter files were found.

## Q6. Significant changes and litigation 2020–2026 (incl. VoteRef/PILF, mass challenges)

### Takeaway
The main legal fault line is whether the NVRA's public-disclosure provision (52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)) pre-empts state bans on republishing or reusing voter lists:
- The 1st Circuit (Maine, 2024) and the 10th Circuit (New Mexico) said yes.
- A Pennsylvania federal court (April 2026) upheld PA's internet-publication ban. The case is now on appeal in the 3rd Circuit.

States also kept narrowing sensitive fields during the period: CT (2026), ME (2025 rewrite) and VT (2025 amendment).

### Cited Findings
**Maine**
- *Public Interest Legal Foundation v. Bellows*, 92 F.4th 36 (1st Cir. Feb. 2, 2024): NVRA § 8(i)(1) covers Maine's Party/Campaign Use Voter File. Maine's use and publication restrictions, and the fines attached to them, are pre-empted. — [Justia, No. 23-1361](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca1/23-1361/23-1361-2024-02-02.html)
- Maine then enacted PL 2025, c. 397, which added paragraph K, the highly-sensitive exclusions and the civil violations, and repealed paragraph J and the criminal penalty. — [§ 196-A history note](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/21-A/title21-Asec196-A.html)

**Pennsylvania**
- *Voter Reference Foundation v. Schmidt* (M.D. Pa., Judge Saporito, April 2026) held that PA's ban on publishing the voter list online is not pre-empted by the NVRA, and rejected VRF's First Amendment claim. The court noted that its ruling runs counter to 1st and 10th Circuit decisions. — [WITF 2026-04-24](https://www.witf.org/2026/04/24/federal-judge-sides-with-pa-over-election-integrity-group-on-use-of-public-voter-registration-data/); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/pennsylvanias-voter-data-online-sharing-ban-stands-judge-says)
- The appeal is pending in the 3rd Circuit (No. 26-2280). EPIC filed an amicus brief supporting PA. — [EPIC](https://epic.org/epic-urges-third-circuit-to-uphold-pennsylvanias-protections-against-posting-voter-data-online/)
- VRF first requested PA data in 2022 and was refused because it would not agree not to publish. In 2024–25 it worked with GOP state representatives Jamie Flick and Eric Nelson to search for people who voted in more than one state. WITF reporting found that the method "would likely produce inaccurate results". — [WITF](https://www.witf.org/2026/04/24/federal-judge-sides-with-pa-over-election-integrity-group-on-use-of-public-voter-registration-data/)
- The 3rd Circuit's earlier PILF decision, 136 F.4th 456 (2025), found that the group seeking voter records lacked standing. — [Secretary Schmidt's MTD brief, 2026-03-23](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/66-2026-03-23-Secretary-Schmidts-brief-in-support-of-motion-to-dismiss.pdf)

**New Mexico (10th Circuit context)**
- In *VRF v. Torrez* the court held that the NVRA pre-empts New Mexico's limits on use and online publication. VRF had earlier pulled NM data under threat of prosecution. — [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/judge-rules-new-mexico-voter-data-usage-restrictions-violate-federal-law/); [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/voter-reference-foundation-v.-torrez:-nvra-preempts-state-limits-on-use-and-online-publication-of-voter-registration-data/view)

**Massachusetts**
- *Committee for Massachusetts Voter Identification Ballot Question v. Galvin*, D. Mass. 1:24-cv-12029 (filed Aug. 2024). The committee claims the statewide list and ERIC member data under the NVRA. Cross-motions for summary judgment were briefed by January 2025. — [Democracy Docket case page](https://democracydocket.com/cases/massachusetts-voter-roll-access-challenge); [Complaint](https://democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/12024-08-06-Complaint.pdf)
- 2025–26 bills: S.534 would keep central-registry names and addresses non-public; H.878 would make regional or statewide lists public records. — [S.534](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S534.Html); [H.878](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H878.pdf)

**Connecticut**
- Public Act 26-1, § 96 (2026) made month and day of birth exempt. — [CT SOTS](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/election-services/election-services/centralized-voter-registration-information---voter-privacy-program)

**Vermont**
- § 2154 was amended by 2021 No. 60 (eff. June 7, 2021) and 2025 No. 126 (Adj. Sess.) (eff. June 8, 2026). The federal-agency non-disclosure language dates from at least the 2017–2018 amendments (2017 No. 128 Adj. Sess., eff. May 16, 2018). — [§ 2154 history](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/17/043/02154); [H.624 (2018)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/Docs/BILLS/H-0624/H-0624%20As%20Passed%20by%20Both%20House%20and%20Senate%20Unofficial.pdf)

**New Hampshire**
- RSA 654:31 was amended in 2022 (ch. 54 and ch. 201) and in 2024 (ch. 335, eff. Oct. 1, 2024). — [RSA 654:31 source note](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LXIII/654/654-31.htm)

**Maryland**
- *Maryland Election Integrity, LLC v. Maryland SBE* (4th Cir., Feb. 4, 2025, Judge Wynn) affirmed dismissal for lack of standing of claims that Maryland's voter rolls and 2020–2022 elections were mismanaged. — [Maryland Matters](https://marylandmatters.org/2025/02/07/appeals-court-dismisses-suit-that-claimed-maryland-elections-voter-registration-are-flawed/)

**District of Columbia**
- A federal judge approved a settlement under which PILF receives access to DC voter registration data. — [Democracy Docket, DC voter roll access challenge](https://www.democracydocket.com/?p=18094)

**New Jersey**
- Daniel's Law litigation over republishing addresses taken from election records (see Q5). — [A-6-24](https://www.njcourts.gov/system/files/court-opinions/2025/a_6_24.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Korea, the US experience suggests that a "publish but restrict reuse" design is legally fragile where a higher-level disclosure mandate exists, as the NVRA does. Restrictions are more durable when they are built into what is released (fields) and who receives it (purpose gate, register of recipients), rather than relying on after-the-fact bans on republication.
- Groups engaged in mass voter-roll analysis or challenges (VRF/VoteRef, PILF) were the main litigants in PA, ME, MA and DC. Data purchases tied to cross-state "double voting" searches produced results described as unreliable (PA).

### Gaps
- No documented case was found in these 12 jurisdictions of purchased voter data being used for mass eligibility challenges (as seen in e.g. Georgia). The PA multi-state search is the closest example.
- The outcome of the Massachusetts committee case was not found.
- The status of PILF settlements or suits beyond DC was not found.
- What NH's 2024 ch. 335 changed was not determined.

## Q7. State responses to the 2025 DOJ demands for full voter rolls

### Takeaway
All 12 jurisdictions refused to hand DOJ the unredacted list (DL numbers, partial SSNs, full DOB). Several offered only the public version (NY, RI, PA, CT). According to the Brennan Center tracker (updated 2026-11-10), all 12 were then sued and all 12 suits have been dismissed. Dismissals in MA, RI, NY and VT were confirmed independently.

The Secretary of the State refused, saying "Federal and state law prohibit its disclosure." Connecticut was the 23rd state sued. — [CT SOTS Q&A, 2026-01-16](https://portal.ct.gov/sots/press-releases/2026-press-releases/questions-and-answers-about-the-lawsuit-filed-by-the-department-of-justice-against-ct)

On Sept. 16, 2025 Secretary Amore offered the public list free and refused the personal data "absent a demonstration of a proper legal basis ... or a court order", citing the Privacy Act and the E-Government Act. ### Inferences
- For a Korean policy comparison, the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic show three practical design levers:
  1. **Who** gets the list: anyone, residents, or political actors.
  2. **Which fields**: the trend is to year of birth only, no contact data, no identifiers.
  3. **Reuse controls**: purpose affirmations, no republication, recipient logging, seed records.
- The NVRA litigation shows that reuse bans are the most legally contested lever in the US. Field-level minimization has been uncontroversial.
