# Public access to statewide voter lists — US West (AK, AZ, CA, CO, HI, ID, MT, NV, NM, OR, UT, WA, WY)

Status date: November 12, 2026. Each state section below follows the same order: who may obtain the list → fields → participation history and mail-ballot data → use restrictions and penalties → cost and format → protections and opt-outs → changes 2020–2026 → response to the 2025 DOJ request. **[OLD]** marks a source dated before 2024, which may be out of date.

Method note: the NCSL compilation "Access to and Use of Voter Registration Lists" returned HTTP 403 and could not be read directly. The cross-state baseline here is the EAC table "Availability of State Voter File and Confidential Information" (PDF created Oct 30, 2020 **[OLD]**), with state statutes and official pages layered on top. The session's web-search budget ran out partway through. The last few items (Arizona litigation dates, Idaho list fields, VoteRef activity in Washington) therefore could not be searched further and are listed as gaps.

## Alaska

### Takeaway
Any person may buy the list, which includes names, addresses, and party. Age/DOB, SSN, DL, voter ID number, place of birth, and signature are confidential by statute. Any voter may ask to keep the residence address confidential. In December 2025 Alaska gave DOJ the full unredacted list under a confidential MOU, and that handover is now being challenged in state court.

### Cited Findings
- **Who:** "Any person may obtain a copy of the list, or a part of the list, or an electronic format containing both residence and mailing addresses of voters, by applying to the director and paying" the fee — AS 15.07.127 (2017-era text **[OLD]**) — [Justia AS 15.07.127](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-15/chapter-07/section-15-07-127/). The EAC rates Alaska "Open" — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Fields:** names, addresses, party affiliation — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf). The list includes residence and mailing addresses — [Justia AS 15.07.127 [OLD]](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-15/chapter-07/section-15-07-127/).
- **Withheld (AS 15.07.195(a)):** age or date of birth, SSN or any part of it, DL number, voter identification number, place of birth, signature — [FindLaw AS 15.07.195](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-15-elections/ak-st-sect-15-07-195/).
- **Participation history:** the sources conflict. NCSL/EAC entries do not list history. KTOO describes the list as containing a voter's name, "how often they've voted," and where they live — [KTOO](https://www.ktoo.org/?p=343870); [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Cost:** $20 for a list with names and party affiliation, plus $1 for a CD — [Peninsula Clarion, c. 2017 [OLD]](https://www.peninsulaclarion.com/news/alaska-to-provide-public-voter-data-if-records-fee-paid/). Another report gives $21 — [Juneau Empire [OLD]](https://www.juneauempire.com/news/trumps-request-for-voter-info-prompts-alaskans-privacy-concerns). EAC gives $20 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Opt-out:** under AS 15.07.195(b) a voter may ask in writing that the residential address be kept confidential, provided a separate mailing address is given. Even then it can be shown to poll watchers, recount observers (candidates, parties, interested groups), and the subject of a recall election — [FindLaw AS 15.07.195](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-15-elections/ak-st-sect-15-07-195/).
- **Exceptions (AS 15.07.195(c)):** confidential data may be released to local, state, or federal agencies for authorized governmental purposes, under a court order, to a holder of a writ of execution, with the voter's consent, or to other states for list maintenance under security standards — [FindLaw AS 15.07.195](https://codes.findlaw.com/ak/title-15-elections/ak-st-sect-15-07-195/).
- **DOJ:** DOJ's letter of July 2, 2025 asked for the voter roll and lists of election officials and of noncitizens — [Democracy Docket, Jul 18 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/all-the-states-where-doj-is-demanding-voting-data/). On Dec 23, 2025 the Lt. Governor's office announced that the list had been sent to DOJ under an MOU governing access, use, and protection — [Alaska Watchman, Dec 23 2025](https://alaskawatchman.com/2025/12/23/alaska-sends-voter-rolls-to-u-s-justice-dept-in-effort-to-ensure-accuracy). The state relies on AS 15.07.195(c)(1) (federal agency, "governmental purposes authorized under law") — [Alaska Beacon, Feb 13 2026](https://alaskabeacon.com/2026/02/13/alaska-permits-federal-government-to-purge-individual-alaskans-from-voter-list/). Alaska is one of two states that signed an MOU under which it will "clean" its roll within 45 days of DOJ's analysis — [Alaska Beacon, Mar 16 2026](https://alaskabeacon.com/2026/03/16/repub/in-bid-for-voter-data-trumps-doj-lays-groundwork-to-undermine-confidence-in-midterms/). The League of Women Voters of Alaska and the Alaska Black Caucus sued in state court (complaint filed Apr 21, 2026), arguing the handover violated the Alaska Constitution's privacy, due-process, and voting-rights protections — [ACLU case page](https://www.aclu.org/cases/league-of-women-voters-of-alaska-et-al-v-nancy-dahlstrom-et-al); [Complaint](https://www.acluak.org/app/uploads/2026/04/4.21.2026-League-of-Women-Voters-et-al-v.-Dahlstrom-et-al-COMPLAINT.pdf). The state moved to dismiss, and briefing ran through August 2026 — [EPIC-hosted reply brief, Aug 2026](https://epic.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/LWV-AK-v.-Dahlstrom-Defs-Reply-in-Support-of-Motion-to-Dismiss-08.26.pdf).

### Inferences
- Alaska shows that a state can have an "open" public list and still hand the confidential fields to the federal government through a broad "government agency" exception. A statutory exception for "government purposes" is therefore the weak point to watch in any design.

### Gaps
- Current fee, format, and whether the public list carries participation history: the Division of Elections pages returned 503/TLS errors. Not found.
- Outcome of LWV v. Dahlstrom (Anchorage Superior Court 3AN-26-06319CI): no ruling found.

## Arizona

### Takeaway
The list is restricted by purpose. Anyone may buy it, but only for political, campaign, election, or redistricting uses. Commercial use and posting online are class 6 felonies. The list is rich: phone, birth year, occupation, four years of voting history, and early-voting data. Parties get it free, including daily early-ballot request lists. Judges, police, and others at risk can have their records sealed by court order.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and uses (A.R.S. § 16-168(E)):** copies are sold for an authorized use only: political or party activity, campaigns, elections, redistricting, or other uses authorized by law. Commercial use (as defined in § 39-121.03) is prohibited. Reselling to a candidate or political committee for an authorized use does not count as commercial — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm).
- **Free to parties (§ 16-168(C)–(D)):** county and state chairs of qualifying parties receive precinct lists free. Within 33 days before an election they get a daily list of early-ballot requesters and a weekly list of returned early ballots. In counties over 800,000 they may also request a daily returned-ballot list — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm).
- **Fields:** name, party preference, registration date, residence address, mailing address, ZIP, phone (if given), birth year, occupation (if given), voting history for the prior four years, and early-voting data — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm); same list in [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Withheld (§ 16-168(F)):** month and day of birth, SSN (full or partial), DL/ID number, Indian census number, father's name or mother's maiden name, state or country of birth, signatures, and email address (never releasable) — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm).
- **Restrictions and penalty:** holders may not let lists be used, bought, sold, or transferred for unauthorized purposes, and may not post the data online or otherwise make it internet-accessible. Violating (E) or (F) is a **class 6 felony** — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm). Recorders must produce authorized requests within 30 days — [Pima County Recorder](https://recorder.pima.gov/RequestVoterData).
- **Cost:** sliding scale, from a $93.75 base plus $0.0005 per record (under 125k records) up to a $328.13 base plus $0.0000625 per record (1M+ records) — [A.R.S. § 16-168](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00168.htm). EAC estimated $516 statewide — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **At-risk protection (A.R.S. § 16-153):** "eligible persons" may ask that the public be barred from their voter record and precinct number. The category includes peace officers, prosecutors, public defenders, code-enforcement and corrections officers, judges and public officials, people protected by an order of protection or injunction against harassment, and household members. They file an affidavit, through the presiding judge of the superior court, stating why their life or safety is in danger, and the record is sealed for five years — [A.R.S. § 16-153](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/16/00153.htm).
- **2025–26:** SB 1040 (2026 session) concerns "voter registration roll internet access," and HB 2425 (2025) would have amended § 16-168. Enactment was not confirmed — [SB 1040 prefiled text](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/2R/bills/SB1040P.htm); [HB 2425](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/bills/HB2425P.htm).
- **DOJ:** DOJ's letter of May 20, 2025 alleged improper identity verification and warned of a lawsuit. The Secretary of State answered on June 2, 2025 that Arizona complies with federal law — [Democracy Docket, Jul 18 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/all-the-states-where-doj-is-demanding-voting-data/). DOJ later sued for the full list, and the case was dismissed — [CBS News, updated Nov 10 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/); [Brennan Center tracker, updated Nov 10 2026](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information).

### Inferences
- My own arithmetic from the statutory schedule: a roughly 4-million-record statewide file would cost about $328 + 4M × $0.0000625 ≈ $580. The voter count is my assumption, not a sourced figure.
- Arizona is the clearest Western example of the model "rich data, but felony-backed purpose limits and an online-posting ban," with a judge-gated sealing process for at-risk people.

### Gaps
- Filing date, judge, and dismissal date of the DOJ suit against Arizona: not found (search budget exhausted).
- Whether SB 1040 (2026) passed: not found.

## California

### Takeaway
California restricts the list by requester and purpose: candidates, ballot-measure committees, and anyone with an election, scholarly, journalistic, or political purpose, plus governmental uses the Secretary of State approves. The SOS screens each application. The statewide file costs $100 and includes **full date of birth**, phone and email, and, on request, participation history with voting method (polling place or mail). Commercial use, online display, and sending the data outside the US are prohibited, and misuse is a misdemeanor. Several confidential-voter programs remove at-risk people entirely.

### Cited Findings
- **Who:** candidates for federal, state, or local office; committees for or against qualified initiatives or referenda; any person for "election, scholarly, journalistic, or political purposes"; and governmental purposes as determined by the SOS. Confidential voters are excluded — [CA SOS file-request page](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request); [Elec. Code § 2194(a)(3)](https://california.public.law/codes/elections_code_section_2194). A county application lists the approved purposes as "election, scholarly, journalistic, political, record review, vendor, or governmental" — [SLO County application](https://slocounty.ca.gov/getattachment/2f303595-2b4f-4237-9f62-43dd05270fa2/Application-for-Confidential-Voter-Registration-Information.aspx).
- **Process:** a written application (California Voter Registration File Request) goes to the SOS, or to a county for county-level data. The SOS checks completeness and whether the proposed use is permitted, which takes 7–14 business days — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request).
- **Fields, Voter Registration Data File:** name, **date of birth**, registration date, residential and mailing address, precinct, contact information, and political party preference. Active voters are included by default, inactive voters only on request. A Political Districts file is also available — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request).
- **Participation history:** a separate Voter Participation History File, on request, gives election type, election date, and **voting method (polling place, conditional registration, or mail)**, but not ballot choices — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request).
- **Withheld (§ 2194(b)):** DL/state ID number, SSN (last four), other unique identifiers, and signature or signature image. Information on voters who failed to sign a vote-by-mail envelope or had a mismatched signature is confidential under § 2194(f)(1) — [Elec. Code § 2194](https://california.public.law/codes/elections_code_section_2194); [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request). Pre-registrants aged 16–17 are excluded — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request).
- **Use restrictions:** no personal, private, or commercial use (harassment, advertising, solicitation, sales, marketing). The data may not be reproduced in print or broadcast or displayed online except as § 2194(a)(3) allows — [Elec. Code § 2194(a)(2)](https://california.public.law/codes/elections_code_section_2194). It may not be sent outside the United States (§ 2188.5), and misuse is a misdemeanor (§ 18109) — [CA regulations, 2 CCR §§ 19001–19013](https://www.sos.ca.gov/administration/regulations/current-regulations/elections/access-voter-registration-information).
- **Cost and format:** statewide file $100. Single records cost $30 each, capped at $100 and 10 records per application. County files are priced by each county. Delivery is three tab-delimited text files — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request). EAC listed $15,000 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf). This conflicts with the current SOS price, and the SOS figure is preferred.
- **At-risk protections:** confidential status under Elec. Code §§ 2166, 2166.5, 2166.7, and 2166.8 removes the voter from released files — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request). Under § 2166.7, public safety officers (where the county board of supervisors authorizes it) may apply under penalty of perjury, citing a "life-threatening circumstance," to keep their residence address, phone, and email confidential for up to two years (renewable). They must become vote-by-mail voters and are left out of all lists — [Elec. Code § 2166.7](https://california.public.law/codes/elections_code_section_2166.7). Confidential elected officials and candidates (§ 2166.9) may have contact information released only for bona fide journalistic or governmental purposes — [CA SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-registration/voter-registration-information-file-request).
- **DOJ:** DOJ sued, and a federal judge rejected the suit — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/). DOJ appealed to the 9th Circuit, which heard argument in May 2026. As of Sept 22, 2026 no decision had issued — [Democracy Docket, Sep 22 2026](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/).

### Inferences
- California's model — anyone may apply, but the SOS reviews the stated purpose and keeps a ban on online display — is the closest US analogue to a "purpose-screened release" design. It still releases full DOB to approved requesters, which is notably more than most open states release.

### Gaps
- District-court judge and date of the CA dismissal, and the 9th Circuit's status after September 2026: not found.
- Exact scope of §§ 2166 / 2166.5 / 2166.8: not read in full.

## Colorado

### Takeaway
The list is fully open: the SOS must sell it to anyone "without regard to how these people might use the information." The statewide registration list costs $50 and the master voter history list another $50. Fields include birth year, party, phone, gender identity, and full participation history. Any voter who believes they or a household member face criminal harassment or bodily harm can become a "confidential voter" for $5.

### Cited Findings
- **Who:** the SOS must provide "public record" information to anyone, "without regard to how these people might use the information." Accredited media and academics pay reduced rates — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html).
- **Public fields:** full name, residential address, party affiliation and affiliation date, phone (if provided), gender identity (if provided), birth year, and whether the voter voted in prior elections — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html).
- **Withheld:** SSN, DL number, month and day of birth, signature, email — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html). Pre-registrants under 18 are excluded (C.R.S. 1-2-227) — [Arapahoe County](https://arapahoevotes.gov/node/31).
- **Participation history:** the Master Voter History List is available for each statewide election from the 2012 primary — [J-PAL admin data catalog (third-party)](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/admindatacatalog/colorado-voter-records). Data on mail ballots and in-person voting at Voter Service and Polling Centers is available countywide from county officials and statewide from the SOS — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html).
- **Cost:** Statewide Registered Voter List (EX-003) $50; Master Voter History List Export (EX-002) $50; Election Cycle Subscription $1,000. The fee schedule was revised Jan 2, 2024 — [CO SOS fee schedule](https://coloradosos.gov/pubs/info_center/fees/elections.html). Data disc requests cost $50 per report — [CO SOS data request form](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/forms/dataRequests.pdf).
- **Use restrictions:** none stated on the SOS FAQ — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html).
- **Confidential voter:** a voter who believes they or a household member "will be exposed to criminal harassment or bodily harm" may file a confidentiality form with the county clerk and pay $5. The voter's information is then not released to the public — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html). County files exclude confidential voters under C.R.S. 24-72-204 — [Boulder County](https://bouldercounty.gov/elections/maps-and-data/data-access/). Address Confidentiality Program participants (survivors of domestic violence, sexual offenses, or stalking) have no voter information released — [CO SOS FAQ](https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/FAQs/VoterRegistrationData.html).
- **DOJ:** on May 12, 2025 DOJ demanded "all records" of the 2024 election. On May 27, 2025 the SOS provided the current public voter file and voter history datasets — [Democracy Docket, Jul 18 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/all-the-states-where-doj-is-demanding-voting-data/). DOJ sent a proposed "confidential memorandum of understanding" dated Dec 1, 2025, then sued, and the case was dismissed — [Brennan Center tracker](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/).

### Inferences
- Colorado pairs maximal openness (cheap, purpose-blind, with history) with a low-friction self-certification opt-out ($5, a stated belief of risk, no court involvement). That combination is directly relevant to a "publish but let at-risk people opt out" design.

### Gaps
- Date and judge of the Colorado DOJ dismissal: not found. A 2025 SOS press release exists ([PR20251118Letter](https://coloradosos.gov/pubs/newsRoom/pressReleases/2025/PR20251118Letter.html)) but was not read.

## Hawaii

### Takeaway
Hawaii is the most restrictive state in the region. Only name, district/precinct, and voter status are public. Addresses and other data go only to requesters with an election or government purpose (for example, campaigns), and commercial use or redistribution is unlawful. A voter can keep even the public fields confidential on showing "good cause." DOJ's suit is pending, stayed behind the 9th Circuit appeals.

### Cited Findings
- **Public vs. confidential (HRS § 11-97(a)):** only the voter's name, district/precinct, and voter status are public. Other registration information is confidential except for election or government purposes defined by the chief election officer's rules — [HRS § 11-97 (1999 archive) [OLD]](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrsarchive/hrs1999/Vol01/hrs011/HRS_11-97.htm); [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Who and uses:** campaigns and other requesters may get addresses for government or election purposes. Under HAR § 3-177-160 the permitted uses include supporting or opposing candidates or ballot measures and encouraging registration and turnout. The information may be used only for the purpose stated in the request, and selling or commercial distribution is prohibited — [Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Jul 19 2022 [OLD]](https://staradvertiser.com/?p=1150683). HRS § 11-14(d) makes it unlawful to use, print, publish, or distribute registration information except as § 11-97 allows — [Justia HRS § 11-14 (2019) [OLD]](https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/2019/title-2/chapter-11/section-11-14/).
- **Public-records law:** the Office of Information Practices treats the county General County Register as not public under the UIPA when requested for purposes other than "government or elections" — [OIP opinion](https://ags.hawaii.gov/oip/?p=1147).
- **Cost:** about $500 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Opt-out:** a voter may ask to keep name, address, and other information confidential on showing "good cause," such as risk of bodily harm (HAR § 3-177-159) — [Star-Advertiser 2022 [OLD]](https://staradvertiser.com/?p=1150683).
- **DOJ:** DOJ sued (United States v. Nago) for the full unredacted file — [ACLU case page](https://www.aclu.org/cases/united-states-v-nago?document=Complaint). The case is stayed pending the 9th Circuit's rulings in the CA and OR appeals — [Democracy Docket, Sep 22 2026](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/). It is listed as one of two pending cases as of Nov 10, 2026 — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/).

### Inferences
- Hawaii is the Western model closest to a "roll available for checking, but not for bulk distribution" approach: identity, precinct, and status are public, and everything else is purpose-gated.

### Gaps
- Whether participation history is released to election-purpose requesters: not found.
- Current statute text after 2022 and the current fee: not verified.
- Filing date of US v. Nago: not found.

## Idaho

### Takeaway
The list is open: anyone gets it for the reproduction cost (historically about $20). The only statutory limit is a ban on using it for commercial advertising or solicitation, and "any political purpose" is expressly allowed. In 2020 a political group posted the daily absentee-ballot list (names, addresses, ballot dates) online, and the SOS said this was legal. Idaho refused DOJ's demand for sensitive data and was sued in April 2026. That case is stayed.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and uses (Idaho Code § 34-437A(3)):** any person who requests a copy of the statewide list "shall be furnished the same." Recipients may not use the information to mail or deliver advertisements, offers, or solicitations for money, services, or anything of value, but may use it for "any political purpose" — [Idaho Code § 34-437A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437A).
- **Cost:** a fee compensating the state for reproduction — [Idaho Code § 34-437A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437A). EAC gives $20 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf). An SOS order form lists "Voter Registration Information ($20/report)" — [Idaho SOS PRR form](https://sos.idaho.gov/downloads/PRR_Absentee_VoterReg_Report.pdf). The absentee voter list is available by email through the SOS "Candidate Vault" for a $50 annual fee — [Bingham County 2026 form](https://www.binghamid.gov/media/Departments/Elections/Candidate%20Packet%20Information/2026/15-2026-Fillable-Election-Public-Record-Request.pdf).
- **Fields:** the statute says the list contains each voter's "name and registration information" without defining it — [Idaho Code § 34-437A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title34/T34CH4/SECT34-437A). EAC lists name, address, precinct, with confidential fields "not specified" — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Mail-ballot list and online republication:** in May 2020 Idaho Freedom Action, the political arm of the Idaho Freedom Foundation, posted a daily-updated spreadsheet of every voter who requested an absentee ballot for the May 19 primary (280,214 names that day). It showed first, middle, and last names, street and mailing addresses, and the dates ballots were received and returned. The SOS chief deputy said nothing in the statute prohibited posting the list "as long as they are not selling it" — [East Idaho News / Idaho Statesman, May 13 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2020/05/a-political-group-is-posting-idaho-voters-names-addresses-to-its-website-%E2%81%A0-and-its-legal/).
- **DOJ:** Secretary of State Phil McGrane declined to give DOJ sensitive voter data — [Idaho Capital Sun, Mar 3 2026](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/03/03/idaho-secretary-of-state-wont-give-us-justice-department-sensitive-voter-information/). DOJ sued on April 1, 2026 for the full unredacted file, including partial SSN and DL numbers. McGrane argues he has no "clear legal duty" to share it — [Idaho Capital Sun, Apr 1 2026](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/04/01/trump-administration-sues-idaho-election-official-to-force-him-to-turn-over-sensitive-voter-data/). Judge B. Lynn Winmill granted Idaho's request for a stay on May 21 (2026), over DOJ's opposition. This came from a search summary of Idaho coverage, and the exact source page was not confirmed; see [Democracy Docket case page](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/idaho-doj-voter-data-access-challenge/) and [Boise State Public Radio, May 19 2026](https://www.boisestatepublicradio.org/politics-government/2026-05-19/primary-election-department-of-justice-suing-idaho-for-voter-records). The May 19 article predates May 21, so it cannot be the source of the stay date. The case is stayed pending the 9th Circuit appeals — [Democracy Docket, Sep 22 2026](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/).

### Inferences
- Idaho shows the "open plus a commercial-use ban only" model, under which near-real-time republication of mail-ballot lists online is lawful. That is a concrete precedent for the risks of publishing lists that update during an election.

## Montana

### Takeaway
The list is open for **noncommercial** use: anyone may buy it for $1,000 per extract or $5,000 for a year's subscription. It comes with participation history back to 2006, an absentee indicator, an absentee-ballot report, and even a petition-signers report. The Montana Supreme Court upheld the high fees in 2024. What Montana sent DOJ is disputed, and the legislature subpoenaed the SOS in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and uses:** "Upon request, the Secretary of State shall furnish to any individual, for noncommercial use, available extracts and reports from the statewide voter registration system," citing MCA 13-2-122. Requesters are told to consult an attorney on what counts as noncommercial — [MT SOS voter file page](https://sosmt.gov/elections/voter-file/); [MCA 13-2-122](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0130/chapter_0020/part_0010/section_0220/0130-0020-0010-0220.html).
- **Cost:** $1,000 for a single copy of the complete statewide file or of an extract (such as the Absentee Ballot Report). $5,000 for a 365-day subscription with unlimited downloads of the voter file, absentee ballot report, and petition signers report. Fees are set in ARM 44.3.1101 — [MT SOS voter file page](https://sosmt.gov/elections/voter-file/).
- **Contents:** two spreadsheets, one of currently registered voters and one of their voting history. History goes back to at least 2006 for all counties. The file has an "Absentee indicator" (true/false). The Absentee Ballot Report covers voters sent an absentee ballot and its status, for elections within ±45 days. The Petition Signers Report is a county-by-county record of who signed a given petition. Field lists are in ARM 44.3.1101 — [MT SOS voter file page](https://sosmt.gov/elections/voter-file/).
- **Withheld:** DL, whole or partial SSN, and address information of protected individuals — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Litigation over fees:** in ACORN International v. State (Mont. 2024), the Montana Supreme Court held that the Secretary may lawfully charge fees for the voter file and that the fees do not violate the state constitution's "right to know" — [ACORN Int'l v. State, DA 23-0382](https://statecourtreport.org/sites/default/files/fastcase/converted/ACORN%20Int%27l%20v.%20State%2C%20Mont.%20DA%2023-0382.pdf).
- **DOJ (conflicting accounts):** DOJ asked for the full file with DL numbers, SSN4, and birth dates. The SOS told the legislature that in August 2025 it gave DOJ the public statewide list "available to any member of the public" and has not released an unredacted file. Montana Free Press reported an unredacted file was provided after Aug 14, 2025, which the SOS denies. Legislators voted to subpoena the SOS in September 2026 — [Daily Montanan, Sep 14 2026](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/09/14/secretary-of-state-responds-to-legislative-subpoena-with-pushback/); [Daily Montanan, Sep 3 2026](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/09/03/legislators-to-subpoena-secretary-of-state-on-voter-roll-question/). The Brennan Center tracker says Montana provided a voter file with SSN4 only for voters who registered without a DL number — [Brennan Center tracker](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information). Montana declined to sign DOJ's proposed MOU and said it would watch other states' litigation — [Democracy Docket (reported in search summary)](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/trump-doj-voter-roll-crusade-contradictions/). Montana was not sued (not on the CBS or Brennan sued lists).

### Inferences
- Montana's petition-signers report shows that some states publish not only who is registered and who voted, but also who signed which petition. The price, not the eligibility rules, acts as the main gate.

### Gaps
- Field list in ARM 44.3.1101 (whether DOB or birth year, phone, or email are included): not retrieved. Voter opt-out options: not found.

## Nevada

### Takeaway
Nevada is very open and releases **full date of birth**. County lists are sold to any person at 1 cent per name and must include address, DOB, phone, and registration serial number. Parties get them free. Only SSN, DL/ID number, and email are withheld by law. **Any voter may, on written request, have their address and phone withheld** with no showing of danger required. A separate Confidential Address Program serves high-risk people.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and cost (NRS 293.440(1)):** "Any person" may obtain a copy of the precinct, district, or county list from the county clerk at 1 cent per name. Major-party state and county central committees and minor-party executive committees get copies free — [NRS 293.440 (Nevada Legislature)](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html).
- **Fields (NRS 293.440(2)):** except as provided in NRS 293.5002 and 293.558, the list "must indicate the address, date of birth, telephone number and the serial number on each application." Computerized lists include the date of the latest change — [NRS 293.440](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html). The voter ID number must be disclosed (NRS 293.558(1)) — [NRS 293.558](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html).
- **Withheld (NRS 293.558):** SSN and DL/ID number, and email addresses given for voting purposes, which may not be released to third parties. "No information relating to a registered voter may be withheld from the public" other than these, address and phone on request, and information other law makes confidential — [NRS 293.558](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html).
- **Opt-out (NRS 293.558(3)):** "A registered voter may submit a written request to the county or city clerk to have his or her address and telephone number withheld from the public." The clerk must then leave them off all public lists — [NRS 293.558](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html). Washoe County notes that such voters' names still appear, and that party and whether they voted are not hidden — [Washoe County](https://washoecounty.gov/voters/register/confidential.php). The Confidential Address Program offers a substitute address for high-risk voters — [Washoe County](https://washoecounty.gov/voters/register/confidential.php).
- **Statewide list:** access is a two-step process: an SOS Online Services account plus a signed "Official Request for List of Registered Voters." Once access is granted, users can filter by county or district, choose a format, and view voter history where available. Recipients of free information may not use it for non-election purposes or sell it (NRS 293.440) — [NV SOS page (UAT domain; form rev. Oct 18, 2018) [OLD]](https://nvsosuat.nv.gov/elections/voters/nevvoter-statewide-list). The statutory no-sale and election-only limit applies to party committees receiving free lists — [NRS 293.440(6)](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html).
- **Newspaper publication:** county clerks may publish an alphabetical list of all registered voters with precincts in newspapers, or give the list free on request — [NRS 293.557](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-293.html).
- **Cost (aggregate):** EAC listed $20,000 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **DOJ:** on June 25, 2025 DOJ demanded the voter rolls and posed 14 compliance questions — [Democracy Docket, Jul 18 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/all-the-states-where-doj-is-demanding-voting-data/). DOJ sued, and the suit was rejected — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/); [Brennan Center tracker](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information).

### Inferences
- The EAC's $20,000 figure is consistent with the 1-cent-per-name county rate applied to roughly 2 million voters (my inference), while the SOS statewide portal appears to be free for approved requesters. The sources conflict, and the current SOS fee is unverified.
- Nevada is unique in the region in combining full-DOB release with an unconditional "withhold my address and phone" right for any voter.

### Gaps
- Current production SOS statewide-list page and fee (only a test-domain page from 2018 was found). Who qualifies for the statewide portal (any person vs. election-related): not confirmed.
- Date and judge of the Nevada DOJ dismissal: not found.

## New Mexico

### Takeaway
The list is restricted by purpose and affidavit. Requesters must swear the data will be used only for governmental or election-campaign purposes, will not be shared outside their organization, and will not be made publicly accessible on the internet. Violations are a fourth-degree felony with a $100-per-line fine. The internet ban was added in 2023 after VoteRef posted New Mexico data online. Federal courts then held that the NVRA preempts New Mexico's restrictions, the 10th Circuit affirming on Nov 25, 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Who and affidavit (NMSA § 1-4-5.5, as amended by 2023 HB 4):** county clerks and the SOS furnish voter data, mailing labels, or special voter lists only on written request, and all requesters are treated equally on charges. Each requester signs an affidavit that the data will be used for "governmental or election campaign purposes only," "shall not be transferred, copied, shared or conveyed to any person outside the requesting party's agency or organization," "shall not be made accessible by the general public on the internet or through other means," and will not be used unlawfully — [2023 HB 4 bill text, §3](https://nmlegis.gov/Sessions/23%20Regular/bills/house/HB0004.PDF). This is the introduced version. The SOS later told the court HB 4 mooted vagueness claims, which indicates enactment — [SOS MSJ brief, Apr 14 2023](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/1212023-4-14-Defs-msj.pdf).
- **Definitions:** "election campaign purposes" means use by a campaign in an election. "Governmental purposes" means noncommercial use by a government agency or organization to analyze the structure, operation, or decision-making of government. The pre-2023 wording was the broader "relating in any way to" — [2023 HB 4](https://nmlegis.gov/Sessions/23%20Regular/bills/house/HB0004.PDF); [Justia NMSA 1-4-5.5 (2020) [OLD]](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/2020/chapter-1/article-4/section-1-4-5-5/).
- **Withheld (§ 1-4-5.5(B)):** SSN, codes identifying the agency where the voter registered, day and month of birth, and phone numbers if the voter so requests — [2023 HB 4](https://nmlegis.gov/Sessions/23%20Regular/bills/house/HB0004.PDF); [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Penalty (§ 1-4-5.6):** unlawful use means selling, loaning, giving access to, or surrendering data for prohibited purposes, or causing data that identifies a voter (name, mailing or residence address) "to be made publicly available on the internet or through other means." It is a **fourth-degree felony**, with a fine of $100 for each line of voter information unlawfully used — [2023 HB 4 §4](https://nmlegis.gov/Sessions/23%20Regular/bills/house/HB0004.PDF).
- **Cost:** about $5,215 statewide — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **VoteRef litigation:** the SOS rejected the Voter Reference Foundation's May 27, 2022 request (submitted with the required affidavits), partly because VRF would post the data online — [D.N.M. Doc. 177 (2024)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-mexico/nmdce/1:2022cv00222/472295/177/). Judge Browning found that the NVRA mandates disclosure of the requested data and that VRF could not be prosecuted under the state provisions for posting it — [KRQE](https://www.krqe.com/news/politics-government/federal-judge-rules-new-mexico-officials-violated-national-voter-registration-act/). The 10th Circuit (No. 24-2133, Nov 25, 2025) affirmed that the NVRA preempts New Mexico's restrictions on using and sharing voter data — [Justia, VRF v. Torrez](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca10/24-2133/24-2133-2025-11-25.html); [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10742190/voter-reference-foundation-v-torrez/) (holding reported from search summaries; the full opinion returned 403 and was not read).
- **DOJ:** DOJ sued, and the suit was dismissed — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/); [Brennan Center tracker](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information).

### Inferences
- New Mexico is the leading US test of whether a state can ban online republication of a voter list it sells. The federal NVRA public-disclosure provision (52 U.S.C. § 20507(i)) has so far defeated that ban in the 10th Circuit. That matters for any comparison: US state-level secrecy rules are constrained by federal transparency law.

### Gaps
- The exact scope of the 10th Circuit's preemption holding (all of §§ 1-4-5.5/5.6 or only parts), and whether New Mexico sought rehearing or certiorari: not verified.
- Current fee and field list: not verified.

## Oregon

### Takeaway
Oregon, an all-mail state, gives anyone the statewide list for $500, and parties get it free. The list includes birth year, phone, party, participation history, and, **during each election period, a daily-updated ballot-status list** showing whether each voter has returned a ballot. Commercial use is banned. Any voter may ask to have their address withheld by showing a safety danger. Election workers have been covered since 2022, and officeholders and candidates can limit address exposure (2023).

### Cited Findings
- **Who (ORS 247.945):** the county clerk "shall deliver to any person a list of electors." The Secretary of State "shall deliver to any person a statewide list of electors" for a $500 fee. ACP participants and non-disclosable data are excluded, and clerks keep a record of every recipient — [ORS 247.945](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html); [OAR 165-002-0020](https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_165-002-0020). Qualified parties get the statewide list free, at most twice per election (ORS 247.940) — [ORS 247.940](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html).
- **Public fields (ORS 247.948(1)):** party; residence address; ballot mailing address; birth year; precinct name or number and precinct split; SOS administrative number; telephone number; "whether or not an elector voted in previous elections"; and, during an election period, ballot status (whether the elector has cast a ballot), which the SOS updates **each business day** — [ORS 247.948](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html).
- **Withheld (ORS 247.948(2)):** birth month, day of birth, SSN, DL number, signature, and ACP/247.965 data — [ORS 247.948](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html); [Read letter to DOJ, Aug 21 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/OR-voter-rolls-response.pdf).
- **Format:** the SEL 510 request form offers "registration only" or "registration and vote history." A statewide list is 11 zipped .txt files — [SEL 510 (Morrow County copy)](https://www.morrowcountyor.gov/sites/default/files/fileattachments/clerk/page/4551/sel510.pdf).
- **Use restrictions (ORS 247.955):** no use "for commercial purposes," except resale to candidates or political committees for political purposes only — [ORS 247.955](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html).
- **Protections (ORS 247.965):** "Any elector may request" that the clerk keep the residence address of the elector and household confidential. The clerk must grant it if the elector shows that personal or family safety is in danger, or the elector is an identified election worker (added 2022 c.114). Exempt voters automatically receive a ballot by mail — [ORS 247.965](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html). Candidates and officeholders who limit public access to their residence address under ORS 249.021 are left off delivered lists (ORS 247.948(3), 2023 c.528) — [ORS 247.948](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html). Disclosure of a protected address requires a court order or law-enforcement request (ORS 247.967) — [ORS 247.967](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors247.html).
- **DOJ:** Secretary Tobias Read's Aug 21, 2025 letter said no federal law compels production of state rolls, that the Civil Rights Act of 1960 covers registration applications rather than the whole list, and that Oregon would produce the public portions (residence address and birth year, among others) only if DOJ followed OAR 165-002-0020, paid the ORS 247.945 fee, and published a Privacy Act System of Records Notice — [Read letter, Aug 21 2025](https://www.democracydocket.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/OR-voter-rolls-response.pdf). DOJ sued, and the suit was dismissed — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/). DOJ's appeal was argued at the 9th Circuit in May 2026 and was undecided as of Sept 22, 2026 — [Democracy Docket, Sep 22 2026](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/).

### Inferences
- Oregon publishes near-real-time mail-ballot return status during each election. This is among the most granular participation disclosures in the region and is directly relevant to whether Korea should publish participation data during voting.

### Gaps
- Penalty for commercial misuse under ORS 247.955 (for example, an ORS 260 provision): not found.

## Utah

### Takeaway
Utah reversed course. Under the law in force from 2023 to 2026, voters could make their records "private" for any reason, or "withheld" if at risk, and only "qualified persons" could get the list. More than a million records went private. **S.B. 153 (2026)**, signed by Gov. Cox in March 2026, made most private and withheld records public again on **May 25, 2026**. Only verified "at-risk" voters stay protected. The list, including name, voter ID, addresses, party, status, and elections voted, costs $1,050. Recipients may not publish it online, and violation is a class A misdemeanor.

### Cited Findings
- **2023 regime:** the § 20A-2-104 version effective May 3, 2023 (superseded May 7, 2025) let a person ask that identifying information be withheld, required the record to be classified private on written application, on the registration form, or through a withholding request, and released the list only to "qualified persons" after identity verification and a signed attestation. Year of birth was released only to verified qualified persons — [Utah Code § 20A-2-104 (eff. 5/3/2023) [OLD]](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title20A/Chapter2/C20A-2-S104_2023050320230503.pdf). The 2025 version (effective 5/7/2025) was superseded on 4/6/2026 — [Utah Code § 20A-2-104 (eff. 5/7/2025)](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title20A/Chapter2/C20A-2-S104_2025050720250507.pdf).
- **Scale before S.B. 153:** just over 1 million private records, about 1 million public, and 295,000 withheld — [KSL, Apr 14 2026](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public). The Lt. Governor mailed letters to "more than 300,000 voters" whose status was about to change — [Utah Lt. Gov. news release, Apr 2 2026](https://millardcounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NEWS-RELEASE_-Voter-registration-records-privacy-legislation-go-into-effect.pdf). The two figures conflict, and the source does not explain the difference.
- **S.B. 153 (2026):** passed "earlier this year" and signed by Gov. Cox in March 2026 — [KSL, Apr 14 2026](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public); [SUU News, Apr 16 2026](https://suunews.net/2026/04/16/your-voting-history-will-become-public-this-may/). On May 25, 2026 "the records of most of the voters who are currently classified as 'private' or 'withheld' will become public" — [Lt. Gov. release](https://millardcounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NEWS-RELEASE_-Voter-registration-records-privacy-legislation-go-into-effect.pdf). One sponsor-side rationale was that the old privacy law conflicted with federal law — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public). Sources disagree on the sponsor (Sen. John Johnson per KSL; Sen. Trevor Lee per [TownLift](https://townlift.com/2026/04/some-utah-voters-weigh-unregistering-because-of-new-law-lifting-certain-privacy-protections/)).
- **Public fields now:** full legal name, voter identification number, residential and mailing address, voting precinct and districts, party affiliation, active or inactive status, last date the record was updated, and the list of elections in which the voter cast a ballot — [Lt. Gov. release](https://millardcounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NEWS-RELEASE_-Voter-registration-records-privacy-legislation-go-into-effect.pdf). The data also shows whether the voter voted by mail or in person — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public).
- **Withheld:** full DOB, DL number, state ID number, and SSN, which are available only to authorized government entities — [Lt. Gov. release](https://millardcounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NEWS-RELEASE_-Voter-registration-records-privacy-legislation-go-into-effect.pdf).
- **Who, cost, and penalty:** "anyone wanting the statewide rolls must pay a $1,050 fee." Recipients must protect the data and cannot publish it online or on social media. Violations are a class A misdemeanor (up to 364 days in jail and a $2,500 fine) — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public). Reports from specific elections cost $35 — [KPCW, Apr 15 2026](https://www.kpcw.org/state-regional/2026-04-15/utah-voters-info-will-soon-be-available-to-anyone-with-1-050).
- **At-risk designation:** available to a voter who is, or lives with, a victim or threatened victim of domestic or intimate-partner violence, a law enforcement officer, a member of the armed forces, a public figure who has received threats, or a person protected by a court order. Applications went to the county clerk by May 6, 2026 — [Lt. Gov. release](https://millardcounty.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/NEWS-RELEASE_-Voter-registration-records-privacy-legislation-go-into-effect.pdf).
- **Reaction:** some voters considered unregistering — [TownLift](https://townlift.com/2026/04/some-utah-voters-weigh-unregistering-because-of-new-law-lifting-certain-privacy-protections/). Critics said the law puts vulnerable residents at risk — [KPCW](https://www.kpcw.org/state-regional/2026-04-15/utah-voters-info-will-soon-be-available-to-anyone-with-1-050).
- **DOJ:** DOJ sued for the complete list including sensitive data. Lt. Gov. Henderson maintained that DOJ is not entitled to it, offered the public list, and officials said the privacy changes were unrelated to the federal demand — [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51483396/elections-officials-explain-why-utah-voter-information-is-becoming-public). The case was still pending as of Sept 22, 2026 — [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/) — and is listed as rejected as of Nov 10, 2026 — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/).

### Inferences
- Utah is the region's natural experiment. When opting out was free and required no reason, about half the electorate chose privacy. The legislature then replaced the general opt-out with a narrow, category-based at-risk exemption. A general opt-out can therefore hollow out a public roll, while a narrow, verified exemption keeps the list largely complete.

### Gaps
- Whether S.B. 153 kept the "qualified person" attestation requirement or truly opened the list to anyone: reports say "anyone" but the statute text was not read.
- Any post-May 2026 litigation over S.B. 153, and the date of the DOJ dismissal: not found.

## Washington

### Takeaway
Washington is the only state in the region that gives the full statewide file (VRDB extract) to anyone **free**, as a download emailed after approval and a click-through agreement. The data includes name, address, gender, year of birth, registration date, registration number, and voting history. Full DOB was replaced by year of birth in 2023. Commercial use is prohibited. Washington gave DOJ only public fields, and DOJ's suit was dismissed with prejudice in September 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Public fields (RCW 29A.08.710(2)(a)):** "name, address, political jurisdiction, gender, year of birth, voting record, date of registration, and registration number." No other information from registration records is available for inspection or copying. Original registration forms are confidential. The section was last amended by 2023 c 466 and 2023 c 361 — [RCW 29A.08.710](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29A.08.710). The SB 5112 (2023) bill report states that "a voter's year of birth, rather than date of birth" is disclosable — [SB 5112 final bill report, 2023](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Biennium/2023-24/Htm/Bill%20Reports/Senate/5112-S2.E%20SBR%20FBR%2023.htm).
- **Withheld:** SSN4, DL number, full DOB — [WA SOS press release, Sep 23 2025](https://www.sos.wa.gov/node/29984). Personally identifiable information of under-18 registrants is exempt (RCW 29A.08.725; WAC 434-324) — [search summary of WAC 434-232-040 (2023)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/WACArchive/2023/htm/WAC%20434%20%20TITLE/WAC%20434%20-232%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20434%20-232%20-040.htm).
- **Who, cost, and format:** extracts are "available free of charge" — [Kittitas County form](https://www.co.kittitas.wa.us/uploads/documents/auditor/forms/ElectionDataRequest.form.pdf). The request is made on the SOS VRDB extract page, the requester agrees "to use the data in accordance with the laws listed above," and a download link is emailed after approval — [WA SOS extract page (search summary)](https://sos.wa.gov/node/10458). The extract and the Voting History Extract are pipe-delimited text files, updated on the first business day of each month — [VRDB field guide (Sep 2023)](https://www.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/vrdb-database-fields.pdf). Lists go to "any person, upon request" at actual reproduction cost — [WAC 434-324-130 (2024)](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/WACArchive/2024/htm/WAC%20434%20-324%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20434%20-324%20-130.htm).
- **Participation history:** the VRDB began collecting voting dates in 2006, and earlier history from counties varies — [VRDB field guide](https://www.sos.wa.gov/_assets/elections/vrdb-database-fields.pdf). One county says history for all elections since 2000 is available — [Lewis County](https://elections.lewiscountywa.gov/votewa-data-requests/).
- **Use restrictions (RCW 29A.08.720/.740, RCW 42.56.070(8)):** the data may not be used to mail or deliver advertisements, offers, or solicitations for money, services, or anything of value, though "may be used for any political purpose." Recipients must take reasonable precautions against commercial use, and a signed request with the penalty terms may be required — [RCW 29A.08.720](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/RCWPDF/RCW%20%2029A%20TITLE/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2029A.%2008%20.720.pdf); [WAC 434-324-130](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/WACArchive/2024/htm/WAC%20434%20-324%20%20CHAPTER/WAC%20434%20-324%20-130.htm).
- **Protections:** the Address Confidentiality Program has a voter-registration component — [WA SOS ACP navigation, "Voter Registration – ACP"](https://www.sos.wa.gov/elections/data-research/reports-data-and-statistics/washington-state-voter-registration-database-vrdb).
- **DOJ:** DOJ's Sept 8, 2025 letter sought all fields — [DOJ letter (Brennan)](https://www.brennancenter.org/media/14395/download/washington_09.08.2025_doj-letter.pdf?inline=1). On Sept 23, 2025 Secretary Hobbs provided only the publicly disclosable fields (name, address, year of birth, voting history) and cited Privacy Act concerns about a national database — [WA SOS press release](https://www.sos.wa.gov/node/29984). DOJ sued in December 2025 (United States v. Hobbs). On Sept 22, 2026 Judge Kymberly Evanson dismissed with prejudice, holding the demand "violates federal law" — [Democracy Docket, Sep 22 2026](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/); [OPB, Sep 23 2026](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/09/23/trump-administration-washington-voter-roll-lawsuit-dismissed/); [Order (Brennan)](https://www.brennancenter.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/096-order-granting-wa-mtd-2026-09-22.pdf?inline=1).

### Inferences
- Washington has the lowest barrier in the region (free, approved by click-through). The 2023 switch from full DOB to birth year shows that open states reduce identity-theft risk by trimming fields rather than restricting who may ask.

### Gaps

- Whether the extract includes phone or email: RCW 29A.08.710 does not list them, which implies they are not public, but this was not confirmed in the field guide.

## Wyoming

### Takeaway
The list is restricted by requester. The SOS furnishes "registry lists" only to candidates, campaign committees, party central committees, elected officials, PACs, ballot-issue proponents and opponents, and voter-participation organizations, under oath and with proof of eligibility, for political purposes only. Public fields are limited to name, gender, addresses, party, and districts. Wyoming nonetheless gave DOJ its complete list, including DL numbers and partial SSNs.

### Cited Findings
- **Who (W.S. 22-2-113(a)):** the SOS "shall furnish at a reasonable price registry lists to any candidate for a political office in the state, candidate's campaign committee, political party central committees and officials thereof, elected officials, political action committees, individuals promoting or opposing a ballot issue or candidate and to organizations which promote voter participation." County clerks may also furnish lists, equally to all. "All lists are for political purposes only and are not available for commercial use" — [WY Legislature interim committee packet, Aug 2022 [OLD]](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf).
- **Oath and evidence:** the request form requires a signed oath selecting the requester's category, plus documentation such as PAC formation papers. It warns that the "data can ONLY be used for political purposes, not for commercial use" — [same packet / SOS form [OLD]](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf); [SOS Voter Product Order Form](https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/Elections/General/VoterProductOrderForm.pdf).
- **Fields:** last, first, and middle name and suffix, residence address, county, precinct, split, mailing address, political party, House district, Senate district — [SOS form in 2022 packet [OLD]](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf). The "registry list" definition (W.S. 22-1-102(a)(xxix)) covers names, addresses, party affiliations, and precinct and district numbers — [same](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf).
- **Confidential (W.S. 22-2-113(d)):** records containing SSN or partial SSN, DL numbers, birth dates, telephone numbers, tribal ID numbers, email addresses, and "other personally identifiable information other than names, gender, addresses and party affiliations are not public records" — [same](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf).
- **Cost and format:** statewide $125, party $50, county $35, legislative district $25, paid in advance, delivered by email, FTP, or Dropbox — [2022 form [OLD]](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf). EAC listed $0 — [EAC 2020 [OLD]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf).
- **Participation and mail-ballot data:** the registry-list definition does not include voting history — [2022 packet [OLD]](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2022/07-202208252-04SOSElectionlawsrelatingtopublicandconfidentialrecords.pdf). The SOS has a separate "Daily Absentee File Request Form" — [SOS form](https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/Elections/General/DailyAbsenteeFileRequestForm.pdf) (contents not read).
- **DOJ:** Wyoming responded on July 24, 2025 — [WY response (Brennan)](https://www.brennancenter.org/media/14548/download/wyoming_07.24.2025_state-response.pdf?inline=1). It "provided a complete list, including driver's license numbers and partial SSNs" — [Brennan Center tracker](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information). It is listed among the states that fully complied — [Ohio Capital Journal, Apr 6 2026](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/04/06/the-department-of-justice-is-suing-states-for-sensitive-voter-data-%E2%88%92-an-election-law-scholar-explains/). It was not sued.

### Inferences
- Wyoming illustrates an asymmetry: strict limits on the public, but no barrier to handing the most sensitive identifiers to the federal government. Restricting access by requester class does not by itself protect data from government re-use.

## Cross-state comparison, DOJ 2025–26 outcomes, and summary table

### Takeaway
The 13 Western states span the full US range. **Fully open** states sell or give the list to anyone (AK, CO, ID, MT, NV, OR, WA, and UT after 2026), usually with a commercial-use ban. **Purpose-restricted** states sell to anyone but only for election, political, scholarly, journalistic, or government uses, often with felony penalties and online-posting bans (AZ, CA, NM). **Requester-restricted** states sell only to campaigns, parties, and similar actors (WY), and Hawaii goes further by making even addresses non-public except for election or government purposes. Participation history is widely released; WY and HI are the exceptions, and Alaska is unclear. Several states release mail-ballot status in near real time (OR daily ballot status; AZ daily early-ballot lists to parties; MT absentee reports; ID absentee lists). No Western state other than Washington offers a free download to the general public, and none posts the list openly on a public website.

### Cited Findings
- **DOJ litigation tally:** 31 suits (30 states plus DC). 28 were dismissed or rejected, 2 are pending (Hawaii and Idaho), and 1 was voluntarily dismissed (Oklahoma). Western states sued and rejected: AZ, CA, CO, NV, NM, OR, UT, WA — [CBS News, updated Nov 10 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-lawsuits-state-voter-rolls-justice-department/). As of Sept 22, 2026 DOJ was 0-for-25 in district courts, with the CA and OR appeals argued at the 9th Circuit in May 2026 and ID and HI stayed pending them — [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/washington-state-hands-trump-doj-its-25th-straight-voter-roll-loss/). The Washington Times describes DOJ as "0-for-28" — [Washington Times, Nov 12 2026](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/nov/12/shutout-justice-department-goes-0-28-noncitizen-voter-list-lawsuits/).
- **States that gave DOJ sensitive data:** Alaska (unredacted, under a confidential MOU) and Wyoming (complete, with DL and partial SSN). ### Inferences
- **For the Korea question:**
  1. Even the most open Western states publish a *file you must request*, not a searchable public website. Washington comes closest, with a free download after a click-through agreement.
  2. Nearly every state, open or restricted, withholds national ID numbers (SSN/DL), signatures, and full DOB. Nevada and California are the DOB exceptions, and California releases DOB only to vetted requesters.
  3. Republication online is the live fault line. Arizona and Utah criminalize it and New Mexico tried to, but the NVRA has been held to preempt New Mexico's ban, while Idaho tolerates it.
  4. Opt-out design matters. Utah's no-reason opt-out drew roughly half the electorate before being repealed, whereas narrow verified categories (Utah 2026, Arizona, California) or self-certified risk (Colorado's $5) keep lists usable.
  5. Requester restrictions alone do not prevent sensitive data reaching the central government (Wyoming, Alaska).
- Real-time mail-ballot status lists (Oregon, Arizona, Idaho, Montana) are a distinct category from the roll itself and carry their own risks (targeting, harassment) and benefits (campaign chase, error correction).
