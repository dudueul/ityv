# Public access to statewide voter files in the US Midwest (IL, IN, IA, KS, MI, MN, MO, NE, ND, OH, SD, WI) — status as of November 2026

Research notes. Prepared 2026-11-12. Primary sources (state statutes, official forms and pages, official letters) were preferred. Items dated before 2024 are marked **[pre-2024, possibly outdated]**. Where a primary page could not be retrieved (bot-blocked: NCSL, Ohio SOS, Iowa SOS, Illinois General Assembly were blocked or down during this session), this is said explicitly and the secondary source used is named.

Cross-cutting compilation used for comparison: U.S. EAC, "Availability of State Voter File and Confidential Information" (created Oct. 30, 2020; sources: NCSL, US Elections Project, state officials) **[pre-2024, possibly outdated]** — https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf. It rates IL "Restricted, $500", IN "Open, $0", IA "Mixed, $1,100", KS "Open, $200", MI "Open, $23", MN "Restricted, $51", MO "Open, $35", NE "Mixed, $500", ND "Restricted, $2,120", OH "Open, $0", SD "Mixed, $2,500", WI "Open, $12,500". Several of these entries are now out of date (SD, NE, MO, KS prices/fields have changed — see below).

## 1. Who may obtain the statewide list, and how (free download, sale, or request only)?

### Takeaway
The 12 states span the full spectrum. **Open to anyone, free download:** Ohio (weekly) and, since 2025, South Dakota (weekly, behind a short online form). **Open to anyone, paid:** Wisconsin (no use limits at all), Michigan (FOIA), Kansas, Missouri, Iowa, Nebraska (sworn oath). **Registered voters only:** Minnesota. **Political actors/government only:** Illinois (registered political committees + government), Indiana (full file only to qualifying parties/independent candidates; others get a redacted copy), North Dakota (no voter registration; the "central voter file" is an exempt record available only to candidates, parties and political committees).

### Cited Findings

**Ohio (OH)**
- Ohio publishes the statewide voter file for free, immediate download, updated weekly; Demos scored Ohio 100% on "data accessibility": "The complete file is fully available to the public, updated weekly, and can be immediately downloaded in an electronic format for free," citing the SOS "Voter Files Download Page" (https://www6.ohiosos.gov/ords/f?p=VOTERFTP:STWD:::#stwdVtrFiles) — [Demos, Ohio excerpt, Nov. 2023 [pre-2024]](https://www.demos.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Ohio%20-%20Excerpt%20-%20Protecting%20Voter%20Registration_1.pdf)
- Files are downloadable as county-range .txt.gz files without login (tutorial demonstrating scripted download) — [r4campaigns tutorial, July 2019 [pre-2024]](https://r4campaigns.netlify.app/state-voter-files)
- A 2025 SOS press release (seen only as a search snippet; ohiosos.gov blocked automated access) states Ohio publishes its full Statewide Voter Registration Database online for free and updates it weekly — [Ohio SOS press release 2025-09-17 (snippet)](https://www.ohiosos.gov/office/media-center/categories/press-releases/2025-09-17)
- Separately, under the 2023 DATA Act, Ohio launched (Feb. 2025) a portal at OhioSoS.gov/Data giving "every Ohioan" daily voter-registration change records (new registrations, updates, removals) from all 88 counties — [Highland County Press, Feb. 4, 2025](https://www.highlandcountypress.com/news/larose-expands-election-transparency-initiative)
- ORC 3503.15 (current version eff. Oct. 3, 2023) establishes the database but contains no public-access clause itself (access is via public-records law and SOS practice) — [ORC 3503.15](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3503.15)

**South Dakota (SD)**
- Since 2025, "The secretary of state shall post the complete statewide voter registration file to the secretary's website before 11:59 p.m. central time each Monday" (SDCL 12-4-37, amended SL 2025 ch 60; SL 2026 ch 66) — [SDCL 12-4-37](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-37.html)
- The SOS must also keep a rolling five-year historical archive of the full file, posted weekly, "made available free of charge" (SDCL 12-4-37.1, new in SL 2025 ch 60) — [SDCL 12-4-37.1](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-37.1.html)
- Implementation: requesters fill an online form with contact information and acknowledge SDCL 12-4-9 and 12-4-41; submission generates a one-time link expiring after 12 hours. AG Official Opinion 25-02 (Aug. 25, 2025) held this harmonizes the weekly-posting mandate with 12-4-41's ban on "unrestricted access on the internet" — [SD AG Op. 25-02](https://atg.sd.gov/OurOffice/OfficialOpinions/opinionhtml.aspx?id=1768); [Dakota Scout, Aug. 25, 2025](https://www.thedakotascout.com/p/jackley-disclosing-voter-registration)
- The county master registration file "must be open to public inspection, free of charge" (SDCL 12-4-9) — [SDCL 12-4-9](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-9.html)
- Contested: the SD Freedom Caucus argued the form-based access violates legislative intent (advocacy source) — [SD Freedom Caucus, July 2025](https://sdfreedomcaucus.com/?p=246339)

**Wisconsin (WI)**
- Anyone may buy voter and absentee data through the WEC "Badger Voters" site; "Wisconsin law does not place any restrictions on the use of data obtained through this website" — [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq)
- The official registration list "is open to public inspection" and electronically accessible, with certain fields restricted (Wis. Stat. 6.36(1)(b)) — [Wis. Stat. 6.36](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/6/II/36)
- WEC says the law requires a fee and "makes no exceptions for journalists, non-profits, academics, or any other group" (2024 WEC letter, via search snippet) — [WEC letter in PILF filing, 2024](https://publicinterestlegal.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/1-2.pdf)

**Michigan (MI)**
- The Qualified Voter File (QVF) "can typically be obtained under the Michigan Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) by submitting a Qualified Voter File Data Request Form" — [MDOS letter to DOJ, Sept. 9, 2025](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/MISOS/2025/09/25/file_attachments/3400391/2025.09.09%20MDOS%20DOJ%20response.pdf)
- Michigan "has no restrictions on who can purchase this data… The only caveat is that it cannot be used commercially" — [FOX 2 Detroit, Nov. 4, 2022 [pre-2024]](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/how-is-the-center-for-voter-information-getting-your-information)
- Not available for immediate download; timeline unclear — [Demos, Michigan excerpt, 2023 [pre-2024]](https://www.demos.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Michigan%20-%20Excerpt%20-%20Protecting%20Voter%20Registration.pdf)

**Kansas (KS)**
- County election officers must allow "any person" to examine registration books and must provide copies to "the person so requesting" at uniform actual cost (K.S.A. 25-2320(a)) — [K.S.A. 25-2320](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li_2024/m/statute/025_000_0000_chapter/025_023_0000_article/025_023_0020_section/025_023_0020_k.pdf)
- Statewide data are sold by the SOS on Form CVR (Rev. 3/2/26), with no requester-category limit; requester certifies "a right of access to the records" — [KS SOS Form CVR](https://sos.ks.gov/forms/elections/CVR.pdf)

**Missouri (MO)**
- "The election authority or secretary of state shall make available electronic media or printouts showing only unique voter identification numbers, voters' names, year of birth, addresses, townships or wards, and precincts"; information is made available "pursuant to chapter 610" (Sunshine Law) (RSMo 115.157.1, .5; version eff. Aug. 28, 2022) — [RSMo 115.157](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.157)

**Iowa (IA)**
- "Any person may request of the registrar and shall receive, upon payment of the cost of preparation, a list of registered voters and other data on registration and participation in elections" (Iowa Code 48A.38(1)) — [Iowa Code 48A.38 (2025)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/48A.38.pdf)
- Statewide and congressional-district lists must be obtained from the SOS (SOS page, seen via search snippet; page bot-blocked) — [Iowa SOS voter list requests](https://sos.iowa.gov/elections/voterreg/voterlistrequests.html)

**Nebraska (NE)**
- SOS/county officials "shall make available a list of registered voters"; any requester must give name, address, phone, email, organization and "the reason for requesting the list" and swear an oath (Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-330(2),(4)) — [Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-330](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-330)
- The full register may be examined by any person at the county office, but only election officials/law enforcement may copy it (32-330(1)) — same source
- Free monthly copies to state party HQs and county party chairs (32-330(6)) — same source

**Minnesota (MN)**
- Copies of public information lists go "to any voter registered in Minnesota" (Minn. Stat. 201.091 subd. 5); SOS page: the information "is only available to registered Minnesota voters" — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091); [MN SOS list requests page](https://www.sos.mn.gov/election-administration-campaigns/data-maps/registered-voter-list-requests/)
- Requester must show ID and state in writing the data will not be used for purposes unrelated to elections, political activities or law enforcement (subd. 4(c)); public inspection is free (subd. 5) — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091)

**Illinois (IL)**
- The SBE statewide file is sold only to registered political committees and to "a governmental entity for a governmental purpose" (10 ILCS 5/1A-25(b), as described by SBE in its Aug. 2025 letter) — [Capitol Fax, Aug. 12, 2025](https://capitolfax.com/2025/08/12/citing-applicable-laws-the-state-board-of-elections-refuses-to-fully-comply-with-federal-demand-for-sensitive-voter-information)
- County level: "Units of government or political committees, who are registered with the State Board of Elections, may purchase voter registration lists and associated voter history in electronic form for bona fide political purposes" — [Lake County Clerk](https://www.lakecountyil.gov/314/List-of-Registered-Voters-Voting-History)
- SBE says a "public version" exists that is similar but shows only street names (no house numbers); who can obtain it and how was not found — [Capitol Fax, Aug. 12, 2025](https://capitolfax.com/2025/08/12/citing-applicable-laws-the-state-board-of-elections-refuses-to-fully-comply-with-federal-demand-for-sensitive-voter-information)

**Indiana (IN)**
- Only certain entities (e.g., state major-party committees) may buy an annual subscription to the "unrestricted voter registration file"; "All other persons may obtain a restricted copy of the list, after certain information such as date of birth and gender, has been redacted by the Division" — [Indiana 2026 Voter Registration Guidebook (rev. Apr. 2026), p. 93–94](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)
- Eligible for the complete list (IC 3-7-26.4-6): state committee of a major party; a bona fide minor party with ≥2 candidates on the next ballot; committee of an independent candidate for federal/state office on the next general-election ballot (summarized from statute text in search results) — [IC 3-7-26.4-6 (Justia 2023)](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-3/article-7/chapter-26-4/section-3-7-26-4-6)
- County data from SVRS: request on State Form VRG-24; each county election board adopts a "non-discriminatory uniform policy" either permitting or refusing electronic copies (IC 3-7-27-6(c)) — [Indiana 2026 Guidebook, p. 93](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)
- A 2017 complaint described the redacted list (name, address, congressional district) as available to media and any person for non-commercial purposes **[pre-2024]** — [LWV Indiana v. Lawson complaint (EPIC)](https://EPIC.ORG/privacy/litigation/voter/epic-v-commission/LWV-Indiana-v-Lawson-complaint.pdf)

**North Dakota (ND)**
- ND has no voter registration; the SOS maintains a "permanent central voter file" (NDCC ch. 16.1-02) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)
- "A voter list or a report generated from the central voter file may be made available to a candidate, political party, or a political committee for election-related purposes… information in the central voter file is an exempt record" (NDCC 16.1-02-15) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)
- SOS told a 2021 public requester the file "is only available to Candidates, Political Parties, or Political Action Committees" **[pre-2024]** — [MuckRock request](https://www.muckrock.com/foi/united-states-of-america-10/north-dakota-voter-registration-file-108461/)
- But precinct pollbooks are open records (after redaction), and on request officials must generate from e-pollbooks a list of "the individuals who voted in the election," by precinct or county (NDCC 16.1-02-13) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rough typology for cross-state comparison: (A) free public download — OH, SD; (B) sale to anyone — WI (no use limits), MI, KS, MO, IA, NE (use-limited); (C) registered voters only — MN; (D) political/government users only — IL, IN (full file), ND. Indiana is hybrid: redacted list to others, full list to parties.
- South Dakota is the main 2020–2026 movement toward openness in this region (2025 law), while Minnesota and Nebraska moved toward tighter use/online-posting restrictions (see Q4/Q7).

### Gaps
- NCSL's "Access to and Use of Voter Registration Lists" page returned HTTP 403; its current state table could not be checked.
- Ohio SOS download page and Iowa SOS page could not be fetched directly (bot protection); current Ohio terms of use, if any, were not found.
- Illinois: who can get the "public version" (street names only) and its price — not found. 10 ILCS 5/1A-25 full text could not be fetched (ilga.gov 503 / TLS errors).
- Indiana: price and terms of the "restricted copy" for non-subscribers — not found.

## 2. Which fields are included, and which are withheld?

### Takeaway
Full date of birth is public in Ohio and Iowa (and appears to be in Kansas's sold file); South Dakota, Michigan, Missouri, Minnesota and Nebraska release birth year only; Illinois releases age; Wisconsin releases no age data at all. Every state withholds SSN digits and driver's licence/state ID numbers. Party appears in OH, IA, KS, NE, MO (field exists), IL (primary ballot choice in history); Wisconsin holds no party data (WEC FAQ) and Michigan's published field lists show no party field. No source found showing race/ethnicity in any of these files (absence confirmed for Ohio and Wisconsin).

### Cited Findings

**Ohio** — Sample file columns: SOS_VOTERID, COUNTY_NUMBER, COUNTY_ID, LAST/FIRST/MIDDLE_NAME, SUFFIX, DATE_OF_BIRTH (full, e.g., "1969-11-15"), REGISTRATION_DATE, VOTER_STATUS, PARTY_AFFILIATION, residential and mailing address fields, precinct name/code, and ~20 district fields (congressional, state house/senate, school, court, ward, township, village), 106 columns total — [r4campaigns tutorial, 2019 [pre-2024]](https://r4campaigns.netlify.app/state-voter-files). File includes voter ID number and status (confirmation notice) but no race (Ohio does not collect it) — [Demos Ohio, 2023 [pre-2024]](https://www.demos.org/sites/default/files/2023-11/Ohio%20-%20Excerpt%20-%20Protecting%20Voter%20Registration_1.pdf). SOS spokesperson (2026): "most of Ohio's voter registration data is already publicly available" (DL, DOB… SSN4 sent only to DOJ) — [Dayton Daily News via Yahoo, Mar. 9, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ohio-sends-data-7-9m-183200592.html). Note: DOB is described as public by the tutorial's sample output; DL/SSN4 are not in the public file.

**South Dakota** — Master file contains all information from the registration card, date last voted, date last updated, and absentee application details (SDCL 12-4-9). Not open to inspection: SSN, DL number, SD nondriver ID number, "month and day of birth" (so birth year is public) — [SDCL 12-4-9](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-9.html); [SDCL 12-4-37.1](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-37.1.html). EAC 2020 likewise: full DOB withheld "though birth year may be disclosed" — [EAC 2020 [pre-2024]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf)

**Wisconsin** — Standard voter data elements: voter reg number (encrypted customer number), name, phone and email "if on file", residential and mailing address, jurisdiction and ~20 district fields, voter status and status reason (e.g., Movers, 4-Year Maintenance, Deceased, Felon), registration date, application source (Online, Clerk's Office, Mail…), IsPermanentAbsentee, voter type (Regular, Military, Overseas, President Only), and a column per election — [WEC Standard Data Elements](https://BADGERVoters.wi.gov/img/Voter%20Data%20Request%20Data%20Elements.pdf). "The WEC will never provide date of birth information (including age or age range), driver license numbers or social security numbers"; WEC has no party, demographic, or vote-choice data — [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq). Statute limits viewing of DOB, DL, SSN, confidential-elector addresses and accommodation indications to election officials (6.36(1)(b)) — [Wis. Stat. 6.36](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/6/II/36)

**Michigan** — Exempt from FOIA (MCL 168.509gg, am. 2023 PA 258, eff. Feb. 13, 2024): record of declining to register; office receiving the application; DL/state ID number; "month and day of birth"; telephone number; digitized signature; last 4 SSN digits — [MCL 168.509gg](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-168-509GG.pdf). Available fields: name, residential and mailing address, birth year, registration date, voter status, voter ID, voting history — [FOX 2 Detroit, 2022 [pre-2024]](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/how-is-the-center-for-voter-information-getting-your-information)

**Kansas** — SSN, DL, nondriver ID "or any part thereof" must be removed (K.S.A. 25-2320(b)) — [K.S.A. 25-2320](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li_2024/m/statute/025_000_0000_chapter/025_023_0000_article/025_023_0020_section/025_023_0020_k.pdf). AG Opinion 2017-10: SOS may not release SSN4 or residence address of voters who requested concealment, but "may, in his (or her) discretion, release records containing the full name, date of birth, and party affiliation" (via search snippet) **[pre-2024]** — [KS AG Op. 2017-10 (ebench)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/kansas/opinions/250156301-ago-2017-10-july-11-2017). A 2025 analysis of the May 2, 2025 state file computed "voter ages… from date of birth in the voter file" and used registration dates and district/precinct fields — [Watchdog Lab, June 10, 2025](https://watchdoglab.substack.com/p/voter-registration-records-say-oldest). Party selection on the order form: Democratic, Republican, Libertarian, United Kansas, No Labels Kansas, Unaffiliated — [KS Form CVR](https://sos.ks.gov/forms/elections/CVR.pdf)

**Missouri** — Public fields "only": voter ID number, name, year of birth, address, township/ward, precinct; electronic fields include senatorial, representative, congressional district and "political party affiliation" (RSMo 115.157.1) — [RSMo 115.157](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.157). Conflict: EAC 2020 listed Missouri as releasing "DOB" — [EAC 2020 [pre-2024]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf); current statute (eff. Aug. 28, 2022) says year of birth.

**Iowa** — Each list contains "the registration data specified by the list purchaser" (48A.38(1)(c)); registrar must remove whole/partial SSN, state-assigned voter ID number, Iowa DL and nonoperator ID numbers (48A.38(1)(f)); inactive records excluded unless requested (48A.38(1)(d)) — [Iowa Code 48A.38](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/48A.38.pdf). SOS spokesperson: data "typically made available to the public includes name, date of birth, party affiliation and address" — [Iowa Public Radio, May 5, 2026](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/state-government-news/2026-05-05/iowa-shares-voter-data-with-justice-department)

**Nebraska** — List may contain "no more than": name; residential address; mailing address; telephone number; status; voter ID number; birth year; registration date; precinct; polling site; party affiliation; political subdivisions; voter history (32-330(3)(b)). Withheld: anything confidential under 32-331 or "marked private on the voter registration application"; digital signature (32-330(1),(3)(a)) — [Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-330](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-330). EAC 2020 had described Nebraska lists as including DOB and DL/SSN4 — that description conflicts with the current statute **[pre-2024]** — [EAC 2020](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf)

**Minnesota** — Public information list: name, address, year of birth, voting history; phone "if provided"; may include voting districts; must not include party choice in presidential nomination primary (subd. 4(a), 4a). Must not include DOB or any part of SSN, DL, ID card, military ID or passport number (subd. 9) — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091). Products: Walking List (name, address, phone, birth year, 6-year summary history); Detailed Voting History (adds voter ID, registration date, county/MCD/precinct/school district codes, detailed history, permanent absentee status) — [MN SOS](https://www.sos.mn.gov/election-administration-campaigns/data-maps/registered-voter-list-requests/)

**Illinois** — Committee/government list: names and addresses, "age when the file is created (not date of birth)", voting jurisdictions, voting history including the party ballot selected in primaries; withheld: SSN, DL/state ID, DOB — [Capitol Fax, Aug. 12, 2025](https://capitolfax.com/2025/08/12/citing-applicable-laws-the-state-board-of-elections-refuses-to-fully-comply-with-federal-demand-for-sensitive-voter-information). DOB removed from committee files in January 2018 — [Capitol Fax, Apr. 5, 2024](https://capitolfax.com/2024/04/05/state-board-of-elections-asks-proft-papers-to-take-down-personal-data-from-websites)

**Indiana** — SVRS includes gender (IC 3-7-26.4-8). Restricted copies/reports exclude date of birth, gender, telephone number, voting history, and registration date unless the recipient has paid for the annual subscription (IC 3-7-26.3-29; 3-7-26.4-4, -8). Full SSN in a record must be redacted to the last four digits for public-records requests (IC 5-14-3-4(a)(12)) — [Indiana 2026 Guidebook, pp. 44, 93–94](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)

**North Dakota** — Central voter file holds name, residential and mailing address, unique ID, status, county/legislative/city/school/commissioner districts and precinct, four years of voting history, DOB, DL/nondriver ID or tribal ID (16.1-02-12). Only items 1–7 and 10 (i.e., not DOB, not ID numbers) may go to candidates/parties/committees; "secured active" records are fully exempt (16.1-02-15) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)

### Inferences
- On date of birth, Ohio and Iowa are outliers (full DOB to any buyer/downloader). The regional norm is birth year (SD, MI, MO, MN, NE) or age (IL) or nothing (WI, ND). Several states narrowed DOB release over time (IL 2018; MI exempts month/day; NE and MO statutes now say birth year where EAC 2020 had reported DOB).
- Phone numbers are released in WI, MN, NE (unless marked private) but exempt in MI; email is released in WI "if on file".

### Gaps
- Gender: included in Kansas or Ohio public files? Not confirmed (Ohio sample columns show no gender field; Indiana excludes it from restricted lists).
- Ohio's current (2026) file layout was not retrievable; field list relies on a 2019 tutorial.
- Iowa: full list of selectable fields on the current order form — not retrievable (403).

## 3. Is voter participation history included (which elections, method)? Is there a separate absentee list?

### Takeaway
Participation history (whether a person voted, election by election) is public in every Midwest state except via Indiana's restricted copy. Method of voting is shown in Wisconsin (polls/absentee/provisional) and partly in Minnesota (permanent absentee status) and South Dakota (absentee method on the master file); Ohio and Illinois show which party's primary ballot was taken. Separate absentee/advance-voting lists are sold in WI, IA, KS, MN and (to candidates/committees only) MO.

### Cited Findings
- **Ohio:** one column per election since 2000 (e.g., "GENERAL-11/07/2000"); "X" marks voted in general elections, a party letter (e.g., "D") marks the primary ballot taken — [r4campaigns, 2019 [pre-2024]](https://r4campaigns.netlify.app/state-voter-files). Boards may release history only for ballots that were counted (OAC 111:3-11-03, via search snippet) — [Ohio Adm. Code 111:3-11-03 (ebench)](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/ohio/regulation/111:3-11-03)
- **Wisconsin:** a column per election (spring primary, spring, partisan primary, general, specials) with codes "At Polls", "Absentee", "PROV" (provisional), "WO" (walk out), "X" (voted, method unknown); history back to 2006 — [WEC Data Elements](https://BADGERVoters.wi.gov/img/Voter%20Data%20Request%20Data%20Elements.pdf); [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq). Separate absentee records: request received date, ballot issued, returned, counted or disqualified "and why"; subscription updates for 365 days — [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq). Statute: registration list records election dates voted and absentee application/mailing/return dates (6.36(1)(a)) — [Wis. Stat. 6.36](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/6/II/36)
- **South Dakota:** master file includes date of the last election voted and each absentee application with mailing address, ballot requested, date requested, date returned, and "method of absentee voting employed" — open to public inspection free (12-4-9) — [SDCL 12-4-9](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-9.html)
- **Minnesota:** walking list = 6-year summary history; detailed history report includes permanent absentee status. Separate products: Absentee Ballot Applicant List, Accepted Absentee Ballot List, Rejected Absentee Ballot List, Early Voting List — [MN SOS](https://www.sos.mn.gov/election-administration-campaigns/data-maps/registered-voter-list-requests/). Presidential-primary party choice is private except to each major party's chair for its own voters (201.091 subd. 4a) — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091)
- **Michigan:** voting history available; requester selects which elections — [FOX 2 Detroit, 2022 [pre-2024]](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/how-is-the-center-for-voter-information-getting-your-information)
- **Iowa:** lists include "data on registration and participation in elections"; participation updated within 60 days after each election (48A.38(1),(2)) — [Iowa Code 48A.38](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/48A.38.pdf). Statewide absentee list with updates $450/year (SOS page via search snippet) — [Iowa SOS](https://sos.iowa.gov/elections/voterreg/voterlistrequests.html)
- **Kansas:** advance-voting lists sold as daily subscription ($300), weekly ($150) or one-time ($50) — [KS Form CVR](https://sos.ks.gov/forms/elections/CVR.pdf). File carries 10 voter-history fields (2024 analysis) — [Watchdog Lab (search snippet)](https://watchdoglab.substack.com/p/does-kansas-have-nearly-320000-registered)
- **Missouri:** election authorities must furnish, for a fee, names, year of birth, addresses and party affiliations of voters "who voted in any specific election, including primary elections" (115.157.3). Absentee-applicant lists (names, addresses, voter ID) are furnished by the SOS only to candidates, campaign committees and party committees (115.157.4) — [RSMo 115.157](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.157)
- **Nebraska:** list includes "voter history," and on request a list limited to registrants who voted in an election held more than 30 days before the request (32-330(2),(3)) — [Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-330](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-330)
- **Illinois:** voting history including party ballot chosen in primaries (committee file) — [Capitol Fax, Aug. 12, 2025](https://capitolfax.com/2025/08/12/citing-applicable-laws-the-state-board-of-elections-refuses-to-fully-comply-with-federal-demand-for-sensitive-voter-information)
- **Indiana:** voting history excluded from reports to non-subscribers (IC 3-7-26.3-29); county policy covers "absentee activity reports" — [Indiana 2026 Guidebook, p. 93](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)
- **North Dakota:** four years of voting history to candidates/parties/committees; anyone may get e-pollbook-generated lists of who voted by precinct/county (16.1-02-12, -13) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)

### Inferences
- In this region "who voted" is treated as public almost everywhere, even where the list itself is restricted (ND pollbooks are open records). Primary-ballot party choice is exposed in OH and IL but deliberately hidden for presidential primaries in MN.

### Gaps
- Ohio, Michigan, Illinois absentee-list availability and terms — not found.
- Whether Ohio's public file shows voting method (absentee vs. in person) — the 2019 sample shows only "X"/party codes; not confirmed for 2026.

## 4. Use restrictions, certification/affidavit requirements, penalties, statute citations

### Takeaway
Most states ban commercial use (KS, MO, IA, SD, NE, MI, IL, IN); Wisconsin and Ohio impose no statutory use limit found. Purpose limits are tightest in MN (elections/political/law enforcement; no internet publication or resale since 2024), NE (sworn oath, no internet, Class IV felony for false oath), IL (sworn affidavit, bona fide political purposes) and ND (election-related only). Penalties range from Class C misdemeanor (KS) to Class B (MO), Class 1 misdemeanor + $2,000 civil penalty per violation (SD), "serious misdemeanor" (IA), Class A misdemeanor (ND), Class IV felony for false oath (NE).

### Cited Findings
- **Kansas:** "Use of voter registration lists for commercial purposes is a class C misdemeanor"; use "solely for political campaign or election purposes, shall not constitute a commercial use" (K.S.A. 25-2320a) — [K.S.A. 25-2320a](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li_2024/m/statute/025_000_0000_chapter/025_023_0000_article/025_023_0020a_section/025_023_0020a_k.pdf). Form CVR notice: commercial use includes "using public records to sell property or services" and obtaining records to give to a third party for such purposes; signature required — [KS Form CVR](https://sos.ks.gov/forms/elections/CVR.pdf)
- **Missouri:** voter registration information "shall not be used for commercial purposes" but may be used "for elections, for candidates, or for ballot measures"; violation is a class B misdemeanor; "commercial purposes" includes resale and obtaining names and addresses for solicitation (RSMo 115.158.6) — [RSMo 115.158](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.158). Also, nothing requires release "to the public over the internet" (115.157.3–.4) — [RSMo 115.157](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.157)
- **Iowa:** information "shall be used only to request the registrant's vote at an election, or for another genuine political purpose, or for a bona fide official purpose by an elected official, or for bona fide political research, but shall not be used for any commercial purposes" (Iowa Code 48A.39) — [Iowa Code 48A.39](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/48A.39.pdf). Signed statement: "using the information for any commercial purpose is a serious misdemeanor under Iowa law"; "political purpose" = "research, advocacy, or education relating to the election, administration, or governance of public policies or officials" (IAC 821—3.2(2), 3.3) — [IAC 821 ch. 3](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/02-18-2026.821.3.pdf). Registrar must log name, address and phone of every list recipient; logs are public records (48A.38(3)) — [Iowa Code 48A.38](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2025/48A.38.pdf)
- **South Dakota:** information "may not be sold and may not be used for any commercial purpose" (campaign or political polling not "commercial"); "may not be placed for unrestricted access on the internet" except the SOS's own posting; Class 1 misdemeanor plus civil penalty up to $2,000 per violation (SDCL 12-4-41, am. SL 2025 ch 60, SL 2026 ch 66) — [SDCL 12-4-41](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-41.html)
- **Nebraska:** lists "shall be used solely for purposes related to elections, political activities, voter registration, law enforcement, or jury selection" and "shall not be posted, displayed, or used for commercial purposes or made accessible on the Internet"; sworn oath not to permit unauthorized use or copying or internet posting, under "penalty of election falsification… a Class IV felony"; recipients must report security breaches (32-330(2),(4),(9)) — [Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-330](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-330)
- **Minnesota:** no use "for purposes unrelated to elections, political activities, or law enforcement"; written statement + ID; recipient must not "publish any of the information from the list on the Internet on any list, database, or other similar searchable format" or "sell, loan, provide access to, or otherwise surrender" it (organizations may share with volunteers/employees for election/political use; aggregated political databases allowed if nothing is published online) (201.091 subd. 4(b),(c),(e)) — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091). Penalty for misuse: not stated on SOS page; statute section not identified in this research.
- **Illinois:** requesting committee's representative "shall make a sworn affidavit that the information shall be used only for bona fide political purposes," and data "shall not be used under any circumstances… for purposes of commercial solicitation or other business purposes" (10 ILCS 5/5-7, text seen via search snippet; similar provisions in 4-8, 6-35) — [10 ILCS 5/5-7](https://ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/001000050K5-7.htm)
- **Indiana:** requester of a statewide compilation must sign an agreement with the Election Division (IC 3-7-26.4-5, -10; summarized from statute in search results). County VRG-24 requester agrees not to use the data "to solicit merchandise, goods, services or subscriptions" and not to "sell, loan, give away or otherwise deliver the information to any other person for a purpose other than political activities or political fund-raising activities" — [Indiana 2026 Guidebook, p. 93](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)
- **North Dakota:** information obtained "may not be sold or distributed for a purpose that is not election-related" (16.1-02-15); intentional violation of the chapter is a class A misdemeanor "unless a different penalty is specifically provided by law" (16.1-02-16(3)) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)
- **Michigan:** cannot be used commercially (news description; statutory cite not located) — [FOX 2 Detroit, 2022 [pre-2024]](https://www.fox2detroit.com/news/how-is-the-center-for-voter-information-getting-your-information)
- **Wisconsin:** "Wisconsin law does not place any restrictions on the use of data obtained through this website" — [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq)
- **Ohio:** no statutory use restriction on the downloadable file was found (ORC 3503.15 has none) — [ORC 3503.15](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3503.15)
- EAC overview: "even the least restrictive states prohibit the commercial use of voter file information," and once data leave the official source "there are no technical limitations on how the data is reused, redistributed, or consolidated" — [EAC 2020 [pre-2024]](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/voters/Available_Voter_File_Information.pdf)

### Inferences
- Internet-republication bans (MN 2024, NE, SD, MO "nothing requires") are the region's main tool against doxxing-style republication; Ohio and Wisconsin rely on none, which is why third-party sites can republish their files.
- Wisconsin's EAC 2020 statement that least-restrictive states still ban commercial use appears inconsistent with WEC's own FAQ (no use restrictions); the WEC FAQ is more current and primary.

### Gaps
- Ohio terms of use (if any) on the download page — not verified.
- Minnesota and Michigan penalty provisions for misuse — not found.
- Indiana penalty for breach of the IC 3-7-26.4 agreement — not found.

## 6. Protections for at-risk voters (address confidentiality, judges, law enforcement) and opt-outs

### Takeaway
Address-confidentiality-program (ACP / "Safe at Home") participants are shielded in every state where this was checked (OH, SD, WI, MO, MN, NE, MI, IN, ND; Kansas by concealment request; Illinois and Iowa not verified). Specific carve-outs for judges and police in the voter file exist in Wisconsin (judicial privacy) and South Dakota (judges and law-enforcement officers, 2026), Missouri (undercover officers, witness protection, protected DV victims via court order) and Minnesota (any voter can withhold their name on a signed safety statement). Indiana explicitly does not extend its officer address-shielding law to the statewide system. Wisconsin offers no opt-out from the public record.

### Cited Findings
- **Ohio:** Safe at Home participants' registration is confidential; residence address/precinct replaced by participant ID number and "no information concerning the program participant, including… name, shall be included in the version of the statewide voter registration database that is available to the public"; participant may vote only absentee (ORC 111.44) — [ORC 111.44](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-111.44)
- **South Dakota:** home address, home/personal phone and personal email of current or retired state/federal judges and law enforcement officers are not open to inspection in the historical archive; city and county of residence remain public (SDCL 12-4-37.1, am. SL 2026 ch 64) — [SDCL 12-4-37.1](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-37.1.html). Exceptions also in 12-4-9.2 and 12-4-9.3 (not reviewed) — [SDCL 12-4-9](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/12-4-9.html)
- **Wisconsin:** "Confidential electors" (victims of domestic abuse, sexual assault, stalking, Wis. Stat. 6.47) and "Protected Voters" (judicial officers and family under 757.07(2)(a)) do not appear in any data request; "There is no provision in the law for removing a voter's data from the public record"; inactivation hides the record only from standard lists — [Badger Voters FAQ](https://badgervoters.wi.gov/faq)
- **Missouri:** undercover officers, witness-protection participants, and DV victims with protection orders may petition circuit court to close their residential address (115.157.6) — [RSMo 115.157](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=115.157)
- **Minnesota:** on a voter's signed statement that withholding "is required for the safety of the voter or the voter's family," SOS and county must withhold the voter's name from the public list (201.091 subd. 4(d)) — [Minn. Stat. 201.091](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/201.091)
- **Nebraska:** a voter may file an affidavit to keep name, residence address and phone confidential if an ACP participant or with a court order finding a "life-threatening circumstance"; such voters vote by mail; officials liable for grossly negligent/willful disclosure (32-331). Voters may also mark phone/email "private" on the application (32-330(3)(a)) — [Neb. Rev. Stat. 32-331](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-331); [32-330](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=32-330)
- **Michigan:** pre-registrants' records confidential until age 17½ (509gg(3), 2023 PA 258); ACP participants' records confidential (MCL 168.509q, via search snippet) — [MCL 168.509gg](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-168-509GG.pdf); [MCL 168.509q](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-168-509Q.pdf)
- **Kansas:** SOS may not release residence address of voters who requested concealment (AG Op. 2017-10, snippet) **[pre-2024]** — [KS AG Op. 2017-10](https://ebench-dev.greeninfo.org/state/kansas/opinions/250156301-ago-2017-10-july-11-2017)
- **Indiana:** AG Address Confidentiality Program participants' name, address, phone confidential (IC 3-11-4-6; 5-26.5); officer/official home-address shielding (IC 36-1-8.5) "would not entitle the person to have their address restricted or made confidential in the statewide voter registration system" — [Indiana 2026 Guidebook, pp. 94–95](https://www.in.gov/sos/elections/files/2026-Voter-Registration-Guidebook-Revised-Apr-2026.FINAL.pdf)
- **North Dakota:** ACP participants are listed "secured active" — exempt and "not available to any candidate, political party, or political committee for any purpose" (16.1-02-01, -15) — [NDCC ch. 16.1-02](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t16-1c02.pdf)
- **Iowa:** rules state nothing authorizes disclosure of data made confidential by the NVRA or other law (IAC 821—3.8) — [IAC 821 ch. 3](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/02-18-2026.821.3.pdf)

### Inferences
- Opt-out for ordinary voters (no court order) exists only in Minnesota (safety statement) and partially Nebraska (phone/email "private"). Elsewhere, protection requires program enrolment or a court order, and in Ohio/Wisconsin it effectively requires voting absentee.

### Gaps
- Iowa Safe at Home voter-list treatment; Illinois ACP/judicial privacy treatment in the SBE file; Kansas current concealment statute — not verified in this session.

## 7. Significant changes 2020–2026 (restrictions, litigation, mass challenges, doxxing) and responses to the 2025 DOJ demands for full voter rolls

### Takeaway
Regional changes cut both ways: South Dakota opened its file (free weekly posting, 2025), while Minnesota (2024 internet/resale ban), Michigan (2024 pre-registrant confidentiality), South Dakota (2026 judge/police shielding) and Illinois (no DOB since 2018) narrowed exposure. Public files fueled a 2024 Illinois doxxing-style episode and Michigan/Ohio mass-challenge projects. On the 2025 DOJ demands, Midwest states split: OH, IN, IA, NE, SD handed over full data (SD without signing DOJ's MOU); IL, MI, MN, WI gave only public versions, were sued, and won dismissals (MI affirmed by the Sixth Circuit); MO and KS gave public data only without being sued (as of the latest reports found).

### Inferences
- The DOJ episode shows the operative line in US practice is not "public vs. secret list" but "public fields vs. identifiers (DOB, DL, SSN4)": even the most open states (OH, SD) keep identifiers out of public files; states that gave identifiers to DOJ did so as government-to-government transfers, not public release.
- Openness creates downstream risks the state cannot recall (IL LGIS republication, MI Check My Vote), which is why several states pair access with internet-republication bans.

### Inferences
- For a Korea comparison: only 2 of 12 Midwest states (OH, SD) publish the full statewide roll free online; 6 sell it to anyone (WI, MI, KS, MO, IA, NE), 1 limits it to registered voters (MN), and 3 limit it to political actors (IL, IN full file, ND). Even the open-download states withhold government ID numbers, and SD withholds month/day of birth; protective carve-outs for DV victims, judges and police are near-universal.
- DOJ outcome mapping: openness of the public file did not predict the DOJ response — open-download OH and SD gave DOJ full identifiers, but so did restricted-access IN; open-sale MI and WI and restricted IL and MN refused and won in court; open-sale MO and KS gave only public data. The decisive factor appears to be each chief election official's legal judgement, not the state's public-access model (inference).
