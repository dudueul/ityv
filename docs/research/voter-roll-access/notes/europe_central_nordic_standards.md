# Public access to electoral registers in DE, AT, NL, BE, SE, FI, DK, NO, EE, plus the GDPR and international-standards framework

Research date: 12 November 2026. Statute texts were read from official or consolidated sources on that date unless a finding says otherwise. Findings marked **[snippet]** come from search-engine summaries of pages I could not open, so treat them as less certain. Findings marked **[possibly outdated]** come from documents older than the current law. Late in the session the shared web-search budget ran out, so some gaps (mostly Estonia, Finland and CJEU detail) could not be closed by further searching.

## 1. Germany (Wählerverzeichnis): who can inspect or copy, and under what limits?

### Takeaway
Germany is the strictest "own-entry" model among the countries with a pre-election inspection period. A voter may check only their own entry, on days 20 to 16 before the election. They may see other people's entries only if they credibly show facts that suggest the register is wrong. People with an Auskunftssperre (information block) are excluded even from that. Parties never receive the Wählerverzeichnis. They can only get age-group extracts from the population register (Melderegister), and voters can opt out of those. After the election, the participation marks may be disclosed only to authorities and courts, and the register is destroyed after six months.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis and own-entry right.** §17(1) BWahlG: municipalities keep a register for each polling district. "Jeder Wahlberechtigte hat das Recht, an den Werktagen vom 20. bis zum 16. Tag vor der Wahl während der allgemeinen Öffnungszeiten die Richtigkeit oder Vollständigkeit der zu seiner Person im Wählerverzeichnis eingetragenen Daten zu überprüfen." — [§17 BWahlG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwahlg/__17.html)
- **Others' entries only on a credible showing.** "Zur Überprüfung der Richtigkeit oder Vollständigkeit der Daten von anderen im Wählerverzeichnis eingetragenen Personen haben Wahlberechtigte … nur dann ein Recht auf Einsicht in das Wählerverzeichnis, wenn sie Tatsachen glaubhaft machen, aus denen sich eine Unrichtigkeit oder Unvollständigkeit des Wählerverzeichnisses ergeben kann." — [§17(1) BWahlG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwahlg/__17.html)
- **Opt-out (Auskunftssperre) overrides the third-party check.** "Das Recht zur Überprüfung gemäß Satz 3 besteht nicht hinsichtlich der Daten von Wahlberechtigten, für die im Melderegister ein Sperrvermerk gemäß § 51 Absatz 1 des Bundesmeldegesetzes eingetragen ist." — [§17(1) BWahlG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwahlg/__17.html)
- **When an Auskunftssperre applies.** It is entered where facts justify assuming that a register disclosure could endanger "Leben, Gesundheit, persönliche Freiheit oder ähnliche schutzwürdige Interessen", including protection from threats and stalking. Membership of a group exposed to hostility because of its professional or voluntary work counts in favour. The block lasts two years and can be renewed. — [§51 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__51.html)
- **How inspection works.** The register is available at least at the municipal office on days 20 to 16. A screen may be used, but "Das Datensichtgerät darf nur von einem Bediensteten der Gemeindebehörde bedient werden." — [§21(1) BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__21.html)
- **Copying is narrowly limited.** "Innerhalb der Einsichtsfrist ist das Anfertigen von Auszügen aus dem Wählerverzeichnis durch Wahlberechtigte zulässig, soweit dies im Zusammenhang mit der Prüfung des Wahlrechts einzelner bestimmter Personen steht. Die Auszüge dürfen nur für diesen Zweck verwendet und unbeteiligten Dritten nicht zugänglich gemacht werden." — [§21(3) BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__21.html)
- **Fields.** Family name, first names, date of birth and address ("nach Familiennamen und Vornamen, Geburtsdatum und Wohnung"), with "je eine Spalte für Vermerke über die Stimmabgabe und für Bemerkungen". — [§14 BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__14.html)
- **Participation is marked.** "Der Schriftführer vermerkt die Stimmabgabe im Wählerverzeichnis in der dafür bestimmten Spalte." Polling-board members may not announce a voter's personal details so that others in the room can hear them. — [§56(4) BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__56.html)
- **After the election, participation is not disclosed to the public.** Registers must be stored "gegen Einsichtnahme durch Unbefugte geschützt". "Auskünfte aus Wählerverzeichnissen … dürfen nur Behörden, Gerichten und sonstigen amtlichen Stellen des Wahlgebiets und nur dann erteilt werden, wenn sie für den Empfänger im Zusammenhang mit der Wahl erforderlich sind", which covers in particular suspected electoral offences, election-review proceedings and electoral statistics. — [§89 BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__89.html)
- **Destruction.** Registers are destroyed six months after the election unless an election-review case or a criminal investigation is pending. — [§90(2) BWO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwo_1985/__90.html)
- **Parties get population-register extracts, not the voter register.** In the six months before an election the registration office may give parties, voter groups and other nominating bodies data on "Gruppen von Wahlberechtigten … soweit für deren Zusammensetzung das Lebensalter bestimmend ist". Birth dates may not be passed on. Use is limited to "Werbung bei einer Wahl", and the data must be deleted within one month after the election. — [§50(1) BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__50.html)
- **Opt-out from party extracts.** "Die betroffene Person hat das Recht, der Übermittlung ihrer Daten nach den Absätzen 1 und 2 zu widersprechen". People must be told this when they register and by annual public notice. An Auskunftssperre also blocks these disclosures. — [§50(5)-(6) BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__50.html)
- **Penalties.** §49a BWahlG lists only two administrative offences: refusing an honorary electoral office, and publishing exit polls before polls close (fine up to €50,000). It does not cover misuse of register extracts. — [§49a BWahlG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bwahlg/__49a.html)

### Inferences
- The German design separates two things. Verifying the register is an individual right limited to one's own entry. Party campaigning is served from a different source (the Melderegister), with a statutory opt-out. The marked register is never a public record.
- The "Tatsachen glaubhaft machen" threshold is a middle path. Third parties can challenge a specific suspect entry, for example a non-resident registered at an address, without anyone being able to browse the register.

### Gaps
- The specific sanction for misusing BWO §21(3) extracts or BMG §50 data was not verified (likely BMG §54 fines and general data-protection law).
- Practice in the Länder (state and municipal election laws) was not examined.

## 2. Austria (Wählerevidenz / Wählerverzeichnis): who can inspect or copy, and under what limits?

### Takeaway
Austria is the most open of the Central European cases. **Anyone** may inspect the whole register, both the permanent Wählerevidenz and the election-specific Wählerverzeichnis, in person. Screen inspection shows lists only, and **search queries are prohibited**. Voters can check their own entry online with a qualified e-signature. Parties receive full printouts or machine-readable data before elections and, if represented in the Nationalrat, twice a year. The law expressly removes the GDPR rights to object and to restrict processing.

### Cited Findings
- **Public inspection of the Wählerverzeichnis.** From the 21st day after the cut-off date (Stichtag), the register is laid out "in einem allgemein zugänglichen Amtsraum" for ten days, or one week where house notices are posted. "Innerhalb des Einsichtszeitraums kann jedermann in das Wählerverzeichnis Einsicht nehmen." Anyone may also check online, after identifying with a qualified electronic signature, whether they are registered (via the central register ZeWaeR). Election observers may inspect registers and correction files even after the period ends. Version shown: Stand 12.11.2026, in force since 1.1.2024. — [§25 NRWO (JUSLINE)](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/nrwo/paragraf/25)
- **QR-code house notices.** In municipalities with more than 10,000 inhabitants, a notice is posted in every building with a QR code "über den jedermann unter Zuhilfenahme der Datenverarbeitung ZeWaeR im Internet eine Überprüfung gemäß § 25 Abs. 3 zweiter Satz vornehmen kann". — [§26 NRWO](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/nrwo/paragraf/26)
- **Copies to parties before elections.** "Ausfolgung von Ausdrucken des Wählerverzeichnisses an die Parteien": on application, printouts go to parties represented in the Nationalrat and to other parties intending to stand. Purposes are §1(2) Parteiengesetz 2012 and statistics. Parties pay the costs (applicants pay 50% on registering). Representatives of published state lists receive the data "in einheitlicher, verarbeitbarer Form", encrypted, between day 43 and day 34 before the election. Recipients must inform the people concerned "in geeigneter Weise". — [§27 NRWO](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/nrwo/paragraf/27)
- **Fields.** Family names, first names, academic titles, date of birth, home address for residents, and a sector-specific personal identifier (bPK, used internally). Austrians abroad: a connecting address and, where possible, an e-mail address. — [§1(3) WEviG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/wevig/gesamt)
- **Permanent register: inspection by anyone, but no searching.** "In die Wählerevidenz einer Gemeinde kann jedermann, der sich von der Vollständigkeit und Richtigkeit der Wählerevidenz überzeugen will, bei der jeweiligen Gemeinde Einsicht nehmen." Inspection covers the §1(3) data except the bPK. On screen it may happen "ausschließlich in Auflistungen" (listings only). "Suchanfragen im Rahmen der Einsichtnahme sind unzulässig." Anyone can check their own entry online via ZeWaeR with a qualified electronic signature. — [§5(1) WEviG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/wevig/gesamt)
- **Copies of the permanent register.** Parties represented in general representative bodies "können überdies aus der Wählerevidenz … Abschriften herstellen", for Parteiengesetz purposes and statistics, and must inform those concerned. Municipalities may hand over printouts or PDFs at cost, on equal terms for all parties. — [§5(2) WEviG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/wevig/gesamt)
- **Routine bulk data to parliamentary parties.** On 10 February and 10 August each year, the register data (except bPK) of all municipalities go free of charge, on application, to parties represented in the Nationalrat, in machine-readable form. — [§4(2) WEviG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/wevig/gesamt)
- **GDPR rights excluded.** "Hinsichtlich der Verarbeitung personenbezogener Daten nach diesem Bundesgesetz besteht kein Widerspruchsrecht gemäß Art. 21 der Verordnung (EU) 2016/679 … sowie kein Recht auf Einschränkung der Verarbeitung gemäß Art. 18". — [§23(5) NRWO](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/nrwo/paragraf/23)

### Inferences
- Austria shows how "anyone may inspect" can coexist with friction that blocks profiling. Inspection is in person and listing-only, search is banned, and the online check covers one's own entry only. Bulk access goes to parties under a purpose limit and a duty to inform voters.
- Because Austria excludes the Art. 21 objection right, there appears to be no general opt-out from party copies.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm how voters with a population-register block (Auskunftssperre under Meldegesetz §18) are handled in public inspection and party copies.
- I could not confirm whether participation marks (Abstimmungsverzeichnis / marked Wählerverzeichnis) are accessible after the election, or what penalty applies to misuse of party copies.
- A search snippet suggested that an earlier (c. 2010) version of §25 let anyone "Einsicht nehmen oder Vervielfältigungen herstellen". The current text quoted above mentions only inspection. I could not confirm when copying was dropped.

## 3. Netherlands (kiezersregistratie): who can inspect or copy, and under what limits?

### Takeaway
The Netherlands has **no public inspection** and **no party copies**. A person may only ask whether they themselves are registered. Voting uses a polling card (stempas) system checked against a register of *invalid* passes, so no named participation mark appears in the Kieswet.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis.** "Burgemeester en wethouders registreren de kiesgerechtigdheid van de ingezetenen van de gemeente." Voters abroad are registered in The Hague (D 2). — [Kieswet art. D 1-D 2](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)
- **Own-entry check only.** "Burgemeester en wethouders … delen een persoon op zijn verzoek onverwijld mee of hij als kiezer is geregistreerd. Indien hij niet als kiezer is geregistreerd, worden hem … de redenen daarvan meegedeeld." — [Kieswet art. D 5](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)
- **Correction runs through the GDPR.** Registrations may be changed "in aanvulling op artikel 16 van de Algemene verordening gegevensbescherming". Decisions on GDPR rectification requests follow AMvB deadlines, and appeal goes straight to the Raad van State. "Artikel 18 van de Algemene verordening gegevensbescherming is niet van toepassing op verwerking van persoonsgegevens op grond van artikel D 1." — [Kieswet art. D 6-D 10](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)
- **No register-inspection provision.** I searched the full Kieswet text for "inzage", "afschrift" and "kiezersregister". The "ter inzage" provisions concern candidate lists and count reports (processen-verbaal), which the burgemeester lays out "voor eenieder ter inzage". None concerns the voter registration. — [Kieswet (full text)](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)
- **Polling-station check is against invalid passes.** "Er is een register van ongeldige stempassen." The polling station has an extract of invalid stempassen on its table, and checks whether the pass number appears in it. — [Kieswet art. J 7a and following](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)

### Inferences
- Because the polling station takes in the stempas and checks it against an *invalid-pass* list instead of ticking off a named roll, the Dutch process does not appear to produce a named record of who voted. This is an inference from the Kieswet structure; I did not find an explicit statement.
- The Dutch model buys secrecy and privacy at the cost of third-party verifiability of the roll. Transparency is focused on count records, which are public.

### Gaps
- Whether parties can obtain voter data from the population register (BRP) under a separate rule was not checked. Nothing in the Kieswet provides for it.
- Sanctions for misuse were not checked.

## 4. Belgium (liste des électeurs / kiezerslijst): who can inspect or copy, and under what limits?

### Takeaway
Belgium combines **public verification by anyone** (anyone may check whether they or *any other person* is correctly listed, at the commune office) with **copies limited to parties that stand and to candidates**. Copies are for electoral use only, exclude the national register number, and carry criminal penalties of up to five years. Because voting is compulsory, a list of **non-voters** goes to the justice of the peace and the prosecutor. The data protection authority has recommended a voter opt-out "at source".

### Cited Findings
- **Version caveat [possibly outdated].** The text used is the official unofficial coordination "jusqu'au 1er juillet 2018" published by the Federal Public Service Interior. — [Code électoral / Kieswetboek (coordination 1.7.2018)](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Fields.** "le nom, les prénoms, la date de naissance, le sexe, la résidence principale et le numéro d'identification" (national register number). Continuous numbering by commune, alphabetical or by street. — [Art. 10 §2 Code électoral](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Anyone may verify anyone.** A public notice announces "que chacun peut, jusqu'au douzième jour précédant celui de l'élection, s'adresser au secrétariat de la commune durant les heures de service afin de vérifier si lui-même ou toute autre personne figure ou est correctement mentionné sur la liste." — [Art. 16](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Third-party challenges.** Any person entitled to vote in the electoral district may file a complaint against inscriptions, removals or omissions of names on the list, up to the 12th day before the election. — [Art. 18-19](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Copies to parties.** The commune "est tenue de délivrer des exemplaires ou copies" to persons acting for a political party who apply by registered letter and undertake in writing to present a list. Each party gets two copies free, "sur papier ou … sur support électronique standardisé", and further copies at cost. A party that does not present a list "ne peut plus faire usage de la liste des électeurs, fût-ce à des fins électorales". — [Art. 17 §1](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Copies to candidates and limits.** Candidates may obtain copies at cost (§2). "L'administration communale ne peut délivrer des exemplaires ou copies de la liste des électeurs à d'autres personnes … Les personnes qui ont reçu ces exemplaires ou copies ne peuvent à leur tour les communiquer à des tiers." Copies "ne peuvent être utilisés qu'à des fins électorales, y compris en dehors de la période se situant entre la date de délivrance de la liste et la date de l'élection" and "ne peuvent faire mention de leur numéro d'identification au Registre national". — [Art. 17 §2-§3](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Penalties.** "Est puni d'un emprisonnement de trois mois à cinq ans et d'une amende de mille à vingt mille euros ou d'une de ces peines seulement" anyone who unlawfully delivers copies, passes them to third parties, or uses list data "à des fins autres qu'électorales". — [Art. 197bis](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **Participation is recorded and used for enforcement, not published.** After the poll, the bureau draws up "le relevé des électeurs … qui n'ont pas pris part à l'élection", which goes to the juge de paix within three days (Art. 146). Within eight days of the result, the procureur du Roi lists non-voters whose excuses were not accepted, for the police court (Art. 209). Fines are €5-10, rising to €10-25 for repeat offences. Four unjustified abstentions in 15 years mean removal from the lists for ten years and a bar on public appointments (Art. 210). — [Art. 146, 207-210](https://elections.fgov.be/sites/default/files/documents/CodeElectoral_Kieswetboek_0.pdf)
- **DPA view on the legal basis and on opt-out.** Use of voter lists by candidates "n'est pas une obligation légale pour le candidat … au sens de l'article 6, paragraphe 1, point c) du RGPD". It rests on legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)), so the Art. 21(2) objection right applies to postal political prospecting. The Authority recommends "la possibilité pour les citoyens de solliciter, auprès de l'administration communale, le retrait de leur nom des copies de liste d'électeurs communiquées à des fins d'actions de propagande électorale", addressed to future legislation. It describes the statutory regime as giving citizens guarantees: clear rules on who may access, for what purposes, which data, and for how long. — [APD/GBA Recommandation d'initiative 01/2023 (11.12.2023, updated 26.4.2024)](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications/recommandation-d-initiative-01-2023-du-11-decembre-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Belgium shows that "public verifiability" (anyone can check any named person, in person) can be kept separate from "bulk copies" (parties and candidates only, purpose-bound, criminally sanctioned).
- A named participation record exists because voting is compulsory. It is routed only to the judiciary, which is consistent with the Venice Commission's "list of persons actually voting should not be published".

### Gaps
- Changes after July 2018 were not verified, including regional electoral codes and the end of compulsory voting in Flemish local elections. A search snippet said that before the law of 30 July 1991 anyone could request a copy. I could not open the underlying source.

## 5. Sweden (röstlängd): what is the effect of offentlighetsprincipen?

### Takeaway
Sweden is the outlier. Under the constitutional public-access principle, the röstlängd is an **allmän handling** (public document). That includes the **marks showing who voted**. Parliament's Constitution Committee has repeatedly refused to make it secret or to destroy or de-identify it after elections, arguing that public participation records guard against electoral fraud.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis.** "Vid val ska den centrala valmyndigheten för varje valdistrikt upprätta en förteckning över dem som är röstberättigade i valet (röstlängd)", based on the population register (folkbokföring) and the property register. Voters must request corrections in writing at least 12 days before election day. — [Vallag (2005:837) 5 kap. 1 §, 6 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/vallag-2005837_sfs-2005-837/)
- **Participation is marked, including how identity was checked.** Polling officials put the envelope in the urn "och markera detta i röstlängden. … I röstlängden ska det anges hur väljarens eller budets identitet har kontrollerats." At the preliminary count, "Antalet väljare som enligt röstlängden deltagit i valet ska räknas." — [Vallag 9 kap. 7 §, 11 kap. 2 §](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/vallag-2005837_sfs-2005-837/)
- **Who voted is deliberately public.** The government bill (prop. 2004/05:163, p. 99) stated "att grundläggande demokratiska principer bjuder att information om vilka väljare som har röstat i val inte hålls hemlig för allmänheten." — [KU betänkande 2009/10:KU26](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/GX01KU26)
- **The Committee's standing rationale (quoting KU 1964:10).** "Att röstlängd är offentlig handling är ej ett uttryck allenast för den allmänna offentlighetsgrundsatsen utan även för vallagstiftningens speciella hänsyn. … Avsikten med dessa bestämmelser är att skapa starka garantier för förrättade vals äkthet och mot obehöriga förfaranden." Also: "Det framstår därför som otänkbart … att avprickning av röstande sker i en handling som är undantagen offentligheten." — [KU 2009/10:KU26](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/GX01KU26)
- **The privacy argument was raised and rejected.** Motion K236 (2009) argued that "Att välja att delta i ett val eller inte kan för den enskilde vara en lika medveten handling som vilket parti eller vilken person denne väljer att rösta på" and asked for röstlängder to be destroyed or de-identified once the election is final. An earlier 1990/91 motion warned that organisations can "studera längden och göra anteckningar", making it possible "att i vissa lägen spåra ändrade sympatier" with "risken för påtryckningar inför nästa val". The Committee rejected the motion; a minority (s, v) entered a reservation. — [KU 2009/10:KU26](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/GX01KU26) **[2010 document; the position is long-standing, but check whether it has changed since]**
- **Protected-identity voters are handled separately [snippet].** County administrative boards' rules say data on people with protected personal data are never given to anyone but the person concerned. — [Länsstyrelsen rules for protection administrators (Valmyndigheten)](https://valcentralen.val.se/download/18.2e80123618be0306a425f7a/1638954724072/bestammelser-for-skyddsadministratorer-pa-lansstyrelsen-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Sweden, offentlighetsprincipen plus an explicit "election-integrity" rationale makes the participation record a public document. This directly conflicts with the Venice Commission guideline I.4.c ("The list of persons actually voting should not be published"), though Sweden makes the list *available on request* rather than actively publishing it.
- For Korea, Sweden is the main European example to cite *for* participation transparency, and its parliamentary record also documents the coercion and profiling counter-arguments.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact fields of the röstlängd (for example whether personnummer and address are shown in the version released to requesters) or how protected-identity voters appear in it. A Valmyndigheten memo [snippet] says the advance-voting register (väljarförteckning) may record name, personnummer, serial number and röstlängd number.
- Any OSL (Public Access to Information and Secrecy Act) secrecy provision that might apply to released röstlängder, current Valmyndigheten practice on releasing them, retention periods, and any changes after 2010 were not verified.

## 6. Finland (äänioikeusrekisteri): who can inspect it, and is participation disclosed?

### Takeaway
The Digital and Population Data Services Agency (DVV) compiles a central electronic voting register for each election. It is open to public inspection from day 41, and advance voting is marked in it. Secondary sources say the register is public except personal identity codes and some exceptions, that voting marks become public only after polling or the election proceedings end, and that entries under a protection order (turvakielto) are never public. **I could not read the primary text of the Election Act (Vaalilaki 714/1998)**, so the publicity rules below are less certain.

### Cited Findings
- **Compilation.** DVV compiles the register 46 days before election day, using population-system data as at day 51 (Vaalilaki §18). — [Sodankylä election info](https://kehitys.sodankyla.fi/vaalit/yleista-tietoa-vaaleista/) **[snippet]**; [vaalit.fi (English)](https://vaalit.fi/en/right-to-vote-and-compilation-of-the-voting-register4) **[snippet]**
- **Public inspection.** The register is publicly available from day 41 before election day. — [Ministry of Justice, "Suomen vaalijärjestelmä" (2020)](https://julkaisut.valtioneuvosto.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/161999/OM_2020_1.pdf) **[snippet]**. A municipal privacy notice (updated 24.6.2025) confirms: "Rekisteri on julkisesti nähtävillä ja sitä koskevan oikaisuvaatimuksen voi tehdä Digi- ja väestötietovirastolle. Äänioikeusrekisteri tulee lainvoimaiseksi 12. päivänä ennen vaalipäivää." — [Järvenpää privacy notice](https://www.jarvenpaa.fi/kaupunki-ja-paatoksenteko/asiointi/tietosuoja-jarvenpaan-kaupungilla/jarvenpaan-kaupunki-rekisterinpitajana/tietosuojaselosteet-palveluittain/vaalien-toimittamiseen-liittyva-aanestajien-vaalitoimitsijoiden-vaalilautakuntien-ja-vaalitoimikunnan-henkilotietojen-kasittely)
- **Correction claims.** Corrections go to DVV at least 16 days before election day, for example where a person believes they were wrongly left out or their data are wrong. — [vaalit.fi](https://vaalit.fi/aanioikeus) **[snippet]**
- **Fields processed and participation marking.** The fields processed for voters are name, personal identity code, sex, voting district, any protection-order marker, citizenship if not Finnish, the basis of voting right, and "tieto äänioikeuden käyttämisestä" (whether the right to vote was used). "Äänioikeusrekisteri on käytössä ennakkoäänestyspaikoissa ja jokaisen ennakkoon äänestävän äänestämisestä tehdään siihen merkintä." Election-day lists are printed from it, or the register is used directly. "Vaalien jälkeen äänioikeusrekisterin tiedot hävitetään". — [Järvenpää privacy notice (24.6.2025)](https://www.jarvenpaa.fi/kaupunki-ja-paatoksenteko/asiointi/tietosuoja-jarvenpaan-kaupungilla/jarvenpaan-kaupunki-rekisterinpitajana/tietosuojaselosteet-palveluittain/vaalien-toimittamiseen-liittyva-aanestajien-vaalitoimitsijoiden-vaalilautakuntien-ja-vaalitoimikunnan-henkilotietojen-kasittely)
- **Publicity rules [snippet].** DVV states that the register is public except personal identity codes and the cases in Vaalilaki §23(4) and §29(2). Municipal election guides say voting-mark information is not public until voting has ended or the election proceedings are complete. Protection-order entries remain non-public even after the election. — [DVV register privacy page](https://dvv.fi/aanioikeusrekisterin-tietosuoja) **[snippet; page returned 403]**
- **Destruction.** DVV destroys register data once the result is final, with archive printouts as ordered by the Ministry of Justice (§§18, 29). — [DVV](https://dvv.fi/aanioikeusrekisterin-tietosuoja) **[snippet]**
- **Statistical use.** Statistics Finland receives register data (identity code, name, language, citizenship, country of residence, voting district) for election statistics and keeps the statistical data permanently. — [Statistics Finland privacy notice](https://stat.fi/fi/tietoa-meista/tietosuojaselosteet/aanioikeusrekisteri) **[possibly outdated: it names Oikeusrekisterikeskus as the source]**

### Inferences
- If the snippets are accurate, Finland is a second Nordic case, after Sweden, where whether someone voted can become public after the election. Turvakielto acts as the coercion and safety valve.

### Gaps
- The verbatim text of Vaalilaki §§18-29, in particular §23(4) and §29(2) and the exact publicity of voting marks, could not be retrieved (Finlex is JavaScript-rendered and DVV returned 403).
- Whether parties receive copies, and the penalties, are not found.

## 7. Denmark (valgliste): who can inspect or copy, and under what limits?

### Takeaway
Denmark has **no public inspection and no party copies** in the Folketing election act. Voters verify through the polling card (valgkort) mailed to them and can object to errors. The marked list is destroyed after the complaint period but may be kept for research. Freedom-of-information requests are assessed restrictively because of "den særlige fortrolighed, hvormed valghandlinger er omgivet".

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis and fields.** "Ingen kan udøve valgret uden at være optaget på valglisten" (§3). The municipal council draws up a list for each polling area once an election is called (§18), based on CPR (§19(1)). "Valglisterne skal indeholde vælgernes navn, fødselsdato og bopæl samt løbe-nr. og en rubrik til afmærkning." Electronic lists are permitted. — [Folketingsvalgloven §§3, 18-19 (consolidated text)](https://danskelove.dk/folketingsvalgloven); current consolidation LBK nr 1137 af 22/09/2025 per [lovguiden](https://www.lovguiden.dk/loven/folketingsvalgloven)
- **Voter verification is via the valgkort, not inspection.** The polling card shows name, address, list number, polling place and time (§20). The minister announces that objections about non-receipt or wrong content are made to the municipality (§21). Corrections are possible up to and including election day (§22). — [Folketingsvalgloven §§20-22](https://danskelove.dk/folketingsvalgloven)
- **Participation is marked.** The list keeper marks the voter's name before handing over a ballot (§47). Accepted postal votes are marked on the list (§66). — [Folketingsvalgloven §§47, 66](https://danskelove.dk/folketingsvalgloven)
- **Destruction and research use.** "Valglisterne, valgkortene, stemmesedlerne samt brevstemmematerialet skal tilintetgøres, når klagefristen er udløbet … og eventuelle klager over valget er endeligt afgjort". The municipality may decide they are used "til forskning og statistik" under the GDPR and the Data Protection Act, and they are destroyed when no longer needed. — [Folketingsvalgloven §104(3)-(4)](https://danskelove.dk/folketingsvalgloven)
- **Restrictive FOI practice [snippet].** The ministry guidance says requests for access to voter lists under the Public Administration Act "skal bedømmes i lyset af dette samt i lyset af materialets særlige karakter og funktion samt den særlige fortrolighed, hvormed valghandlinger er omgivet." — [Vejledning om afholdelse af folketingsvalg, "8 dage efter valgdagen" (lovguiden)](https://www.lovguiden.dk/loven/vejledning-om-afholdelse-af-folketingsvalg/8-dage-efter-valgdagen)
- **Comparative characterisation [snippet].** ACE cites Denmark as a country where the electoral register is not published or accessible to the public or political parties. — [ACE Encyclopaedia](https://aceproject.org/ace-en/topics/ei/eif/eif06/eif06c) **[snippet; page returned 500]**

### Inferences
- My search of the consolidated act for public-inspection or copy-to-party provisions found none, consistent with the ACE description. Denmark relies on universal CPR-based automatic registration plus personal valgkort as its accuracy mechanism.
- §104(4) shows a privacy-compatible way to reuse participation data: research and statistics under the GDPR, not publication.

### Gaps
- Local-election law (kommunal- og regionalvalgloven) and any party access to CPR extracts were not checked. Penalties are not found.

## 8. Norway (valgmanntall): what is the effect of offentlighet?

### Takeaway
Norway has a strong public-access tradition, but its approach differs from Sweden's. The **unmarked** manntall must be laid out for public inspection until election day (name, address, birth date only; not downloadable; not on internet-connected PCs). **Parties and lists get a free copy** for election-related political use with a two-year deletion duty, and voters with blocked addresses are left out of it. The **marked** manntall is closed: by law access is limited to officials and researchers.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis.** "Departementet skal opprette og oppdatere manntall." (§2-3(1)). "Valgstyret skal legge manntallet ut til offentlig ettersyn. Manntallet skal ligge ute til og med valgdagen." The board must announce when and where. — [Valgloven 2023 (LOV-2023-06-16-62) §2-3, §2-7](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2023-06-16-62/KAPITTEL_2)
- **Third-party correction.** Anyone who believes that they *or someone else* is wrongly included in or excluded from the manntall "kan kreve at feilen skal rettes", by a written, reasoned claim to the municipal election board. Affected persons are notified of changes. — [Valgloven §§2-8, 2-9](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2023-06-16-62/KAPITTEL_2)
- **Marked manntall is closed.** "Tilgang til manntallet kan bare gis til a. offentlig ansatte som har behov for slik tilgang for å kunne utføre sine arbeidsoppgaver, b. forskere i vitenskapelig øyemed etter samtykke fra folkeregistermyndigheten, c. andre når det følger av loven her." There is also a confidentiality duty (§21-3) for anyone who learns how a voter voted. — [Valgloven §21-2, §21-3](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2023-06-16-62/KAPITTEL_2)
- **Continuity with old law.** The Storting committee said chapter 2 "viderefører gjeldende lov", except that the ministry takes over a central digital manntall. — [Innst. 431 L (2022-2023)](https://stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Publikasjoner/Innstillinger/Stortinget/2022-2023/inns-202223-431l/?m=3) **[snippet]**. The old law's §2-6 had the same public-inspection rule. — [Folldal 2023 case file](https://www.folldal.kommune.no/_f/p1/idc2227e0-ebfa-40ac-88b1-2c98e0f245cc/utlegging-av-manntall-ved-kommunestyre-og-fylkestingsvalg-2023.pdf) **[snippet]**
- **Fields; public version restricted; no download.** The full manntall contains name, address, national ID number (fødselsnummer) and voting district. The public-inspection version contains only names, addresses and dates of birth. If shown electronically, it must be done so that the manntall cannot be altered or downloaded ("laste det ned"). — [Valgforskriften 2024 (FOR-2024-11-05-2662) §§1-1, 1-5](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2024-11-05-2662/KAPITTEL_1)
- **Blocked addresses.** Persons with a blocked address ("sperret adresse") are not entered in the ordinary manntall. A separate paper list (name and national ID number) goes to the municipality, accessible only to staff who need it to check votes. — [Valgforskriften §§1-1(2), 1-8](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2024-11-05-2662/KAPITTEL_1)
- **Copies to parties.** "Partier og grupper som stiller liste ved valget, har rett til ett gratis eksemplar av manntallet … for hver kommune i valgdistriktet." More copies or group extracts are available at cost. They may contain only names, addresses and birth dates or age. Recipients become data controllers. "Det utleverte materiellet kan bare brukes til politiske formål i forbindelse med valg", it may not be linked with other data, and it must be deleted at the latest two years after handover. — [Valgforskriften §§1-6, 1-7](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2024-11-05-2662/KAPITTEL_1)
- **Practice guidance.** Inspection starts as soon as possible after the 30 June cut-off. "Ved bruk av pc skal den ikke være tilkoblet internett." Copies come out of the EVA system as an .xlsx file. Extracts "skal ikke benyttes til kommersielle formål … skal ikke selges eller lånes ut. Det er ikke adgang til å koble manntallet til andre offentlige registre." Voters are ticked off in the electronic manntall. "Avkryssede manntallslister bør oppbevares i en valgperiode før det kasseres på en betryggende måte." — [Valgdirektoratet, "Mer om manntall"](https://valgmedarbeiderportalen.valg.no/artikler/mer-om-manntall/)
- **Change from the old regulation [possibly outdated].** The 2015 commentary said copies were paper only and that "politiske formål" was *not* limited to elections. The 2024 text adds "i forbindelse med valg". — [Valgforskriften with commentary (2015)](https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/65ef16de8087456e9c10530b68b2fcb6/valgforskriften_m_merknader_30062015.pdf) **[snippet]**

### Inferences
- In Norway, offentlighet applies to the *eligibility* roll (who may vote), not to the *participation* record (who did vote). The 2023 Act's §21-2 overrides general FOI access to the marked manntall. This is the key contrast with Sweden.
- Norway's technical controls (no download, offline PC, field minimisation, protected-address exclusion, no linking, two-year deletion) are a ready-made template for "transparent but not harvestable".

### Gaps
- Penalties for misusing party copies were not found. The Act's penalty provision §21-9 covers only results and forecast publication on election day.

## 9. Estonia (valijate nimekiri): how do voter lists work with internet voting, and how does a voter check their own entry?

### Takeaway
Estonia's list is drawn automatically from the population register, and since 2021 it is a single **electronic list** checked at any polling place. A voter may check only **their own data**, mainly through eesti.ee login with eID. The State Electoral Office feeds list changes into the i-voting system daily. Repeat votes are reconciled centrally, with paper votes taking primacy. I found **no** provision for public inspection or party copies, but I could not read the primary statute text.

### Cited Findings
- **Compilation.** "The list of voters is compiled on the basis of the data in the population register." A voter needs a residence address in that register (at least at municipality level). Voters who do not report a move "will remain in the list of voters with the address data of their former residence." — [State Electoral Office, "List of Voters"](https://www.valimised.ee/en/estonian-elections-nutshell/list-voters)
- **Electronic list since 2021.** Voters are no longer tied to a specific district. "A voter's right to vote will be checked against the electronic list of voters" at polling places, and ballots are issued against a signature. — [valimised.ee](https://www.valimised.ee/en/estonian-elections-nutshell/list-voters)
- **Own-entry check.** The current consolidated Riigikogu Election Act provides that a voter may check the correctness of data entered in the list of voters concerning themselves. — [Riigikogu Election Act (Riigi Teataja, current version link)](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/en/akt/514112013015?leiaKehtiv=) **[snippet; page did not render]**. The State Electoral Office's privacy notice says any voter has the right to consult their personal data on the list. — [SEO data protection terms](https://www.valimised.ee/en/electoral-organizers/state-electoral-office/state-electoral-office-data-protection-terms-and) **[snippet]**
- **How to check in practice.** Population-register data are accessible "through the information portal eesti.ee" with an ID card or bank login. Errors are reported to the register administrator. — [Ministry of the Interior, Population Register](https://www.siseministeerium.ee/en/activities/population-procedures/population-register) **[snippet]**. Each voter receives a voter card with the election date and place. — [valimised.ee](https://www.valimised.ee/en/estonian-elections-nutshell/list-voters) **[snippet]**
- **i-voting integration.** "The State Electoral Office organises the entry of the amendments to the list of voters in the electronic voting system at least once a day." Voters can verify that the i-voting application transferred their vote as intended, until 20:15 after advance voting ends. — [Riigikogu Election Act](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/en/akt/514112013015?leiaKehtiv=) **[snippet]**
- **Paper primacy.** In the 2007 system, a voter could change their e-vote by re-voting electronically, and "a vote cast by paper is final and annuls all internet votes cast by the voter." — [OSCE/ODIHR EAM report, Estonia 2007](https://odihr.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/1/1/25925.pdf) **[possibly outdated]**. A recent ERR explainer says the list of in-person paper voters is compared with the list of online voters and repeat votes are annulled. — [ERR News](https://news.err.ee/1608906230/online-voting-how-estonia-counts-and-secures-its-electronic-votes) **[snippet]**
- **No participation history in the population register [snippet].** "The population register also does not register whether a voter has previously voted at elections or not." — [Ministry of the Interior](https://www.siseministeerium.ee/en/activities/population-procedures/population-register)

### Inferences
- With i-voting, the "who has voted" list is a live operational dataset: it is needed to stop double voting across channels and to cancel superseded e-votes. Estonia appears to keep it inside the electoral administration. I found no public or party access.
- For Korea, Estonia shows that a central electronic roll with own-entry checks by eID can replace public inspection as the main accuracy mechanism, at the cost of third-party verifiability.

### Gaps
- The verbatim statutory text (section numbers on the voter list, data fields, complaints, storage and destruction) could not be retrieved, because Riigi Teataja blocked automated access.
- I did not confirm whether parties or observers can access voter lists, or the current rules on cancelling i-votes by paper vote (advance days versus election day).

## 10. What does the GDPR and EU framework require for processing and disclosing voter registers?

### Takeaway
Electoral authorities process registers under **Art. 6(1)(c)/(e)** as controllers. Disclosure to parties is lawful only where **Member State law specifically authorises it**, for electoral advertising, and only the data needed (such as name and address). **Art. 9** makes "political opinions" special-category data. The Venice Commission says abstention "may indicate a political choice", and the CJEU reads "revealing" broadly, so participation marks carry a real risk of being treated as Art. 9 data. **Art. 86** lets national law reconcile public access to official documents with data protection, which is the hook for Sweden-type regimes. CJEU case law on public registers (WM/Sovim, 2022) treats access by the *general public* as a serious interference that needs strict-necessity justification.

### Cited Findings
- **Recital 56 (verbatim).** "Where in the course of electoral activities, the operation of the democratic system in a Member State requires that political parties compile personal data on people's political opinions, the processing of such data may be permitted for reasons of public interest, provided that appropriate safeguards are established." — [GDPR Recital 56](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-56/); official text [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- **Art. 9(1) (verbatim).** "Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, … shall be prohibited." Relevant exceptions: (d) not-for-profit political bodies, "on condition that the processing relates solely to the members or to former members … and that the personal data are not disclosed outside that body without the consent of the data subjects"; (e) "personal data which are manifestly made public by the data subject"; (g) "necessary for reasons of substantial public interest, on the basis of Union or Member State law which shall be proportionate to the aim pursued, respect the essence of the right to data protection and provide for suitable and specific measures". — [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- **Art. 86 (verbatim).** "Personal data in official documents held by a public authority … may be disclosed by the authority or body in accordance with Union or Member State law … in order to reconcile public access to official documents with the right to the protection of personal data pursuant to this Regulation." — [GDPR Art. 86](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-86-gdpr/)
- **Commission guidance COM(2018) 638 (12.9.2018): controllers and lawful basis.** "national electoral authorities are controllers for the electoral registers." "Public authorities involved in the electoral context process personal data in order to comply with a legal obligation or for the exercise of a public task. … Political parties and foundations can also process data on the grounds of public interest if so provided by national law." — [Commission guidance on the application of Union data protection law in the electoral context](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXVI/EU/34428/imfname_10838040.pdf)
- **Commission guidance: disclosure to parties (verbatim).** "Public authorities may disclose certain information on individuals included in electoral lists or in registers of residents to political parties only when specifically authorised by Member State law and only for the purpose of advertising in the electoral context and as far as necessary for that purpose, such as name and address." — [COM(2018) 638](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXVI/EU/34428/imfname_10838040.pdf)
- **Commission guidance: Art. 9 and the party-member exception.** Processing of "sensitive data, including inferred 'sensitive data', is generally prohibited". Parties may rely on 9(2)(g) with a legal basis and safeguards. The 9(2)(d) members exception "cannot be used by a political party to process data of prospective members or voters". Parties using data "from electoral registers, data brokers … typically need to inform and explain to the individuals concerned how they combine and use this data". Its checklist for electoral authorities: "Legal basis for processing: legal obligation or task of public interest based on law" and "Conduct a data protection impact assessment if impact not already assessed in the law". — [COM(2018) 638](https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XXVI/EU/34428/imfname_10838040.pdf)
- **EDPB Statement 2/2019 (adopted 13 March 2019).** "Personal data revealing political opinions is a special category of data under the GDPR. As a general principle, the processing of such data is prohibited and is subject to a number of narrowly-interpreted conditions". "Personal data which have been made public, or otherwise been shared by individual voters, even if they are not data revealing political opinions, are still subject to, and protected, by EU data protection law." The "manifestly made public" derogation "should be interpreted narrowly, as it cannot be used to legitimate inferred data." — [EDPB Statement 2/2019](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb-2019-03-13-statement-on-elections_en.pdf)
- **Party use rests on legitimate interest plus objection (Belgian DPA).** Candidates' use of voter lists is not an Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation for the candidate. It rests on Art. 6(1)(f), which triggers the Art. 21(2) right to object to political prospecting. — [APD/GBA Recommendation 01/2023](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications/recommandation-d-initiative-01-2023-du-11-decembre-2023.pdf)
- **National derogations seen in this research.** Austria excludes Art. 21 and Art. 18 for electoral processing ([§23(5) NRWO](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/nrwo/paragraf/23)). The Netherlands excludes Art. 18 for voter registration ([Kieswet D 10](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004627/)). Denmark allows research and statistical reuse of marked lists under the GDPR ([§104(4) Folketingsvalgloven](https://danskelove.dk/folketingsvalgloven)).
- **Is participation a "political opinion"? (standards view).** "since abstention may indicate a political choice, lists of persons voting should not be published." — [Venice Commission Code, Explanatory Report §54](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD(2002)023rev2-cor-e). The Swedish motion made the same argument; see Q5.
- **CJEU C-184/20 OT v Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija (Grand Chamber, 1 August 2022).** Commentators report that the Court held that publishing personal data that can *indirectly* disclose sensitive characteristics (there, sexual orientation inferred from a spouse's name) falls within Art. 9 special categories. This widens "revealing" to inference. The case concerned online publication of public officials' declarations of interests. — [Covington Inside Privacy commentary](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/); [CURIA press release (FR)](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-08/cp220133fr.pdf) **[secondary; judgment not opened]**
- **CJEU C-37/20 and C-601/20 WM and Sovim (Grand Chamber, 22 November 2022).** The rule making beneficial-ownership information "accessible in all cases to any member of the general public is invalid". "the general public's access … constitutes a serious interference with the fundamental rights to respect for private life and to the protection of personal data", since it "enables a potentially unlimited number of persons" to see the data, and "once those data have been made available to the general public, they can not only be freely consulted, but also retained and disseminated." The interference was "neither limited to what is strictly necessary nor proportionate", compared with the former "legitimate interest" access regime. — [CURIA Press Release No 188/22](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2022-11/cp220188en.pdf)
- **Newer EU law in the background.** The Regulation on transparency and targeting of political advertising was adopted in February/March 2024, with most provisions applying 18 months later. The Belgian DPA points to it as relevant to online profiling of voters. — [APD/GBA Recommendation 01/2023, footnote 8](https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/publications/recommandation-d-initiative-01-2023-du-11-decembre-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Eligibility roll versus participation record.** A roll of eligible voters (name, address, birth date) is ordinary personal data. Disclosing it is lawful under Art. 6(1)(c)/(e) with a specific statutory basis, purpose limitation and minimisation, which is the Commission's "name and address" benchmark. A participation record (who voted, or who abstained) risks Art. 9 classification after C-184/20, because inference is enough and abstention "may indicate a political choice". That would require Art. 9(2)(g) (substantial public interest, proportionate law, specific safeguards) or an Art. 86 public-access law, with a strict proportionality test.
- **Publication to the general public.** WM/Sovim (2022) suggests that "open to anyone, retainable, redistributable" access, such as an online downloadable roll, is a far more serious interference than access for defined stakeholders or people with a legitimate interest. The authority must show that the less intrusive regime was insufficient. This is persuasive authority for Korea, not binding, but it maps directly onto the publish-online versus inspect-in-person choice.
- **Party copies.** Under EU law these need specific Member State authorisation, purpose limits (election campaigning), minimised fields, and in practice deletion deadlines and penalties. The Belgian DPA reads them as triggering an Art. 21(2) opt-out for direct mail.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the text of C-439/19 *Latvijas Republikas Saeima* (22 June 2021, public disclosure of penalty-point data and Art. 86). Its press release URL could not be located before the search budget ran out. C-252/21 *Meta v Bundeskartellamt* (2023), on Art. 9 "manifestly made public", was also not verified.
- I found no CJEU judgment directly on electoral registers or turnout lists. No EDPB guideline specifically on electoral rolls was found.
- Council of Europe Convention 108+ was not reviewed.

## 11. What do international standards say about voter-register transparency?

### Takeaway
The Venice Commission's Code requires that **registers "must be published"** and be correctable through an administrative or judicial procedure, and that **"the list of persons actually voting should not be published"**. ODIHR treats a public scrutiny period and equal party access as good practice, subject to data protection. Only the data needed to identify eligibility should be public, and uses and sanctions must be defined by law. ODIHR flags publication of signed or marked lists as a contested issue that must be weighed against ballot secrecy.

### Cited Findings
- **Venice Commission Code of Good Practice in Electoral Matters (CDL-AD(2002)023rev2-cor), Guideline I.1.2 (verbatim).** "Fulfilment of the following criteria is essential if electoral registers are to be reliable: i. electoral registers must be permanent; ii. there must be regular up-dates, at least once a year. Where voters are not registered automatically, registration must be possible over a relatively long period; iii. electoral registers must be published; iv. there should be an administrative procedure - subject to judicial control - or a judicial procedure, allowing for the registration of a voter who was not registered; the registration should not take place at the polling station on election day; v. a similar procedure should allow voters to have incorrect inscriptions amended; vi. a supplementary register may be a means of giving the vote to persons who have moved or reached statutory voting age since final publication of the register." — [Venice Commission Code](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD(2002)023rev2-cor-e)
- **Explanatory Report §7 (verbatim extracts).** "The proper maintenance of electoral registers is vital in guaranteeing universal suffrage. … few citizens bother to check the electoral registers when they are presented for inspection". "iii. The electoral registers must be published. The final update should be sent to a higher authority under the supervision of the impartial body responsible for the application of the electoral law." "v. … A procedure of the kind mentioned in the previous paragraph should make it possible for electors to have erroneous entries corrected. The capacity for requesting such corrections may be restricted to electors registered in the same constituency or at the same polling station." — [Venice Commission Code, Explanatory Report](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD(2002)023rev2-cor-e)
- **Secret suffrage, Guideline I.4 (verbatim).** "a. For the voter, secrecy of voting is not only a right but also a duty … b. Voting must be individual. Family voting and any other form of control by one voter over the vote of another must be prohibited. c. The list of persons actually voting should not be published. d. The violation of secret suffrage should be sanctioned." Explanatory Report §54: "Moreover, since abstention may indicate a political choice, lists of persons voting should not be published." — [Venice Commission Code](https://www.venice.coe.int/webforms/documents/default.aspx?pdffile=CDL-AD(2002)023rev2-cor-e)

### Inferences
- In Venice Commission terms, "published" means the eligibility register is made available for checking and challenge. All nine countries satisfy this in some form: public display (AT, BE, NO, FI, SE), own-entry checks (DE, NL, DK, EE), or both. The standard does not require online bulk publication, and ODIHR's "downloadable … with a search function" suggestion is explicitly qualified by data-protection compliance.
- The standards split cleanly. Register *publication* is encouraged. Publication of *participation* is discouraged (Venice I.4.c), with ODIHR acknowledging that some states do it and that the main counterweight is effective remedies for turnout-manipulation complaints.

- No quantitative comparative dataset on voter-list publicity worldwide (ACE Comparative Data or IDEA) could be retrieved. ACE pages returned errors and the search budget was exhausted.
- Later Venice Commission or ODIHR documents specifically on electronic registers (for example the 2020s ICT handbook) were not reviewed in detail.

## 12. Comparison table and implications for whether Korea should publish its voter roll

### Takeaway
The nine countries fall into four models. **(A) own-entry only:** NL, DK, EE. **(B) own-entry plus a third-party check on a credible showing:** DE. **(C) public in-person inspection by anyone, with party copies:** AT, BE, NO, and FI (inspection). **(D) full public document including participation:** SE, and possibly FI after the election. None publishes a downloadable, searchable national roll online to the general public. Online access, where it exists, covers one's **own** entry only (AT via qualified e-signature, EE via eID).

### Inferences (for judging Korea's options)
- **No peer publishes the roll online to the public.** Even the most open systems (AT, BE, NO) restrict *mode* (in person, listing-only, no search or download, offline PC) and *fields* (name, address, birth date). Online access is limited to one's own entry. A Korean proposal to post the full roll online would go beyond every European comparator, and under WM/Sovim-style reasoning would need a strong showing that in-person or own-entry access is inadequate.
- **Separate the eligibility roll from the participation record.** Seven of nine keep participation non-public. DE, AT (apparently), NO and DK give access to authorities or researchers only. BE routes non-voters to the courts. NL appears not to create a named record. Only SE, and possibly FI after the election, make it public, on an explicit anti-fraud rationale. Venice Commission I.4.c and §54 counsel against publishing participation. ODIHR says that where it is not published, effective complaint remedies for turnout-manipulation suspicions must exist.
- **Calibrated third-party verification instead of mass publication.** Workable intermediate designs: Germany's "credible facts" gate; Belgium's and Norway's rule that anyone may check or challenge a *named* person; Austria's "inspect any list but no search queries". Each lets citizens and parties detect phantom or ineligible entries without enabling profiling.
- **Party copies with guardrails.** Where parties get copies (AT, BE, NO), the common guardrails are: candidacy as a condition (BE, NO); purpose limitation to election campaigning (all three); field minimisation, with the national ID number excluded (BE, NO); no onward transfer or linking (BE, NO); deletion deadlines (NO 2 years, DE 1 month); duty to inform voters (AT); and criminal penalties (BE up to 5 years). The EU Commission benchmark is "only when specifically authorised by Member State law … as far as necessary … such as name and address".
- **Coercion resistance needs safety valves.** Every open system keeps people at risk out of public view: DE Auskunftssperre, NO blocked-address exclusion, FI turvakielto, SE protected identities, plus New Zealand's "unpublished roll" per ACE. Any Korean publication regime would need an equivalent, ideally automatic for people with existing address protection.
- **GDPR-style legal architecture to borrow.** A specific statute naming who gets what, for which purpose and for how long (Art. 6(1)(c)/(e) plus 6(3)). For any participation data, a substantial-public-interest justification with specific safeguards (Art. 9(2)(g)) or an Art. 86-type reconciliation with public-records law. A data protection impact assessment by the electoral authority as controller (Commission checklist).
